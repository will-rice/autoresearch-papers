# TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments

Yicheng Xu    Zhikai Xu    Ziyuan Chen    Yuhang Dong    Tao Zhang   
Zhucun Xue    Yong Liu    Jiangning Zhang Affiliation: \[ Affiliation:
\[ Email: <186368@zju.edu.cn>

###### Abstract

Robots must act under physical and social constraints while adapting to
changes in their environment and human needs. Existing embodied-agent
systems often focus on specified goals or abstract execution into
symbolic actions, limiting evaluation in an evolving world. We introduce
TARSphere, a robot-oriented full-stack framework for physically and
socially grounded long-horizon embodied agents. TARSphere-Infra runs
robot policies in a physics simulator with embodied NPCs and constrained
dialogue. An event-driven finite-state machine (FSM) uses completed
actions and delivered information to update task states and evaluate
outcomes. TARSphere-Harness, its embodied brain harness, lets a frozen
multimodal large language model (MLLM) act through multi-level
executable skills, using observations, hierarchical memory, and
execution feedback to revise its decisions. On this foundation,
agent-in-the-loop curation aligns task semantics, causal dependencies,
and scene geometry to construct TARSphere-Bench: 360 episodes across 30
indoor scenes. Five paired conditions assess perceptual robustness,
disruption adaptation, task update, human coordination, and intent
understanding for navigate-and-report and fetch-and-deliver tasks. Our
evaluation of 8 MLLMs shows that general-purpose models can outperform
embodied models, while task updates and human interaction remain
difficult. Frequent collisions and falls further motivate evaluating
execution safety alongside task success, even with a shared action
interface and decision budget.

## 1 Introduction

Robots operating in everyday environments must account for physical
constraints \[[11](#bib.bib24)\], human safety, appropriate interaction,
and social norms \[[19](#bib.bib13), [23](#bib.bib1)\]. As the world
evolves, sensory conditions, physical access, and people’s actions and
needs can change during execution \[[9](#bib.bib8), [23](#bib.bib1),
[8](#bib.bib15)\]. Long-horizon tasks therefore require tracking world
states, interpreting human intentions, and adjusting plans using
execution feedback \[[34](#bib.bib10), [4](#bib.bib2), [9](#bib.bib8)\]
([Figure 1](#S0.F1 "In TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")).

Vision-language-action (VLA) models \[[3](#bib.bib5), [10](#bib.bib6)\]
and world-action models (WAMs) \[[38](#bib.bib38)\] have advanced robot
control, yet they often lack the ability to adapt to dynamic and
uncertain environments. Large language models (LLMs) provide broad
knowledge for reasoning yet lack the ability to perform complex,
real-world tasks \[[1](#bib.bib7), [9](#bib.bib8), [8](#bib.bib15)\].
This complementarity motivates embodied agents that connect reasoning to
action \[[1](#bib.bib7), [9](#bib.bib8), [37](#bib.bib4),
[14](#bib.bib23)\]. However, Harness VLA \[[39](#bib.bib39)\], RoboClaw
\[[13](#bib.bib31)\], and HumanCLAW \[[14](#bib.bib23)\] focus on tasks
with specified goals, leaving an evolving world with human involvement
largely unexamined. Other settings, including EmbodiedBench’s high-level
tasks \[[37](#bib.bib4)\], LoTa-Bench \[[5](#bib.bib17)\], ALFWorld’s
text environments \[[28](#bib.bib18)\], and Embodied Agent Interface
\[[12](#bib.bib11)\], abstract physical execution into discrete symbolic
actions. GRBench \[[35](#bib.bib9)\] combines robot execution with
virtual NPC dialogue, while PARTNR \[[4](#bib.bib2)\] evaluates embodied
human-robot collaboration with temporal task constraints. These gaps
motivate a physically and socially grounded foundation for benchmarking
robotic agents in an evolving world, with task outcomes tied to
execution and human interaction.

Insight. Studying embodied intelligence requires physically and socially
grounded execution and tasks revealing capabilities across varied
situations in a changing world
([Sections 4.2](#S4.SS2 "4.2 Main Results ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments"),
[4.3.2](#S4.SS3.SSS2 "4.3.2 Controlled Intervention Study ‣ 4.3 Ablation Study and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")
and [4.3.4](#S4.SS3.SSS4 "4.3.4 Execution Checks ‣ 4.3 Ablation Study and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")).

Challenge 1: Embodied execution is not
symbolic \[[Sections 4.2](#S4.SS2 "4.2 Main Results ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")
and [4.3.4](#S4.SS3.SSS4 "4.3.4 Execution Checks ‣ 4.3 Ablation Study and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")\].
A valid action call does not guarantee successful execution: the robot
must contend with contact, friction, balance, and human responses. The
agent must therefore revise decisions using physical execution feedback.

Challenge 2: The world changes during
execution \[[Sections 4.3.2](#S4.SS3.SSS2 "4.3.2 Controlled Intervention Study ‣ 4.3 Ablation Study and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")
and [4.3.3](#S4.SS3.SSS3 "4.3.3 Failure Analysis ‣ 4.3 Ablation Study and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")\].
Physical conditions, human activity, and task requirements can change
while a plan unfolds. The agent must interpret the current situation and
reconsider what to do next.

Challenge 3: Evaluation must assess diverse
capabilities \[[Section 4.2](#S4.SS2 "4.2 Main Results ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")\].
Within this setting, evaluation must reveal how agents handle varied
situations across multiple capability dimensions. Aggregate success
alone can mask different capability gaps across task families
([Section 4.2](#S4.SS2 "4.2 Main Results ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")).

We introduce TARSphere, a robot-oriented full-stack framework for
embodied agents under physical and social constraints. TARSphere-Infra
couples robot controllers with embodied NPCs in a physics simulator
(Challenge 1). An event-driven FSM maintains world and task states,
while TARSphere-Harness combines observations, memory, skills, and
execution feedback for replanning (Challenge 2). We build
TARSphere-Bench through agent-in-the-loop curation, pairing
navigate-and-report and fetch-and-deliver tasks with five capability
conditions: perceptual robustness, disruption adaptation, task update,
human coordination, and intent understanding (Challenge 3). Paired
variants retain their Base scene and task family.

Across 8 MLLMs, general-purpose models can outperform embodied models,
with capability gaps varying by task family
([Section 4.2](#S4.SS2 "4.2 Main Results ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")).
Planning credit does not ensure successful execution, and collisions and
falls remain common
([Sections 4.2](#S4.SS2 "4.2 Main Results ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")
and [4.3.4](#S4.SS3.SSS4 "4.3.4 Execution Checks ‣ 4.3 Ablation Study and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")).
These findings call for agents that combine general reasoning,
adaptation to world changes, and safe execution
([Sections 4.2](#S4.SS2 "4.2 Main Results ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")
and [4.3.2](#S4.SS3.SSS2 "4.3.2 Controlled Intervention Study ‣ 4.3 Ablation Study and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")).

Our contributions are three-fold.

- •
  We formulate long-horizon embodied tasks under physical and social
  constraints, where execution outcomes and world changes continually
  shape the agent’s decisions.
- •
  We develop TARSphere Framework, where physical execution and
  interaction with embodied humans update task states and supply
  evaluation evidence; an embodied brain harness uses observations,
  memory, executable skills, and world feedback to guide model
  decisions.
- •
  We introduce agent-in-the-loop curation for 360 TARSphere-Bench
  episodes in 30 scenes and five paired capability conditions, aligning
  task semantics, geometry, and execution.

## 2 Related Work

### 2.1 Embodied Interactive Simulation Environments

VirtualHome \[[22](#bib.bib19)\] represents household activities as
executable programs; Habitat \[[27](#bib.bib12)\] and RoboTHOR
\[[6](#bib.bib21)\] support navigation and indoor interaction. Habitat
2.0 \[[29](#bib.bib25)\] adds rearrangement, and BEHAVIOR-1K
\[[11](#bib.bib24)\] broadens household activities and object-state
changes. RoboCasa \[[20](#bib.bib3)\] provides diverse kitchen scenes
and composite tasks, while ManiSkill3 \[[30](#bib.bib14)\] enables
GPU-parallel contact-rich manipulation. Human participation introduces
additional demands: Habitat 3.0 \[[23](#bib.bib1)\] studies social
navigation and collaborative rearrangement; PARTNR \[[4](#bib.bib2)\]
evaluates household collaboration under spatial, temporal, and
capability constraints. GRBench \[[35](#bib.bib9)\] combines robot
execution with virtual NPC dialogue. TARSphere couples physical robot
execution, embodied NPC behavior, and changing task requirements in one
event-driven environment. Communication depends on proximity,
visibility, hearing range, and human availability, while completed
actions and delivered messages commit task-state changes. Paired
conditions compare adaptation and interaction within matched scenes and
task families, relating capability demands to decisions, physical
execution, and completion.

### 2.2 Embodied Foundation Models

SayCan \[[1](#bib.bib7)\] grounds skill selection in executable
behaviors, and Code as Policies \[[15](#bib.bib16)\] generates programs
combining perception, control APIs, and feedback loops. Inner Monologue
\[[9](#bib.bib8)\] uses scene descriptions, success feedback, and human
interaction to revise decisions. SayPlan \[[26](#bib.bib20)\] uses
hierarchical 3D scene graphs and simulator feedback for planning, while
VoxPoser \[[8](#bib.bib15)\] composes observation-grounded 3D value maps
for closed-loop manipulation. Complementary action models, including
RT-2 \[[3](#bib.bib5)\], OpenVLA \[[10](#bib.bib6)\], and GR00T
\[[2](#bib.bib22)\], connect multimodal inputs to robot control;
HY-Embodied \[[33](#bib.bib27)\] targets spatial understanding and
embodied action. TARSphere evaluates frozen multimodal models through a
shared harness exposing bounded primitives, higher-level skills,
hierarchical memory, and measured execution feedback. This setting
examines how model decisions respond to evolving goals, access, and
human needs, alongside ablations of the perception, memory, and skill
interface supporting those decisions.

### 2.3 Robotic Agent Systems and Interfaces

ROS \[[24](#bib.bib28)\] and ROS 2 \[[18](#bib.bib29)\] provide modular
infrastructure for robot perception, planning, and control. OK-Robot
\[[16](#bib.bib26)\] integrates open-vocabulary perception, navigation,
and manipulation for household pick-and-drop tasks. RoboClaw
\[[13](#bib.bib31)\] links data collection, policy learning, and
deployment through a VLM controller orchestrating learned primitives.
ASPIRE \[[17](#bib.bib32)\] repairs control programs and accumulates
reusable skills, while Zetta \[[7](#bib.bib33)\] develops runtime
critics and recovery skills around a frozen policy. To separate
decisions from execution, HumanCLAW \[[14](#bib.bib23)\] studies VLM
decisions through a simulated human body excluding execution-side
disturbances. TARSphere retains robot dynamics and records collisions,
falls, motion residuals, and task outcomes. Its event-driven evaluator
checks evidence against the requirements active when it was produced,
including authorized task revisions and recipient acceptance, supporting
analysis of how physical execution, adaptation, and human interaction
jointly affect completion.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Problem Formulation

We study physically and socially grounded long-horizon embodied
execution in a changing simulated world. The agent interprets human
intent from egocentric observations, communication, and interaction
history, then pursues the required physical outcome or accurate report.

At time $`t_{k}`$, policy $`\pi`$ selects action sequence $`B_{k}`$ from
observation $`o_{t_{k}}`$ and interaction history $`h_{t_{k}}`$:

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
B_{k}=\pi(o_{t_{k}},h_{t_{k}}),\qquad\langle s_{t+1},f_{t}\rangle=\mathcal{W}(s_{t},\operatorname{Exec}(a_{t}),e_{t}),\quad a_{t}\in B_{k}.
``` |  | (1) |

Here $`s_{t}`$ comprises robot, object, human, and active task states;
$`e_{t}`$ is a human or environmental event. $`\operatorname{Exec}`$
invokes the backend; $`\mathcal{W}`$ updates the world and returns
measured feedback $`f_{t}`$. Actions execute sequentially; new
observations and feedback inform the next decision. TARSphere Framework
implements this loop; TARSphere-Bench supplies tasks and controlled
capability conditions there.

### 3.2 TARSphere Framework

TARSphere Framework has two layers: TARSphere-Infra, the Execution
Infrastructure
([Figure 2](#S3.F2 "In 3.2 TARSphere Framework ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")),
manages physical execution, embodied human interaction, task states, and
evaluation. TARSphere-Harness, the Embodied Brain Harness
([Figure 3](#S3.F3 "In 3.2.1 TARSphere-Infra ‣ 3.2 TARSphere Framework ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")),
organizes observations, memory, executable skills, and feedback for a
frozen MLLM. TARSphere-Infra returns synchronized RGB-D and LiDAR
observations, measured feedback, and delivered public events to the
harness; task requirements and scoring are private. We separately test
execution fidelity and harness components
([Sections 4.3.4](#S4.SS3.SSS4 "4.3.4 Execution Checks ‣ 4.3 Ablation Study and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")
and [4.3.1](#S4.SS3.SSS1 "4.3.1 Harness Ablations ‣ 4.3 Ablation Study and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")).

#### 3.2.1 TARSphere-Infra

Simulation and robot execution. In
[Figure 2](#S3.F2 "In 3.2 TARSphere Framework ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments"),
the harness sends robot actions to a shared execution environment
containing G1, task objects, and NPCs in GRScenes-100
\[[35](#bib.bib9)\] scenes. Isaac Sim \[[21](#bib.bib30)\] and PhysX
execute robot control under contact and friction: locomotion follows a
learned policy, while grasping executes planned arm motion and requires
physical object acquisition and retention
([Section 4.3.4](#S4.SS3.SSS4 "4.3.4 Execution Checks ‣ 4.3 Ablation Study and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")).
Episode reset and synchronization keep sensing, robot control, and NPC
motion consistent. Kinematic motion and skeletal animation keep NPCs’
visible poses aligned with their collision proxies, so visible human
motion and robot contact refer to the same embodied person throughout
execution and interaction.

Embodied NPCs and constrained dialogue. The NPC runtime maintains each
person’s pose, behavior, availability, and authorized knowledge. Its LLM
backend routes natural questions to authorized topics and returns
episode-defined replies. Robot-to-NPC queries require proximity and
visibility; NPC speech reaches the robot only within hearing range, so
available information depends on the current spatial configuration.

Event-driven task state machine. The FSM
([Figure 2](#S3.F2 "In 3.2 TARSphere Framework ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments"))
tracks event and NPC action status and active task bindings. It admits
events when DAG prerequisites and live physical and interaction
conditions hold; NPC actions follow *Idle–Queued–Acting–Done*. Confirmed
action completion and actual message delivery commit state transitions,
applying authorized updates and enabling dependent stages. The evaluator
scores five dependency-checked outcomes against the applicable
requirements; task-update events are unscored. A fixed semantic judge
scores plans and reports; handover requires physical execution and
recipient acceptance. We compare semantic scores with human judgments
([Section 4.4](#S4.SS4 "4.4 Human Evaluation ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments"))
and trace responses to task updates through message delivery, goal
revision, and physical completion
([Sections 4.3.2](#S4.SS3.SSS2 "4.3.2 Controlled Intervention Study ‣ 4.3 Ablation Study and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")
and [4.3.3](#S4.SS3.SSS3 "4.3.3 Failure Analysis ‣ 4.3 Ablation Study and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")).

#### 3.2.2 Embodied Brain Harness

Closed-loop agent–world interaction. Execution outcomes and world
changes can invalidate a plan
([Sections 4.3.2](#S4.SS3.SSS2 "4.3.2 Controlled Intervention Study ‣ 4.3 Ablation Study and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")
and [4.3.3](#S4.SS3.SSS3 "4.3.3 Failure Analysis ‣ 4.3 Ablation Study and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")).
TARSphere-Harness executes actions serially and can return control at
tool boundaries, canceling the remaining actions. Each decision receives
current egocentric RGB, body-frame LiDAR, public events, and execution
feedback through
[Figure 3](#S3.F3 "In 3.2.1 TARSphere-Infra ‣ 3.2 TARSphere Framework ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")(a,
f). Motion residuals and collision bearings report movement error and
contact direction for subsequent decisions. We ablate motion residuals
and LiDAR observations to examine their contributions to replanning and
task completion
([Section 4.3.1](#S4.SS3.SSS1 "4.3.1 Harness Ablations ‣ 4.3 Ablation Study and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")).

Multimodal context and hierarchical memory. As views and task
requirements change, TARSphere-Harness retains context through current
observations and the memory layers in
[Figure 3](#S3.F3 "In 3.2.1 TARSphere-Infra ‣ 3.2 TARSphere Framework ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")(a,
b), whose contributions we examine through ablations
([Section 4.3.1](#S4.SS3.SSS1 "4.3.1 Harness Ablations ‣ 4.3 Ablation Study and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")).
*Short-term history* retains recent actions, outcomes, and visual-state
notes; *long-term records* retain the active plan, dialogue, and robot
state. *Semantic spatial memory* grounds model-selected people, objects,
and places in synchronized RGB-D, storing measured locations and
supporting keyframes alongside the online geometric map. *Self-managed
key memory* lets the model retain, revise, or forget selected facts
during execution.

Unified multi-level skill interface. Through the interface in
[Figure 3](#S3.F3 "In 3.2.1 TARSphere-Infra ‣ 3.2 TARSphere Framework ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")(d),
the model combines bounded *primitives* for body, gaze, and end-effector
motion; *manipulation skills* for locating and grasping visible targets;
and *navigation skills* for reaching points, visual frontiers, or
remembered entities using the online map. Social actions use the same
interface with spatial preconditions. The controller backends in
[Figure 3](#S3.F3 "In 3.2.1 TARSphere-Infra ‣ 3.2 TARSphere Framework ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")(e)
can be replaced by vision-language navigation and action policies. We
ablate navigation and grasp skills and separately assess execution
accuracy
([Sections 4.3.1](#S4.SS3.SSS1 "4.3.1 Harness Ablations ‣ 4.3 Ablation Study and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")
and [4.3.4](#S4.SS3.SSS4 "4.3.4 Execution Checks ‣ 4.3 Ablation Study and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")).

### 3.3 TARSphere-Bench

Task Design and Benchmark Taxonomy. We construct 360 TARSphere-Bench
episodes for navigate-and-report and fetch-and-deliver in 30
GRScenes-100 \[[35](#bib.bib9)\] scenes: 10 homes and 20 commercial
spaces. Each Base episode has five paired conditions sharing its scene,
task family, and five-stage evaluation. We test perceptual robustness
(PR) through sensing changes and disruption adaptation (DA) through
temporary blockage or person/tool unavailability while the goal remains
fixed. For task update (TU), an authorized event revises the task; human
coordination (HC) requires another person’s information or onsite work,
and intent understanding (IU) requires clarification or contextual
inference. HC seeks help toward a known goal; IU resolves what the
person wants. Base comparisons quantify each condition’s effects
([Section 4.2](#S4.SS2 "4.2 Main Results ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")).

## 4 Experiments

### 4.1 Experimental Setup

We evaluate 8 frozen MLLMs: four proprietary models, GPT-5.6-Terra,
Claude Sonnet-5, Gemini-3.8-Flash, and Qwen3.7-Plus; two general-purpose
open VLMs, Qwen3.8-27B \[[25](#bib.bib36)\] and Gemma-4-31B
\[[32](#bib.bib37)\]; and two open embodied models, Hy-Embodied-VLM-1.0
\[[36](#bib.bib34)\] and RoboBrain2.0-32B \[[31](#bib.bib35)\]. All use
TARSphere-Harness on TARSphere-Bench’s 360 episodes, sharing prompts,
observations, actions, a 100-decision cap, and termination rules. By
default, decisions use the current image and 10 text-history steps to
propose 1-5 actions. All model weights remain frozen, allowing
comparisons through the same harness without benchmark-specific
training, parameter updates, or adaptation to the evaluation tasks.

Evaluation Metrics. We use quality scoring for the main results and
ablations. The five stages are task understanding, onsite target
observation, onsite task completion, recipient observation, and final
task completion. The onsite outcome is an inspection conclusion, an
answer received from a source, or a physical grasp, depending on the
task. For episode $`e`$, let $`g_{e,i}\in\{0,1\}`$ indicate eligible
evidence for stage $`i`$ under its event dependencies and live
conditions, and let $`q_{e,i}\in[0,1]`$ score that evidence. Plan,
inspection, and delivered-report quality are continuous; observation,
information receipt, and physical outcomes are binary.

Let $`B_{m,t}^{+}`$ contain successful scored Base episodes for model
$`m`$ and task family $`t`$. We report

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathrm{Prog}(e)=\frac{1}{5}\sum_{i=1}^{5}g_{e,i}q_{e,i},\quad\mathrm{Succ}(e)=c_{e}(1-u_{e}),\quad\mathrm{Eff}_{m,t}=\frac{1}{|B_{m,t}^{+}|}\sum_{e\in B_{m,t}^{+}}\frac{L_{m,e}}{d_{e}^{\star}}.
``` |  | (2) |

Here $`c_{e}`$ indicates a correct, complete report to the intended
recipient or accepted handover of the correct object, with all
prerequisites fulfilled; $`u_{e}`$ indicates terminal fall or object
loss.

Initial plan and inspection quality contribute to Progress; Success
depends on the completed task. Terminal physical failure preserves
earned Progress; ordinary contact alone does not veto Success. Scores
average valid runs and are shown as percentages; Avg. equally weights
the six conditions.

In the path ratio, $`L_{m,e}`$ integrates pelvis XY movement at native
physics steps, excluding reset and warmup, and $`d_{e}^{\star}`$ is the
authored Base route length. Eff. uses successful Base runs with complete
paths; unavailable entries are dashes. We also report collision/fall
incidence and decision/token cost over successful and failed runs.

#### 4.3.2 Controlled Intervention Study

Using a dedicated sample of 30 fetch-and-deliver Base tasks and their
DA, TU, and HC variants, we evaluate matched locomotion, attachment,
grounding, and mapping interventions to assess whether execution
assistance narrows capability gaps under unchanged task requirements.
For condition $`c`$ and assistance setting $`a`$, let $`S_{c}^{a}`$
denote Success in percent. The within-setting capability gap and its
change from normal execution are

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
G_{c}^{a}=S_{c}^{a}-S_{\mathrm{Base}}^{a},\qquad I_{c}^{a}=G_{c}^{a}-G_{c}^{\mathrm{normal}}.
``` |  | (3) |

Positive $`I_{c}^{a}`$ narrows a negative Base-relative gap; the same
definitions apply to Progress.
[Table 4](#S4.T4 "In 4.3.1 Harness Ablations ‣ 4.3 Ablation Study and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")
reports these descriptive contrasts for matched runs under the same task
requirements and evaluation rules.

Execution gains do not imply smaller capability gaps. Full assistance
improves Success across all conditions, but narrows the DA deficit by
only 3.3 percentage points and leaves the TU/HC deficits unchanged at
13.3/16.7 points. HC shows the largest Progress gain ($`+7.3`$) without
a smaller Success gap relative to Base. Thus, the tested execution
assistance improves completion but does not eliminate sensitivity to
task updates and human coordination. Crossing execution interventions
with capability variants reveals which deficits they reduce, extending
evaluation beyond aggregate model rankings.

## 5 Conclusion

TARSphere grounds long-horizon robot tasks in shared physical and social
constraints
([Sections 4.2](#S4.SS2 "4.2 Main Results ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")
and [4.3.4](#S4.SS3.SSS4 "4.3.4 Execution Checks ‣ 4.3 Ablation Study and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")).
TARSphere Framework couples robot dynamics, embodied NPC interaction,
and state-machine control and evaluation; TARSphere-Harness lets frozen
MLLMs act through memory, executable skills, and measured feedback
([Sections 4.3.1](#S4.SS3.SSS1 "4.3.1 Harness Ablations ‣ 4.3 Ablation Study and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")
and [4.4](#S4.SS4 "4.4 Human Evaluation ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments")).
TARSphere-Bench pairs two service task families with five capability
demands, supporting trajectory-based comparisons of model decisions,
completion, and execution quality as demands change during execution,
including revised goals and dependencies on human participation.

Limitations and Future Work. We evaluate one robot body and two indoor
service task families in simulation. Real-robot transfer must address
differences in sensing, contact, and human behavior. We plan
Mobile-Franka transfer and task-adapted VLA, navigation, and
manipulation backends.
````
