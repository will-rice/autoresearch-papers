###### Abstract

A research community is only as strong as the way it divides its
cognitive labour. The philosopher Philip Kitcher argued that researchers
should not all pursue the most promising approach. Credit rules, not a
central plan, keep some of them on other lines. We bring this question
to autonomous research, where Large Language Model agents improve
programs against a deterministic grader. Agents that share only a score
follow the leader and converge onto one approach. Coral Discovery gives
the community a direction board. A verifier labels every attempt with a
direction, the mechanism earning the score, and a method, one change
within it. Per direction, the board tracks how much each eval improves
the score, adds an exploration bonus for little-tried directions, prices
the option of opening a new direction, and suggests how many agents each
direction deserves. Credit is earned against a direction’s own best, so
a lagging direction keeps its agents. Agents finish their lines, then
treat the board as advice. We test on nine tasks with the same model and
budget, without grader access except for Kernel. Coral Discovery beats
the strongest open autonomous multi-agent system on 6 tasks, ties on 1,
and loses on 2. Against published numbers, it is best or tied on 4. It
improves the reported Kernel result from 1103 to 1095 cycles and passes
1103 at eval 275, under half of the 596 used before. It sets a new
Polyominoes state of the art, 91.78 against 87.0. Direction labels show
that the leading scores rest on directions a score-only community never
entered. Code will be released shortly.

## 1 Introduction

Figure 1: The division of cognitive labour in a community of research

spreads its agents over them, keeps opening new ones, and earns credit
against each direction’s own best, so a lagging direction keeps its

[Sections 5](#S5 "5 Results ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research")
and [6](#S6 "6 Analysis: Two Why-Questions ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research")
quantify both.

Science is done by communities, and a community’s progress depends on
how it divides its cognitive labour. [Kitcher (1990)](#bib.bib10) argued
that a group of researchers should not all pursue the single most
promising approach. A distribution that keeps some of them on less
promising lines explores more of the space. This distribution does not
require a central planner. Credit rules, above all the priority that
goes to whoever is first on a line, make credit-seeking researchers
spread themselves over approaches. [Weisberg and Muldoon
(2009)](#bib.bib27) made the picture concrete with an epistemic
landscape. Followers who climb towards the best known result crowd onto
one peak. A community that also rewards work on other peaks covers the
landscape. [Kuhn (1977)](#bib.bib12) called the balance between pursuing
the established line and striking out on a new one the essential tension
of research.

Autonomous research now lets us run this question as an experiment. Many
important problems have no ready-made answer but do have a definite
criterion of judgement. A logistics heuristic is judged by its cost on
instances ([Chen et al., 2026](#bib.bib6)), and a kernel by its cycle
count ([Ouyang et al., 2025](#bib.bib20)). A mathematical construction
is judged by substituting it into the objective function. Placing a
large language model inside an evaluator-guided search loop has become
an effective way to tackle such problems. FunSearch ([Romera-Paredes et
al., 2023](#bib.bib24)), AlphaEvolve ([Novikov et al.,
2025](#bib.bib19)), OpenEvolve ([Sharma, 2025](#bib.bib25)),
ShinkaEvolve ([Lange et al., 2026](#bib.bib13)), and EvoX ([Liu et al.,
2026](#bib.bib15)) allow the model to propose candidates. A fixed
evolutionary algorithm decides which parents to select, what to retain,
and when to evaluate. A more recent line hands these decisions to
long-running agents ([Qu et al., 2026](#bib.bib22)). The agents
retrieve, propose, evaluate, and write back in their own workspaces, and
they collaborate through a shared memory. Several equal agents, a
deterministic grader, and a shared memory form a small research
community. We can observe its division of labour at every evaluation and
change it by design.

Left to a shared memory alone, the community behaves like Weisberg and
Muldoon’s followers. The shared memory of such systems is organised
around a leaderboard sorted by score. It answers “what have others done,
and who scores highest”. Facing it, an agent has two choices: to modify
along the leader’s line or to start from scratch on its own. In our
runs, the first choice produced groups in which three out of four agents
worked in the same direction for most of the run, and whole thirds of a
run fell into a single direction. The second choice gave up
collaboration altogether. What the leaderboard cannot answer is the
question Kitcher asked. Under a limited budget, when should an agent dig
deeper into an existing approach (exploitation), and when should it move
to a different one (exploration)? The leaderboard has neither “approach”
as a unit nor “rate of return” as a quantity.

We propose Coral Discovery, a direction board that provides a community
of research agents with this missing unit and this missing quantity. A
credit rule keeps lagging approaches alive
([Figure 1](#S1.F1 "In 1 Introduction ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research")).
We maintain the setting of autonomous multi-agent evolution. A task is
defined by a description $`x`$ and an evaluator $`E`$. Evaluating a
candidate $`y`$ returns $`E(x,y)=(s,f)`$ with a score $`s`$ and feedback
$`f`$. $`N`$ agents run asynchronously in their own workspaces, each
repeating “retrieve context $`\rightarrow`$ propose a candidate
$`\rightarrow`$ evaluate $`\rightarrow`$ write back to shared memory”.
We add three components, summarised in
[Figure 2](#S1.F2 "In Contributions. ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research").

Two-level labelling into directions and methods, plus a verifier. Each
attempt carries two labels. The direction, an approach in Kitcher’s
sense, is the mechanism by which the program earns its score. A verifier
determines it from reading the diff. The method, what was changed this
time, is written by the agent itself. The direction is the unit of
budget allocation and return statistics. The method is the working
record inside a direction.

The direction board. For each direction the board maintains four
quantities. Yield is how much improvement each eval spent buys.
Potential is what the direction is worth once the uncertainty of having
been tried little is added. Wanted is a suggested head count allocated
by potential, and claimed is how many agents are on it now. It also
treats opening another direction as an option, NEW, whose value is
estimated from the past performance of new directions. Credit is
measured against a direction’s own best. This is the priority rule of
the community: a record inside a lagging direction is worth as much as a
record inside the leading one.

The round protocol. In each round, an agent first judges whether its own
line is finished. It reads the board only once it is, claims a direction
and a method, edits freely, runs one eval and writes an insight. The
board only directs attention, and the decision rests with the agent. The
verifier intervenes at two points, claim and eval.

## 2 Related Work

#### Automated research with LLMs.

FunSearch ([Romera-Paredes et al., 2023](#bib.bib24)) and AlphaEvolve
([Novikov et al., 2025](#bib.bib19)) place a language model inside an
evaluator-guided evolutionary loop. OpenEvolve ([Sharma,
2025](#bib.bib25)), ShinkaEvolve ([Lange et al., 2026](#bib.bib13)),
EvoX ([Liu et al., 2026](#bib.bib15)), AdaEvolve ([Cemri et al.,
2026](#bib.bib5)), GEPA ([Agrawal et al., 2026](#bib.bib1)) and
TTT-Discover ([Yuksekgonul et al., 2026](#bib.bib29)) refine that loop,
but a fixed algorithm still decides what to retain. The AI Scientist
([Lu et al., 2024](#bib.bib16)), the AI co-scientist ([Gottweis et al.,
2026](#bib.bib8)) and MIT CORAL ([Qu et al., 2026](#bib.bib22)) hand the
loop to autonomous agents. Coral Discovery keeps the autonomous setting
and adds the layer that divides the community’s evaluations over
approaches.

#### Multi-agent systems.

MetaGPT ([Hong et al., 2024](#bib.bib9)), ChatDev ([Qian et al.,
2024](#bib.bib21)), AutoGen ([Wu et al., 2024](#bib.bib28)) and CAMEL
([Li et al., 2023](#bib.bib14)) trade one long context for division of
labour and negotiation. AgentRadio ([Ren et al., 2026](#bib.bib23))
shows that the gains concentrate on decomposable tasks, and that
interdependent subtasks need coordination during execution. Coral
Discovery divides a single objective over directions instead of
subtasks. A shared, verified ledger replaces negotiation.

#### Exploration and exploitation.

UCB1 ([Auer et al., 2002](#bib.bib3)), UCT ([Kocsis and Szepesvári,
2006](#bib.bib11); [Coulom, 2007](#bib.bib7); [Browne et al.,
2012](#bib.bib4)) with progressive widening as in AlphaGo ([Silver et
al., 2016](#bib.bib26)), and quality-diversity methods such as
MAP-Elites ([Mouret and Clune, 2015](#bib.bib18)) are the standard
tools. The board treats directions as arms. Yield is a windowed mean,
potential is a UCB-style bonus on lifetime counts, and NEW is an
expansion arm. Two differences matter. The reward is a direction’s own
new high rather than the raw score, and the policy is advisory, since
the agent reads the evidence and decides.

## 3 Method: Coral Discovery

Coral Discovery adds directions and methods with their verifier
([Section 3.1](#S3.SS1 "3.1 Directions and Methods ‣ 3 Method: Coral Discovery ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research")),
the direction board
([Section 3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 The Direction Board ‣ 3 Method: Coral Discovery ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research"))
and the round protocol
([Section 3.3](#S3.SS3 "3.3 Protocol ‣ 3 Method: Coral Discovery ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research")).

### 3.1 Directions and Methods

#### Definitions.

A direction is the mechanism by which a program earns its score. It has
three parts: the objective or proxy optimised, the solution object or
decision unit, and the principle of search or construction. Programs
with one mechanism share a direction, however they are written. A method
is a concrete change within a direction: a parameter, variant,
reordering, substitution or speed-up. The verifier assigns the
direction, which is the unit of allocation and return statistics. The
agent supplies the method on the node card.

Figure 3: Directions and methods. Left: the two definitions, with a
direction being an approach in Kitcher’s sense. Middle: the verifier’s
judgement at every eval, with its verdict recorded on the node card.
Right: code lineage (solid edges) and direction membership (node
colours) are independent; borrowing is declared with --inspired-by
(dashed edges), and direction labels attach to nodes rather than
subtrees.

#### Verifier.

The verifier is a tool-less single-turn session of the agents’ model,
run at every coral eval. It reads the task description, the direction
table, the (direction, method) pairs of parent and candidate, the diff
and the declared inspired-by nodes. It answers one question: does the
candidate preserve the mechanism by which the parent earns its score? If
so, the candidate inherits the parent’s direction. Otherwise it joins a
matching or new direction, and provisional duplicates of earlier
mechanisms are merged. The verdict defaults to “preserved” when
uncertain and goes on the node card. Calls run serially, and a 5-minute
timeout keeps the agent’s declaration. Code lineage and direction
membership are independent graphs
([Figure 3](#S3.F3 "In Definitions. ‣ 3.1 Directions and Methods ‣ 3 Method: Coral Discovery ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research")).
A direction labels nodes, not subtrees.

### 3.2 The Direction Board

The board keeps four quantities per direction. Let $`W`$ be the window
length, $`N`$ the evals in the window, $`n_{d}`$ and
$`n_{d}^{\mathrm{all}}`$ the evals of direction $`d`$ in the window and
over the run, and $`m`$ the mean credit in the window. Failed evals have
credit 0, and minimisation scores are negated. Defaults are $`W=32`$,
$`\mathrm{prior}_{n}=4`$, $`k=3`$, $`\kappa=0.05`$ and
$`n_{\mathrm{assign}}`$ the number of agents.

#### Credit.

The credit of node $`X`$ is its score above a threshold, normalised by
the current global best
([Equation 1](#S3.E1 "In Credit. ‣ 3.2 The Direction Board ‣ 3 Method: Coral Discovery ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research")):

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
\operatorname{credit}(X)=\frac{\max\!\Bigl(0,\,s(X)-\max\bigl(s(\mathrm{parent}),\ \max_{i\in\text{inspired-by}}s(i),\ \mathrm{best}_{d(X)}\bigr)\Bigr)}{\lvert\text{global best}\rvert}
``` |  | (1) |

The threshold includes the direction best, so only a direction record
earns credit. Copying a better program merely raises the threshold.
Directions are therefore compared on what they add rather than on what
they borrow. Normalisation keeps the constants below task-independent.

#### Yield.

The yield of direction $`d`$ is its mean credit per eval in the window,
shrunk towards the overall mean with $`\mathrm{prior}_{n}`$ pseudo-evals
([Equation 2](#S3.E2 "In Yield. ‣ 3.2 The Direction Board ‣ 3 Method: Coral Discovery ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research")):

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathrm{yield}_{d}=\frac{\sum_{X\in\text{window},\,d(X)=d}\operatorname{credit}(X)+\mathrm{prior}_{n}\cdot m}{n_{d}+\mathrm{prior}_{n}}
``` |  | (2) |

Shrinkage keeps a sparsely sampled direction near the mean. The window
returns a stalled direction there within $`W`$ evals.

#### Potential.

Potential adds an exploration bonus to yield
([Equation 3](#S3.E3 "In Potential. ‣ 3.2 The Direction Board ‣ 3 Method: Coral Discovery ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research")):

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathrm{potential}_{d}=\mathrm{yield}_{d}+c\sqrt{\ln N/(n_{d}^{\mathrm{all}}+1)},\qquad c=\kappa\,y^{+}
``` |  | (3) |

where $`y^{+}`$ is the highest yield in the run. Whole-run counts keep
an abandoned direction from looking untried. Because $`y^{+}`$ never
decays, the bonus keeps proposing probes after all directions stall.

#### NEW.

How many directions a task rewards is unknown in advance. The NEW arm
estimates it from the run. If new directions gained soon after opening,
opening more pays. Otherwise depth pays. Consider every direction $`d`$
opened after a global best $`G_{d}`$ existed. Let $`g_{d}`$ be the
improvement over $`G_{d}`$ of its best score in its first $`k`$ evals
(its infancy), truncated at 0, and let
$`n_{d}^{0}=\min(k,n_{d}^{\mathrm{all}})`$.
[Equation 4](#S3.E4 "In NEW. ‣ 3.2 The Direction Board ‣ 3 Method: Coral Discovery ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research")
gives the estimate and its potential:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\hat{y}_{\mathrm{NEW}}=\frac{\sum_{d}g_{d}+k\,y^{+}}{\sum_{d}n_{d}^{0}+k},\qquad\mathrm{potential}_{\mathrm{NEW}}=\hat{y}_{\mathrm{NEW}}+c\sqrt{\ln N/\bigl(\textstyle\sum_{d}n_{d}^{0}+1\bigr)}
``` |  | (4) |

With $`k`$ pseudo-evals of prior $`y^{+}`$, $`\hat{y}_{\mathrm{NEW}}`$
equals $`y^{+}`$ before any opening, and $`y^{+}/(j+1)`$ after $`j`$
openings without an infancy record. NEW therefore starts high and decays
as the global best rises.

#### Wanted.

Each round, $`n_{\mathrm{assign}}`$ seats go to the directions and NEW
in proportion to potential (largest remainder). When every potential is
0, all seats go to the global-best direction. A direction whose wanted
exceeds its claimed is under-served.

#### Advisory, not binding.

Wanted is not enforced, because the four quantities are statistics. A
high-yield direction may have room or be exhausted. A long-untouched
direction lifted by the bonus may be dead or promising. Statistics
cannot tell these apart. They point to the directions worth inspecting,
and the judgement after reading attempts, insights and diffs is the
agent’s.

### 3.3 Protocol

Agents touch the shared state only through coral commands, since native
git is disabled in their workspaces. We inherit the command set of MIT
CORAL (coral checkout, coral show, coral diff, coral skills and coral
eval). We add five commands for the board and the tree (coral board,
coral direction, coral claim, coral insight, coral tree show). We extend
coral eval with a declared direction and inspired-by nodes, and we
withdraw the leaderboard commands.
[Appendix A](#A1 "Appendix A Command Set ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research")
lists the complete set. Each step below names the command it uses.

The manager drives each round in two phases. It injects only the agent’s
own line (direction, method, last 5 scores and bests) and the other
agents’ current claims. (1) After a line best, or with a concrete next
step, the agent re-claims its direction with coral claim without reading
the board. After 5 scored evals without a personal best, a Diagnose
block asks whether the bottleneck is structural or removable. (2)
Otherwise it reads the board with coral board, a direction’s attempts
with coral direction \<id\>, a node card with coral tree show \<commit\>
and a diff with coral show \<hash\> --diff. (3) coral claim \<id\>
--method "…" claims a direction. coral claim --new "\<name\>"
--description "…" --method "…" opens a provisional one. Both pass three
checks: no method repeats another agent’s verbatim, at most 2 agents per
direction (1 in the first round), and no --new duplicating an existing
mechanism. (4) coral checkout \<hash\> takes any node of any direction
as parent, and the agent edits freely. Nodes it borrowed from are
declared at eval time with --inspired-by, which enters them into the
credit threshold. (5) coral eval -m "…" --direction \<id\>
\[--inspired-by c1,c2\] commits, writes the node card and releases the
claim. Scoring and the verifier run in parallel. A round has one eval.
Once both results arrive, the manager interrupts the session and starts
phase 2. (6) coral insight \<commit\> "…" records what happened, why and
what it implies. It is the only write operation of phase 2.

## 4 Experimental Setup

### 4.1 Tasks, Baselines and Cited Numbers

Tasks and scoring follow the MIT CORAL paper ([Qu et al.,
2026](#bib.bib22)). The nine tasks fall into five groups. Kernel
optimisation: Kernel Eng., a VLIW SIMD kernel from [Anthropic
(2026)](#bib.bib2) scored in cycles. Combinatorial packing: Polyominoes
([Mang et al., 2026](#bib.bib17)) and Circle-Pack., both maximising a
packing objective. Analysis and combinatorics: Signal Proc., a filter
scored on held-out signals, and Erdős Over., the minimum overlap
constant. LLM inference systems: EPLB, expert load balancing, and
LLM-SQL, query ordering for prefix caching. Data and cloud systems: Txn
Sched., transaction scheduling for makespan, and Cloudcast, multi-cloud
broadcast cost. The baselines are MIT CORAL under our conditions: one
Claude Code agent, or four sharing a repository and a leaderboard. Coral
Discovery adds the direction board
([Section 3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 The Direction Board ‣ 3 Method: Coral Discovery ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research"))
to the same four agents and eval budget. We also cite numbers for SOTA,
OpenEvolve (OE) ([Sharma, 2025](#bib.bib25)), ShinkaEvolve (SE) ([Lange
et al., 2026](#bib.bib13)), EvoX ([Liu et al., 2026](#bib.bib15)) and
CORAL (single-agent, with 4-agent numbers only on Polyominoes and
Kernel). They are marked ^(†) in
[Section 5.1](#S5.SS1 "5.1 Main results ‣ 5 Results ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research").

### 4.2 Experimental Conditions

The MIT CORAL repository by default allows web research and reading the
grader. Some graders, however, contain data an agent should not see. The
Signal Proc. grader holds the scored test signals, and the systems
graders hold the workloads on which a policy is scored. All three
methods therefore run with no internet access and no reading of the
grader. WebSearch and WebFetch are disabled, the grader directory is on
the Read deny list, and the agent prompt states both rules. The only
exception is Kernel Eng., whose grader is the VLIW simulator the task is
about. We scanned the tool calls of every run afterwards and found no
attempt by any agent to access the internet or to read a grader. The
model is Claude Opus 4.6 (effort medium).

### 4.3 Evaluation and Reporting

Each configuration ran 3 times.
[Section 5.1](#S5.SS1 "5.1 Main results ‣ 5 Results ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research")
reports the best run (best of 3, the counterpart of MIT CORAL’s Bo4)
with its eval count (crashes and timeouts included) and best@k, the
first eval reaching the run’s final best. Except for kernel, only the
first 100 evals count. Cloudcast counts the first 20, within which all
three methods reach the optimal cost 618. NA marks a missing run.

## 6 Analysis: Two Why-Questions

[Section 5.2](#S5.SS2 "5.2 Where the lead comes from ‣ 5 Results ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research")
left two questions, taken in turn here.

### 6.1 Why does Discovery open directions the baseline never does?

The direction board makes opening a direction part of the protocol.
Agents open distinct directions in the first round. A direction holds at
most two agents. The claim check rejects duplicate proposals. The NEW
arm always prices one more direction, and the verifier registers new
directions after every eval. Without these rules, the baseline’s agents
read the same leaderboard and crowd into one approach.
[Figure 5](#S6.F5 "In 6.1 Why does Discovery open directions the baseline never does? ‣ 6 Analysis: Two Why-Questions ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research")
counts the directions opened.

### 6.2 Why does Discovery’s champion direction merge in more directions?

On the three tasks of phenomenon b, Discovery’s champion merged in other
directions’ results because a lagging direction does not die on the
board. A node’s credit is its improvement over the best of where it
stands,
$`\max\bigl(0,\ \text{score}-\max(\text{parent's best},\text{inspired-by node's best},\text{own direction's best})\bigr)`$,
divided by the global best. A direction that progresses on its own track
therefore keeps its yield rising and keeps its agents. Its node cards
let others take its components. The baseline rewards only the global
best and abandons whatever falls behind. Lagging directions on the board
keep setting local records (direction bests short of the global best).
Discovery vs baseline: Signal Proc. 9 vs 0, Circle-Pack. 4 vs 0 and
Erdős Over. 15 vs 4
([Figure 6(a)](#S6.F6.sf1 "In Figure 6 ‣ 6.2 Why does Discovery’s champion direction merge in more directions? ‣ 6 Analysis: Two Why-Questions ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research")).

Exploration and exploitation proceed simultaneously
([Figure 6(b)](#S6.F6.sf2 "In Figure 6 ‣ 6.2 Why does Discovery’s champion direction merge in more directions? ‣ 6 Analysis: Two Why-Questions ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research"),
window HHI over the last 16 evals). The baseline locks in and Discovery
does not. HHI over the last third, baseline vs Discovery, is 1.00 vs
0.66 on Signal Proc., 1.00 vs 0.36 on Circle-Pack. and 0.82 vs 0.29 on
Erdős Over. All three champion directions were opened in the second half
of the run (eval 62 / 100, 34 / 40 and 58 / 60). After the Signal Proc.
champion’s first global record, 76% of the remaining 38 evals went to
it. The HHI therefore rises but rarely reaches 1. The other two first
records came fewer than 8 evals before the end.

above, and it has a price. On two tasks the price was not repaid. Coral

#### LLM-SQL: opening directions brings headroom, but not necessarily the direction with headroom.

Coral Discovery opened 7 directions where the baseline stayed in 2, and
15% of its evals went to directions that never produced a record. Its
champion, tree-row-greedy-col, was opened at eval 12 and absorbed 72
evals for 18 records. Its best still stayed below the baseline’s
lexsort-greedy-prefix, which produced 17 records in the same direction
over 99 evals. The single agent did better than both. It opened
backward-tail-reopt at eval 45, a direction Coral Discovery never found.
The per-direction credit rule that keeps lagging directions alive
([Section 6.2](#S6.SS2 "6.2 Why does Discovery’s champion direction merge in more directions? ‣ 6 Analysis: Two Why-Questions ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research"))
is the same rule that kept agents in a direction with little headroom.
Opening more directions raises the chance of finding headroom. Within a
fixed budget, it does not guarantee that the direction with headroom is
among those opened.

#### Txn Sched.: when one direction decides the outcome, a single agent holds the whole line.

All three methods ended in a single champion direction, and the single
agent won inside the direction it shared with the others. Its
simulated-annealing, opened at eval 2 and worked for 99 evals, reached
4608 at eval 33. Coral Discovery beat the 4-agent baseline through a
direction the baseline never had, adaptive-large-neighborhood-search.
Its 4566 at eval 71 remained below the single agent. We can offer a
conjecture. When the result is decided by method work inside one
direction, a single agent carries the complete history of that
direction. Every step was its own, and it knows which changes paid off.
In a multi-agent system each agent inherits a direction through other
agents’ insights, cards and diffs. It receives the code but not the
process. The four agents each walk only a short segment of the line.

## 8 Conclusion

When several equal agents conduct autonomous research on the same
problem, the gain in speed is immediate. The gain in diversity must be
secured by how the community divides its cognitive labour. A shared
leaderboard alone makes every agent a follower of the leader. Coral
Discovery provides this structure with three components
([Section 3](#S3 "3 Method: Coral Discovery ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research")).
A verifier files every attempt under a direction. A direction board
estimates the return of each direction and treats opening another
direction as an option. A round protocol lets agents finish their own
line first and then take the board as advice. On the nine MIT CORAL
tasks, with no internet access and without reading the grader, it
outperforms the reproduced MIT CORAL system on 6 tasks
([Section 5](#S5 "5 Results ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research")).
Under the same model and the same four-agent budget, it improves the
Kernel Eng. result reported by MIT CORAL from 1103 to 1095 cycles. It
passes 1103 at eval 275, fewer than half of the 596 evals MIT CORAL
used. It also sets a new state of the art on Polyominoes (91.78,
previously 87.0).

Labelling the direction of every submission shows that the leading
scores come from directions the baseline never entered. These directions
could be opened and survive until they produced records because of the
combined effect of the claim rule, the NEW arm and per-direction credit
([Section 6](#S6 "6 Analysis: Two Why-Questions ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research")).
The cost is equally clear
([Section 7](#S7 "7 Limitations: Exploration Has a Cost ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research")).
A budget that is spread out does not necessarily land in a direction
with headroom (LLM-SQL). When the outcome is decided by polishing the
method inside a single direction, several agents taking over each
other’s process do not necessarily beat a single agent that walks the
whole line itself (Txn Sched.).

## Appendix A Command Set

Agents read and write shared state only through coral commands, since
native git is disabled in their workspaces.
[Table 5](#A1.T5 "In Appendix A Command Set ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research")
lists the complete agent-facing command set of Coral Discovery and its
relation to MIT CORAL. Five commands are new. coral board prints every
direction’s statistics, the NEW row, every agent’s claim and the global
best. coral direction lists one direction’s attempts with their credit
and insights. coral claim takes a seat on a direction, or opens a
provisional one with --new, under the three checks of
[Section 3.3](#S3.SS3 "3.3 Protocol ‣ 3 Method: Coral Discovery ‣ Coral Discovery: The Division of Cognitive Labor in Autonomous Research").
coral insight is the only write of phase 2. coral tree show prints a
node card. coral eval is extended with --direction (the declared
direction, defaulting to the current claim) and --inspired-by (nodes
whose code was borrowed, which enter the credit threshold). coral
checkout, show, diff and skills are inherited unchanged. Four MIT CORAL
commands are withdrawn in this mode because the protocol replaces them.
The leaderboard (log) is replaced by board, direction and tree show.
Shared notes are replaced by the method and insight on every node card.
revert is replaced by checkout. The heartbeat prompts are replaced by
the Diagnose block (pivot) and the insight (reflect), with no
counterpart for consolidation.
````
