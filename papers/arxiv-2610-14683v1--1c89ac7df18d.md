###### Abstract

Large language model (LLM)-based AI Scientists advance scientific
discovery, yet they suffer from inefficiencies due to noisy
observations. Existing principle-evolving agents act on every anomaly,
leaving its source unresolved: a new mechanism or an evaluation
artifact, so invalid revisions bias evolution. To address this, we
formulate revision as _evidence admission_: anomalies steer search
immediately, but only independently verified evidence may rewrite
persistent principles, and contradicted principles are retracted. We
realize this in Triage-PiEvo , which pairs an anomaly-responsive search
portfolio with a staged verification gate that alone controls what
enters principle state. Across four scientific tasks with three
backbones, Triage-PiEvo  (1) attains the highest average solution
quality with $`+7.5\%\sim 11.8\%`$ over the strongest baseline per
backbone, matching the strongest baseline’s full-budget quality with
one-third of the evaluation budget on Qwen3-235B; and additional stress
tests and cross-harness deployment demonstrate that (2) in constructed
stress tests, it admits none of the corrupted, hidden-source, or exploit
evidence that ungated variants admit, while missing no planted
mechanism, and (3) plugged unchanged into three external research
harnesses, it cuts false evidence admissions by over $`60\%`$ and raises
verified scores by $`31\%\sim 43\%`$.

## 1 Introduction

Figure 1: Source-aware triage. Latent source $`Z_{t}`$ mediates the
verified retain-or-revise decision.

Autonomous scientific agents now automate scientific research within a
single closed loop ([Lu et al., 2024](#bib.bib8); [Yamada et al.,
2025](#bib.bib3); [Schmidgall et al., 2025](#bib.bib18); [Gottweis et
al., 2025](#bib.bib19)). On the premise that true knowledge often lies
outside the initial hypothesis space, principle-evolution agents ([Pu et
al., 2026](#bib.bib1); [Pu et al., 2025](#bib.bib2)) make every anomaly
a decision in this loop: steer hypothesis search, or rewrite the
persisted theory and explanation.

While surprise-driven revision ([Pu et al., 2026](#bib.bib1)) lets
agents escape their initial hypothesis space, it treats the
observation’s reliability as given. Consequently, the revision may
degrade because it rests on a signal that cannot discriminate, trading
false revisions against missed theory blindly. Nor do existing methods
cover this gap: verification loops critique transient responses rather
than persisted theory ([Shinn et al., 2023](#bib.bib36); [Madaan et al.,
2023](#bib.bib37); [Gou et al., 2024](#bib.bib38); [Dhuliawala et al.,
2024](#bib.bib39)), and self-correction without external evidence is
unreliable ([Huang et al., 2024](#bib.bib40); [Kamoi et al.,
2024](#bib.bib41)). Accordingly, community evaluations find that
auto-research agents produce extensive artifacts yet seldom revise their
internal theory under contradictory evidence ([Ríos-García et al.,
2026](#bib.bib7); [Kirgis et al., 2026](#bib.bib17)). This rigidity
leads to significant inefficiencies: _anomalies are read as evidence of
science, corrupted feedback enters trusted scientific state_.

These limitations reduce to three challenges: (a) one anomaly score has
source ambiguity that cannot tell a new mechanism from corrupted or
misspecified evaluations; (b) the role conflation of one history serves
both search and principle acceptance, so unreliable observations can
rewrite trusted principles; and (c) the revision irreversibility that
accepted principles cannot be retracted under contradictory evidence.

To bridge this gap, we propose Triage , an evidence-admission layer that
decouples anomaly detection from principle revision ([Pu et al.,
2026](#bib.bib1)). Triage treats each anomaly as source-ambiguous and
routes evidence along two separately logged paths: (a) observations
steer candidate selection, while (b) verification records update
per-channel reliability estimates. A principle revision is trusted only
after it survives independent tests on unseen cases, as shown in
Figure [1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents").
We instantiate this in Triage-PiEvo , which pairs Triage  with Active
Flow Expansion ([De Santi et al., 2026](#bib.bib15)): the portfolio
allocates the evaluation budget across exploitation, frontier expansion,
and mechanism-discriminating probes, while the evidence-admission
decides which evidence alters principle state.

We evaluate Triage-PiEvo on four discovery tasks under three backbone
LLMs. Empirical results demonstrate that Triage-PiEvo : (a) attains the
highest performance, raising average solution quality from PiEvo ’s
$`62.87`$–$`70.35`$% to $`79.63`$–$`83.37`$%; (b) admits no corrupted or
exploit-region observations in constructed stress tests where gate-off
variants admit every one, under evaluator corruption and proxy
exploitation alike (the latter further raising held-out score by
$`0.209`$); (c) cuts false expansions on injected noise, surrogate
failure, and non-generalizing local artifacts from rate of $`1.0`$ to
$`0.0`$ in the same tests without missing any planted mechanism, and
backtracks to the correct state after mechanism reversal; and (d)
transfers unchanged to three external research harnesses, cutting false
evidence admissions by over $`60\%`$ and raising verified scores by
$`31\%\sim 43\%`$.

In summary, our contributions are:

1.  (a)
    We introduce Evidence Admission as the decision layer of
    anomaly-driven agents for changing scientific state. Anomalies are
    treated as source-ambiguous, and revision is governed by asymmetric
    costs of false revision versus missed mechanism.
2.  (b)
    We propose Triage-PiEvo , which realizes this layer through
    per-channel trust estimation, a principle lifecycle gated by
    independent held-out support.
3.  (c)
    We conduct extensive evaluations across four scientific tasks and
    three backbones: Triage-PiEvo  raises average solution quality by
    $`+7.5\%\sim 11.8\%`$ over the strongest baseline per backbone, and
    controlled corruption, hidden-source, and proxy-exploitation studies
    show that the gate rejects all unreliable evidence injected in these
    studies while missing no planted mechanism.

## 2 Related Work

Autonomous scientific agents and evolving state. Scientific agents
automate growing shares of the research cycle, from end-to-end paper
production and multi-stage pipelines ([Lu et al., 2024](#bib.bib8);
[Yamada et al., 2025](#bib.bib3); [Tang et al., 2026](#bib.bib4);
[Schmidgall et al., 2025](#bib.bib18); [Gottweis et al.,
2025](#bib.bib19)) to tool-coupled domain execution and closed-loop
laboratory discovery ([Bran et al., 2023](#bib.bib22); [Boiko et al.,
2023](#bib.bib23); [Ruan et al., 2024](#bib.bib49); [Yang et al.,
2025](#bib.bib21); [Ghafarollahi and Buehler, 2025](#bib.bib20); [Ding
et al., 2025](#bib.bib24); [Ghareeb et al., 2026](#bib.bib27);
[Narayanan et al., 2024](#bib.bib50); [Laurent et al.,
2024](#bib.bib25); [Mitchener et al., 2025a](#bib.bib26); [Skarlinski et
al., 2024](#bib.bib29); [Mitchener et al., 2025b](#bib.bib28)). What
persists between iterations there is an artifact, not a revisable
scientific account. A narrower line makes the internal state editable:
PiEvo  and PiFlow expand a principle space on conflicting
observations ([Pu et al., 2026](#bib.bib1); [Pu et al.,
2025](#bib.bib2)), ATLAS selects mechanism-discriminating
experiments ([Éltető et al., 2026](#bib.bib9)), self-revising frameworks
log regime changes ([Wang and Buehler, 2026](#bib.bib10)), and evolving
architectures such as EvoMaster ([Zhu et al., 2026](#bib.bib43)) and
ADMET-EvO ([Zhou et al., 2026](#bib.bib44)) pursue cumulative adaptation
across tasks, with agentic laboratory world models unifying hypothesis
generation and state tracking ([Fu et al., 2026](#bib.bib45)). Editable
state is also corruptible: without explicit evidence admission,
anomalous or corrupted feedback rewrites persistent principles
directly ([Ríos-García et al., 2026](#bib.bib7); [Kirgis et al.,
2026](#bib.bib17)). Triage  separates the history that steers search
from the evidence authorized to alter scientific state.

Active experimentation under heterogeneous sources. In active learning
and Bayesian optimization, one line decides where to spend
evaluations ([MacKay, 1992](#bib.bib30); [Houlsby et al.,
2011](#bib.bib6)), constraining exploration for safety ([Sui et al.,
2015](#bib.bib31)) or scalability ([Eriksson et al., 2019](#bib.bib32)).
Another exploits heterogeneous costs, fidelities, and failure rates
across sources ([Swersky et al., 2013](#bib.bib33); [Kandasamy et al.,
2017](#bib.bib11); [Poloczek et al., 2017](#bib.bib34); [Ghoreishi and
Allaire, 2019](#bib.bib13)), extending to unreliable channels ([Mikkola
et al., 2023](#bib.bib12); [Fan et al., 2024](#bib.bib35)),
quality-controlled loops ([Harris et al., 2025](#bib.bib14)), and
out-of-distribution expansion ([De Santi et al., 2026](#bib.bib15)).
Both decide which observation to acquire _before_ it is run, so an
anomalous outcome inherits whatever reliability the acquisition rule
assumed. The difference is in what the decision protects: multi-source
methods weight a noisy source inside a regression model, while the
object at risk here is a persistent principle that a single admitted
artifact can corrupt for the rest of the run. Our AFE portfolio takes
the acquisition-side view for candidate generation; Triage  makes the
post-observation decision of whether an anomaly may update that
principle.

Verification, provenance, and agent monitoring. In agent systems,
verification targets transient outputs through feedback recycling ([Yao
et al., 2022](#bib.bib5); [Shinn et al., 2023](#bib.bib36); [Madaan et
al., 2023](#bib.bib37)), tool-based checking ([Gou et al.,
2024](#bib.bib38)), independent verification questions ([Dhuliawala et
al., 2024](#bib.bib39)), and evidence accumulation ([Zhang et al.,
2026](#bib.bib16)), though self-correction without external evidence is
unreliable ([Huang et al., 2024](#bib.bib40); [Kamoi et al.,
2024](#bib.bib41)). Related work argues for monitoring agentic systems
before trusting them ([Boston et al., 2026](#bib.bib46)) and for
immutable provenance trails over agent-generated artifacts ([Liu et al.,
2026](#bib.bib47)), while Sibyl ([Rangelov, 2026](#bib.bib48)) uses
temporal backtesting as held-out evidence. Triage  carries these
provenance and monitoring ideas to persistent principles: same-source
agreement keeps observations search-active, acceptance requires
independent held-out support, and contradictory held-out evidence forces
retraction.

## 3 Methodology

Overview. Triage-PiEvo focuses on maintaining a revisable account of
what observations mean. In this context, each experiment has two
possible consequences: (a) guiding the next experiment, and (b) changing
the principles used to interpret future experiments. Based on our
intuition:

_A surprising observation can steer hypothesizing long before it is
reliable enough  
to revise persistent scientific principles._

As shown in
Figure [2](#S3.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents"),
Triage-PiEvo  separates _using an observation_ from _believing its
explanation_: it keeps exploration responsive to new signals while
requiring independent evidence before revising a principle.
Triage-PiEvo  builds on the principle-hypothesis-testing cycle of
PiEvo ([Pu et al., 2026](#bib.bib1)).

This separation is implemented as a feedback loop with three components:
(a) an evidence-admission layer checks whether an unexpected outcome
generalizes to held-out cases
(Section [3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 Escalating from Repeats to Held-Out Probes ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents")),
(b) a search policy proposes experiments from outcomes that have been
admitted for exploration
(Section [3.3](#S3.SS3 "3.3 Proposing Candidates without Seeing the Gate ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents")),
and (c) a principle manager then updates the principle state accordingly
(Section [3.4](#S3.SS4 "3.4 A Gated Principle Lifecycle with Reversible Revision ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents")).
Two histories keep these roles apart: the primary history accumulates
observations the search may act on, while the verification history
records linked checks that alone can justify—or overturn—a principle
revision.

### 3.1 Evidence Admission under Source Ambiguity

We define the objects on which Triage  operates, proceeding in three
steps: the round-level state, the source-ambiguity model, and the
decision objective that any runtime admission rule must respect.

Round state and the anomaly event. The agent interacts with an
environment over rounds $`t=1,\dots,T`$, and each round produces exactly
one record through three steps: (i) the agent proposes a testable
candidate $`x_{t}\in\mathcal{X}`$; (ii) an internal predictor—the
_Gaussian Process (GP) experts_
$`\{\mathcal{M}_{P}\}_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{t}}`$ of PiEvo  ([Pu et al.,
2026](#bib.bib1)), one likelihood model per principle record, fit on the
history of admitted observations—issues a prediction $`\hat{y}_{t}`$
with predictive scale $`\sigma_{t}>0`$; (iii) an observation channel
$`c_{t}\in\mathcal{C}`$ (e.g., an evaluator or data source) evaluates
$`x_{t}`$ and returns an outcome $`y_{t}`$ with evaluator’s metadata
$`m_{t}`$ (e.g., configuration). The tuple
$`(x_{t},\hat{y}_{t},y_{t},c_{t},m_{t})`$ constitutes the record; how
far $`y_{t}`$ deviates from what $`\mathcal{P}_{t}`$ predicted
determines what happens to it next, per
Definition [3.1](#S3.Thmtheorem1 "Definition 3.1 (Anomaly score and anomaly event). ‣ 3.1 Evidence Admission under Source Ambiguity ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents").

###### Definition 3.1 (Anomaly score and anomaly event).

The anomaly score of a record is its standardized residual
$`s_{t}\;\coloneqq\;\nicefrac{{|y_{t}-\hat{y}_{t}|}}{{\sigma_{t}}}`$.
Given a threshold $`\tau_{\mathrm{anom}}`$ fixed before evaluation, the
record is ordinary if $`s_{t}\leq\tau_{\mathrm{anom}}`$ and an anomaly
if $`s_{t}>\tau_{\mathrm{anom}}`$.

An ordinary record is admitted directly to the primary history and
triggers no further action. An anomaly, by contrast, opens a diagnostic
state: it is withheld from search scores and principle updates until the
verification procedure of
Section [3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 Escalating from Repeats to Held-Out Probes ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents")
resolves it.

The anomaly score is blind to its source. A large residual is ambiguous
in origin. We make this explicit by attaching to each evaluation a
source variable $`Z_{t}`$ taking values in

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
\mathcal{Z}\;=\;\underbrace{\{z_{\mathrm{noise}},\,z_{\mathrm{fault}},\,z_{\mathrm{exec}},\,z_{\mathrm{surr}},\,z_{\mathrm{ood}},\,z_{\mathrm{drift}}\}}_{\text{artifact sources }\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{art}}}\;\cup\;\underbrace{\{z_{\mathrm{mech}}\}}_{\text{new mechanism}},
``` |  | (1) |

denoting measurement noise, device fault, execution failure, mismatch of
the external surrogate evaluator, out-of-distribution input,
distribution drift, and a genuine new mechanism. Exactly one source
generates each evaluation, and only $`z_{\mathrm{mech}}`$ justifies
revision; the remaining *artifact sources*
$`\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{art}}`$ should leave the principle state
untouched. Because $`s_{t}`$ is a deterministic function of
$`(y_{t},\hat{y}_{t},\sigma_{t})`$ and not of $`Z_{t}`$, the anomaly
score is uninformative about which source produced it; thresholding
$`s_{t}`$ alone therefore cannot separate a discovery from an artifact.
The consequences of deciding wrongly are asymmetric, as
Example [3.2](#S3.Thmtheorem2 "Example 3.2 (Two anomalies with identical scores). ‣ 3.1 Evidence Admission under Source Ambiguity ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents")
illustrates.

###### Example 3.2 (Two anomalies with identical scores).

Suppose two rounds produce the same large score $`s_{t}`$. In round
$`t_{1}`$, a corrupted evaluator channel ($`z_{\mathrm{fault}}`$)
inflates the outcome of a mediocre candidate; revising
$`\mathcal{P}_{t}`$ on this record redirects every subsequent prediction
toward an artifact, corrupting all future rounds. In round $`t_{2}`$, a
genuinely novel candidate ($`z_{\mathrm{mech}}`$) violates the current
principles; retaining $`\mathcal{P}_{t}`$ merely postpones the discovery
until stronger evidence arrives. The first error propagates through
persistent state; the second only costs time.

To address this asymmetry, we define the admission decision as choosing
$`d\in\{\mathsf{retain}\,,\mathsf{revise}\,\}`$ after observing an
anomaly, with loss

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
L(d,z)=\ell_{\mathrm{FR}}\,\mathbb{1}[d=\mathsf{revise}\,,\,z\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{art}}]+\ell_{\mathrm{FM}}\,\mathbb{1}[d=\mathsf{retain}\,,\,z=z_{\mathrm{mech}}],
``` |  | (2) |

where $`\ell_{\mathrm{FR}}`$ and $`\ell_{\mathrm{FM}}`$ price false
revision and false retention, respectively, with
$`\ell_{\mathrm{FR}}\gg\ell_{\mathrm{FM}}`$ per
Example [3.2](#S3.Thmtheorem2 "Example 3.2 (Two anomalies with identical scores). ‣ 3.1 Evidence Admission under Source Ambiguity ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents").
Under this loss, the risk-optimal action is to revise only when the
posterior $`q(z_{\mathrm{mech}}\mid y_{t},\hat{y}_{t},m_{t})`$ exceeds
$`\ell_{\mathrm{FR}}/(\ell_{\mathrm{FR}}+\ell_{\mathrm{FM}})`$—a
threshold close to one.

Remark. The *reference model* of
Eq. ([2](#S3.E2 "Equation 2 ‣ 3.1 Evidence Admission under Source Ambiguity ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents"))
dictates three essential properties: (i) anomaly detection must trigger
further evidence collection; (ii) the evidence required for revision
must be independent of the channel $`c_{t}`$; and (iii) revising
persistent state must demand strictly stronger evidence than search use
does.

### 3.2 Escalating from Repeats to Held-Out Probes

Because an anomaly may affect candidate search, principle proposal, or
principle revision, Triage  applies three checks (C1$`\sim`$C3) of
increasing independence from the originating experiment channel
$`c_{t}`$, stopping at the first failure. Each check rules out one class
of artifact sources in $`\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{art}}`$ (see
Eq. ([1](#S3.E1 "Equation 1 ‣ 3.1 Evidence Admission under Source Ambiguity ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents")))
and unlocks exactly one privilege.

C1: Rules out transient faults; unlocks search use. Re-evaluating
$`x_{t}`$ on $`c_{t}`$ yields $`v_{t}^{c_{t}}`$. Agreement,
$`|y_{t}-v_{t}^{c_{t}}|\leq\epsilon_{\mathrm{rep}}`$, excludes transient
noise and execution failures ($`z_{\mathrm{noise}},z_{\mathrm{exec}}`$).
This cannot authorize revision, since repeating on $`c_{t}`$ replicates
whatever systematic bias $`c_{t}`$ carries.

C2: Rules out channel-specific corruption; unlocks revision proposals.
Re-evaluating $`x_{t}`$ on an independent channel
$`c^{\prime}\neq c_{t}`$ yields $`v_{t}^{c^{\prime}}`$. Agreement,
$`|v_{t}^{c_{t}}-v_{t}^{c^{\prime}}|\leq\epsilon_{\mathrm{ind}}`$,
excludes corruption specific to $`c_{t}`$ ($`z_{\mathrm{fault}}`$), so
the anomaly may propose a principle and become provisional (see
Section [3.4](#S3.SS4 "3.4 A Gated Principle Lifecycle with Reversible Revision ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents")).

C3: Rules out non-generalizing artifacts; unlocks acceptance. Evaluation
by an independent evaluator on held-out inputs excludes effects tied to
the original evaluation context
($`z_{\mathrm{surr}},z_{\mathrm{ood}},z_{\mathrm{drift}}`$), and is the
only evidence the acceptance gate counts (see
Section [3.4](#S3.SS4 "3.4 A Gated Principle Lifecycle with Reversible Revision ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents")).
A record failing any check is quarantined—excluded from both histories’
influence until resolved; a verified replacement may remain
search-active but cannot update persistent state by itself.

Reconciliation outcomes update per-channel reliability, so that
frequently contradicted channels are progressively excluded from
verification duty. Each channel $`c`$ carries a reliability estimate
$`r_{c}\sim\operatorname{Beta}(\alpha_{c},\beta_{c})`$ with trust score
$`p_{c}^{\mathrm{trust}}=\alpha_{c}/(\alpha_{c}+\beta_{c})`$; agreement
increments $`\alpha_{c}`$ and disagreement increments $`\beta_{c}`$ for
every participating channel (see
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ Appendix G Algorithmic Specification of the TRIAGE Decision Protocol ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents")).

Remark. Trust selects which channels may verify, but does not lower the
standard of evidence. All thresholds are fixed before evaluation and are
independent of $`p_{c}^{\mathrm{trust}}`$, so no amount of accumulated
trust lets a channel certify its own anomalies.

### 3.3 Proposing Candidates without Seeing the Gate

Figure 3: AFE portfolio schematic. Three complementary roles on a shared
design manifold: exploitation ($`\uparrow\hat{\mu}`$), frontier
expansion ($`\uparrow N,D`$), and mechanism probes
($`\uparrow\hat{\sigma}`$).

We use Active Flow Expansion (AFE) ([De Santi et al., 2026](#bib.bib15))
as a search component in Triage-PiEvo . It proposes candidates in *three
roles*: best-region exploitation, frontier expansion, and
mechanism-discriminating probes.

Scoring candidates. For each role $`r`$, AFE scores a candidate
$`x\in\mathcal{X}`$ by four terms computed from the GP experts of
Section [3.1](#S3.SS1 "3.1 Evidence Admission under Source Ambiguity ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents"),
refit on the admitted primary history:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
A_{r}(x)\;=\;w_{r}^{\mu}\,\hat{\mu}(x)\;+\;w_{r}^{\sigma}\,\hat{\sigma}(x)\;+\;w_{r}^{n}\,N(x)\;+\;w_{r}^{d}\,D(x),
``` |  |

where $`\hat{\mu}(x)`$ and $`\hat{\sigma}(x)`$ are the GP experts’
predicted outcome and predictive uncertainty, $`N(x)`$ is the distance
of $`x`$ from already evaluated inputs, and $`D(x)`$ is a
descriptor-novelty measure. The role weights
$`\{w_{r}^{\mu},w_{r}^{\sigma},w_{r}^{n},w_{r}^{d}\}`$ are fixed a
priori and differ across roles: exploitation weights $`\hat{\mu}`$,
expansion weights $`N`$ and $`D`$, and probes weight $`\hat{\sigma}`$ in
regions where competing principles disagree
(Figure [3](#S3.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 3.3 Proposing Candidates without Seeing the Gate ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents")).

AFE reads admitted evidence; Triage  alone writes it. Two append-only
histories keep search evidence and revision evidence apart: the *primary
history* $`H_{t}^{\mathrm{prim}}`$ stores the records the search policy
may act on, and the *verification history* $`H_{t}^{\mathrm{ver}}`$
stores the C1–C3 checks (see
Section [3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 Escalating from Repeats to Held-Out Probes ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents")),
each linked to the primary record it concerns. “Outcome-blind” is then a
one-way boundary over these histories: AFE reads only
$`H_{t}^{\mathrm{prim}}`$ and candidate descriptors, outputs $`x_{t}`$,
and is forbidden from verification channels, quarantine flags, and gate
state; Triage  alone writes back, entering the admitted, substituted, or
quarantined outcome into $`H_{t+1}^{\mathrm{prim}}`$ for the next refit
of the GP experts—PiEvo ’s per-principle likelihood models
$`p(y\mid h,P)`$ ([Pu et al., 2026](#bib.bib1)), which we use
unmodified. Unable to observe gate state, the search policy can never
smuggle unverified evidence into persistent state; the only write path
to what the agent believes is the acceptance gate of
Section [3.4](#S3.SS4 "3.4 A Gated Principle Lifecycle with Reversible Revision ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents").

### 3.4 A Gated Principle Lifecycle with Reversible Revision

The final component determines how verified evidence changes persistent
state. Each principle $`P`$ is a record
$`(\mathrm{parent}(P),E(P),\mathrm{state}(P))`$—a pointer to the
principle it refines, its linked evidence set, and a lifecycle state
that advances through
$`\textsc{Proposed}\;\longrightarrow\;\textsc{Provisional}\;\longrightarrow\;\textsc{Accepted},`$
with any state sent to Retracted upon contradiction. We specify the two
forward transitions and the retraction rule in turn.

From proposal to acceptance: only admissible evidence counts. An
anomalous record passing C1–C2 (see
Section [3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 Escalating from Repeats to Held-Out Probes ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents"))
creates or updates a Proposed principle, and C2’s cross-channel
agreement promotes it to Provisional. Acceptance, however, is gated by
the following class of evidence:

###### Definition 3.3 (Admissible evidence for revision).

A record $`e`$ is *admissible* for principle $`P`$ if (i) it is produced
by an evaluator process sharing no memory, cache, or random seed with
the generator (*independence*), and (ii) it is measured on
$`X_{\mathrm{heldout}}`$ disjoint from the generator-visible
$`X_{\mathrm{train}}`$ (*held-out*). An admissible record *supports*
$`P`$ if its held-out gain
$`\Delta_{\mathrm{heldout}}(e;P)\geq\tau_{\mathrm{gen}}`$ and
*contradicts* $`P`$ if
$`\Delta_{\mathrm{heldout}}(e;P)\leq-\tau_{\mathrm{gen}}`$; records in
between are uninformative.

Writing $`n_{P}^{+}`$ and $`n_{P}^{-}`$ for the numbers of supporting
and contradicting admissible records in $`E(P)`$,
$`\textsc{Provisional}\;\longrightarrow\;\textsc{Accepted}\Longleftrightarrow n_{P}^{-}=0\ \ \wedge\ \ n_{P}^{+}\geq k.`$
The two conditions of
Definition [3.3](#S3.Thmtheorem3 "Definition 3.3 (Admissible evidence for revision). ‣ 3.4 A Gated Principle Lifecycle with Reversible Revision ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents")
each remove one failure mode: independence prevents corroboration from
being manufactured by the proposing channel, and the held-out
requirement makes acceptance test generalization rather than fit.

Retraction makes revision reversible. A single contradicting admissible
record ($`n_{P}^{-}\geq 1`$) moves any active principle—Proposed,
Provisional, or Accepted—to Retracted, and the retraction propagates
through $`\mathrm{parent}(\cdot)`$, so descendants of a contradicted
principle cannot remain trusted. Retraction is standing rather than
proposal-time only: every newly admitted record is scored against each
active principle it bears on, so evidence arriving after acceptance can
still overturn an Accepted principle
(Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ Appendix G Algorithmic Specification of the TRIAGE Decision Protocol ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents")).

In summary, the full loop of Triage-PiEvo , i.e., AFE proposal,
evaluation, staged verification (C1–C3), trust update, and lifecycle
transition, is summarized in
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ Appendix G Algorithmic Specification of the TRIAGE Decision Protocol ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents"),
with thresholds and sensitivity analyses in
Appendix [A](#A1 "Appendix A Evidence-Admission Hyperparameters and Sensitivity Analysis ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents").

## 4 Experiments

We evaluate Triage-PiEvo  along four axes: (1) matched downstream
performance across four scientific discovery tasks under three backbone
LLMs; (2) search efficiency in optimization speed, candidate coverage,
and computational cost; (3) controlled stress tests of the admission
gate under evaluator corruption, hidden anomaly sources, mechanism
reversal, proxy exploitation, and cross-harness deployment; and (4)
ablations isolating search expansion from evidence admission.

### 4.1 Experiment Setup

Tasks and Benchmarks. Following [Pu et al. (2026)](#bib.bib1), we
evaluate Triage-PiEvo  on four optimization tasks: Molecular
Bio-activity (MBO), Nanohelix (NHO), Superconductor (SPO), and
Transition Metal Complex (TMC). Each task is scored by its domain
evaluator and maximizes a task-specific objective under a shared budget
of $`24`$ evaluations.

Baselines. We evaluate three categories of scientific agents : (a)
generic and classic: Vanilla MAS and ReAct ([Yao et al.,
2022](#bib.bib5)), which represent tool-use loops without persistent
scientific state; (b) autonomous research: AI Scientist v1 ([Lu et al.,
2024](#bib.bib8)), AI Scientist v2 ([Yamada et al., 2025](#bib.bib3)),
and AI Researcher ([Tang et al., 2026](#bib.bib4)), which automate
multi-stage pipelines over transient artifacts; and (c)
principle-evolving: PiFlow ([Pu et al., 2025](#bib.bib2)) and
PiEvo  ([Pu et al., 2026](#bib.bib1)), the state of the art that revises
a principle set by anomalous—precisely the response our method
re-governs.

Implementation Details. We evaluate three backbones: Qwen3-235B ([Team,
2025](#bib.bib51)), Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite ([Google, 2025](#bib.bib52)),
GPT-5.6-Luna ([OpenAI, 2026](#bib.bib53)) without extended
chain-of-thought, over three fixed seeds, reported as mean $`\pm`$ std.
Gate parameters in Triage-PiEvo are fixed a priori across all tasks and
backbones. Full specification and sensitivity sweeps in
Appendix [A](#A1 "Appendix A Evidence-Admission Hyperparameters and Sensitivity Analysis ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents").

### 4.2 Evaluation Metrics

With $`y_{1:T}`$ the primary outcomes of a run, we employ metrics that
capture abilities to (a) reach high-quality solutions, (b) reach them
early, and (c) cover the candidate space broadly:

a. Solution Quality (SQ, %, $`\uparrow`$). The best outcome found,
normalized by a published per-task reference $`\mu_{\mathrm{ref}}`$ from
PiEvo  ([Pu et al., 2026](#bib.bib1)):
$`\mathrm{SQ}=100\cdot\nicefrac{{\max_{t}y_{t}}}{{\mu_{\mathrm{ref}}}}`$,
with $`\mu_{\mathrm{ref}}=6.5`$ (MBO), $`2.0`$ (NHO), $`298.5`$ (SPO),
and $`493.8`$ (TMC). These references are not attainable upper bounds,
so SQ passes $`100`$ when a run beats the reference; we apply no
clipping.

b. Area Under the Optimization Curve (AUOC, %, $`\uparrow`$). The
budget-averaged best-so-far quality,
$`\mathrm{AUOC}=100\cdot\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t}\nicefrac{{\max_{t^{\prime}\leq t}y_{t^{\prime}}}}{{\mu_{\mathrm{ref}}}}`$,
crediting how early quality is reached.

c. Average Pairwise Distance (APD, $`\uparrow`$). The mean pairwise
cosine distance between proposed candidates. A higher APD indicates
broader candidate coverage.

## 5 Conclusion

An anomalous residual is source-ambiguous: new mechanisms and evaluation
artifacts produce identical surprisal. Triage  separates the two uses of
such evidence: observations steer search immediately, while persistent
principles require independent held-out support and are retracted on
held-out contradiction. Instantiated in Triage-PiEvo , this design
attains the highest average solution quality across four tasks and three
backbones, admits none of the constructed corrupted or exploit evidence
while missing no planted mechanism, recovers from mechanism reversal,
and transfers unchanged to three external harnesses—all at $`+13.6\%`$
runtime.

## Appendix E Controlled Stress Tests and Detailed Per-Condition Protocols

This section details the formal experimental protocols, simulation
environments, and per-condition parameterizations for the controlled
stress evaluations summarized in
Section [4.4](#S4.SS4 "4.4 Stress Tests under Unreliable Feedback ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents").

### E.1 Protocol for Evaluator Corruptions and Artifact Injections (Table [7](#A5.T7 "Table 7 ‣ E.4 Protocol and Case Trace for Dynamic Mechanism Reversal ‣ Appendix E Controlled Stress Tests and Detailed Per-Condition Protocols ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents"), Panel A)

To benchmark the robustness of evidence admission against spurious
feedback, we systematically inject non-generalizing anomalies into the
observation channel across all four benchmark domains (MBO, NHO, SPO,
TMC). A total of $`96`$ controlled evaluation cells are designed across
three distinct failure regimes:

- •
  Transient Noise and Execution Dropout
  ($`z_{\mathrm{noise}},z_{\mathrm{exec}}`$, 24 cells): For each of the
  4 tasks, we inject sporadic stochastic failures under 3 configurations
  across 2 independent random seeds: (i) zero-mean impulse noise
  $`y_{t}\leftarrow y_{t}+\delta`$ with
  $`\delta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,5\sigma_{t})`$, (ii) heavy-tailed Cauchy
  perturbations, and (iii) simulated execution timeouts returning
  default fallback scalars. These anomalies represent transient
  execution artifacts; they are detected and filtered at Stage 1 (C1:
  same-channel repeat).
- •
  Channel-Specific Faults ($`z_{\mathrm{fault}}`$, 24 cells): In
  multi-channel setups, a single instrument channel may suffer
  systematic calibration drift. We simulate this across 4 tasks under 2
  fault regimes across 3 seeds: (i) an additive offset
  $`y_{t}\leftarrow y_{t}+3\sigma_{t}`$, and (ii) a multiplicative scale
  distortion $`y_{t}\leftarrow 1.5y_{t}`$. These anomalies reproduce
  consistently under C1, but fail C2 (cross-channel check
  $`|v_{t}^{c}-v_{t}^{c^{\prime}}|\leq\epsilon_{\mathrm{ind}}`$),
  preventing unwarranted principle proposals.
- •
  Hidden Surrogate and Out-of-Distribution Artifacts
  ($`z_{\mathrm{surr}},z_{\mathrm{ood}},z_{\mathrm{drift}}`$, 48 cells):
  Machine learning surrogate evaluators often exhibit pathological
  pseudo-optima in out-of-distribution regions. Across 4 tasks, we
  design 4 boundary failure modes across 3 seeds: (i) high-order
  polynomial extrapolation explosion, (ii) sparse kernel density
  estimation under-estimation artifacts, (iii) unregularized feature
  co-occurrence spikes, and (iv) gradual covariate shift. Because
  primary and cross-check evaluators share the surrogate training
  distribution, these artifacts pass C1 and C2, but fail generalization
  on the disjoint test set during Stage 2b (C3: held-out verification),
  triggering quarantine.

### E.2 Protocol for Planted Scientific Mechanisms (Table [7](#A5.T7 "Table 7 ‣ E.4 Protocol and Case Trace for Dynamic Mechanism Reversal ‣ Appendix E Controlled Stress Tests and Detailed Per-Condition Protocols ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents"), Panel B)

To confirm that the admission gate does not filter genuine discoveries,
we embed deterministic ground-truth physical mechanisms into the
evaluation landscape across $`144`$ independent trials:

- •
  Planted Discontinuous Step Shift (72 trials): We simulate abrupt phase
  transitions or critical concentration thresholds. For candidate $`x`$,
  the true latent target satisfies
  $`y^{*}(x)=f_{\mathrm{base}}(x)+\Delta\cdot\mathbb{I}[x_{d}>\theta]`$,
  where $`x_{d}`$ is a target design dimension, $`\theta`$ is a planted
  threshold, and $`\Delta=2.5\sigma_{0}`$ denotes the effect size. We
  evaluate across 4 tasks, 3 step thresholds $`\theta`$, and 6
  independent seeds ($`4\times 3\times 6=72`$ trials).
- •
  Planted Synergistic Interaction Peak (72 trials): We model non-linear
  molecular binding cooperativity or dual-dopant superconductivity
  enhancement via a localized interaction term
  $`y^{*}(x)=f_{\mathrm{base}}(x)+\gamma\exp(-\|x_{\mathcal{S}}-x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}}\|^{2}/2\ell^{2})`$,
  where $`\mathcal{S}`$ indexes a coupled feature pair. We evaluate
  across 4 tasks, 3 feature pairs, and 6 seeds ($`4\times 3\times 6=72`$
  trials).

A mechanism is scored as successfully recalled if an anomalous candidate
triggers a principle proposal that passes C1–C3 and reaches the Accepted
state within the 24-round budget. Ungated baselines suffer from
confirmation masking (where prior admitted artifacts misguide the GP
prior and preempt exploration), while Triage-PiEvo  achieves $`100.0\%`$
recall across all 144 trials.

### E.3 Protocol for Proxy Evaluator Exploitation (Table [8](#A5.T8 "Table 8 ‣ E.4 Protocol and Case Trace for Dynamic Mechanism Reversal ‣ Appendix E Controlled Stress Tests and Detailed Per-Condition Protocols ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents"))

In automated discovery, agents often optimize against fast proxy
evaluators $`\hat{f}_{\mathrm{proxy}}(x)`$ rather than high-fidelity
ground truth $`f_{\mathrm{true}}(x)`$. We construct a benchmark
environment where $`\hat{f}_{\mathrm{proxy}}`$ contains an exploitable
shortcut: maximizing a non-physical descriptor dimension
$`x_{\mathrm{spur}}`$ inflates $`\hat{f}_{\mathrm{proxy}}(x)>0.90`$,
whereas the ground-truth held-out evaluator $`f_{\mathrm{true}}(x)`$
heavily penalizes $`x_{\mathrm{spur}}`$ due to steric clash or
structural instability ($`f_{\mathrm{true}}(x)<0.40`$). Each agent is
allocated $`T=24`$ rounds. We measure: (i) the number of exploit
shortcut candidates admitted into the search history or persistent
principles, (ii) the apparent proxy score reported by the agent, and
(iii) the true generalization score on
$`f_{\mathrm{true}}(X_{\mathrm{heldout}})`$.

### E.4 Protocol and Case Trace for Dynamic Mechanism Reversal

To assess revision reversibility under non-stationary environments, we
introduce a controlled environmental polarity flip at round $`t=17`$.
Specifically, the optimal objective landscape is inverted
($`y\leftarrow y_{\max}-y`$), rendering all previously learned
principles detrimental. We track the chronological recovery trajectory:

- •
  Anomaly Detection ($`t=17`$): The inverted feedback induces a residual
  $`s_{t}>\tau_{\mathrm{anom}}`$, immediately opening diagnostic state.
- •
  Cascade Retraction ($`t=18`$): Held-out test on the candidate yields
  negative gain $`\Delta_{\mathrm{heldout}}\leq-\tau_{\mathrm{gen}}`$,
  moving the invalidated principle to Retracted and recursively
  retracting its descendants.
- •
  Optimum Recovery ($`t=23`$): With the search space unburdened from
  poisoned prior principles, the AFE probe flow explores the inverted
  landscape and identifies a candidate reaching $`\geq 95\%`$ of the new
  global optimum at round $`23`$, whereas ungated baselines remain
  trapped in the invalidated subspace.

## Appendix G Algorithmic Specification of the TRIAGE Decision Protocol

Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ Appendix G Algorithmic Specification of the TRIAGE Decision Protocol ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents")
formalizes the executable decision loop of the Triage 
evidence-admission gate.

0:  Candidate $`x_{t}`$, observed primary outcome $`y_{t}`$, primary
prediction $`\hat{y}_{t}`$, predictive scale $`\sigma_{t}>0`$, primary
channel $`c`$, active principles $`\mathcal{P}_{t}`$, threshold
$`\tau_{\mathrm{anom}}`$, agreement tolerances
$`\epsilon_{\mathrm{rep}},\epsilon_{\mathrm{ind}}`$, held-out margin
$`\tau_{\mathrm{gen}}`$, confirmation threshold $`k=2`$.

1:  Initialize $`\mathcal{P}_{t+1}\leftarrow\mathcal{P}_{t}`$.

2:  Compute anomaly residual:
$`s_{t}\leftarrow|y_{t}-\hat{y}_{t}|/\sigma_{t}`$.

3:  if $`s_{t}\leq\tau_{\mathrm{anom}}`$ then

4:   Admit $`(x_{t},y_{t})`$ to primary search history
$`\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{search}}`$; return $`\mathcal{P}_{t}`$.

5:  end if

6:  {Stage 1 — same-channel repeat. Controls admission to search only.}

7:  $`v_{t}^{c}\leftarrow\mathrm{Evaluate}(x_{t},\texttt{repeat},c)`$
{same channel $`c`$ as the primary observation}

8:  if $`|y_{t}-v_{t}^{c}|\leq\epsilon_{\mathrm{rep}}`$ then

9:   $`\alpha_{c}\leftarrow\alpha_{c}+1`$; admit $`(x_{t},y_{t})`$ to
$`\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{search}}`$ as search-active.

10:  else

11:   $`\beta_{c}\leftarrow\beta_{c}+1`$; substitute the verified median
$`y_{t}\leftarrow v_{t}^{c}`$ and admit to
$`\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{search}}`$.

12:   return $`\mathcal{P}_{t}`$ {unreconciled observation: no revision
path}

13:  end if

14:  {A same-channel repeat never authorizes revision, however often it
agrees:}

15:  {a reproducible artifact agrees with itself. Revision requires
Stage 2.}

16:  if a principle revision proposal $`P`$ is generated from
$`(x_{t},y_{t})`$ then

17:   {Stage 2a — cross-channel check against a distinct channel}

18:   Select verifier channel
$`c^{\prime}\leftarrow\arg\max_{c^{\prime\prime}\neq c}p_{c^{\prime\prime}}^{\mathrm{trust}}`$
among declared independent channels.

19:
  $`v_{t}^{c^{\prime}}\leftarrow\mathrm{Evaluate}(x_{t},\texttt{cross-channel},c^{\prime})`$

20:   if $`|v_{t}^{c}-v_{t}^{c^{\prime}}|>\epsilon_{\mathrm{ind}}`$ then

21:    $`\beta_{c}\leftarrow\beta_{c}+1`$;
$`\beta_{c^{\prime}}\leftarrow\beta_{c^{\prime}}+1`$; return
$`\mathcal{P}_{t}`$ {channels disagree: source unresolved}

22:   end if

23:   $`\alpha_{c}\leftarrow\alpha_{c}+1`$;
$`\alpha_{c^{\prime}}\leftarrow\alpha_{c^{\prime}}+1`$; set state of
$`P`$ to Provisional.

24:   {Stage 2b — held-out generalization, the only admitting test}

25:   Draw $`X_{\mathrm{heldout}}\sim\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{heldout}}`$
with
$`X_{\mathrm{heldout}}\cap\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{search}}=\emptyset`$,
fixed before the run.

26:
  $`E(P)\leftarrow\{e=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}):x^{\prime}\in X_{\mathrm{heldout}},\ e.\mathrm{ind}=\textsc{true}\}`$,
with each stance set by the $`\tau_{\mathrm{gen}}`$ margin of
§[3](#S3 "3 Methodology ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents").

27:   if $`\exists\,e\in E(P)`$ with $`\mathrm{contradict}(e,P)=1`$ then

28:    Set state of $`P`$ to Retracted; recursively retract every
principle listing $`P`$ as parent.

29:   else if
$`|\{e\in E(P):e.\mathrm{ind}\wedge e.\mathrm{heldout}\wedge\mathrm{support}(e,P)\}|\geq k`$
then

30:    Set state of $`P`$ to Accepted;
$`\mathcal{P}_{t+1}\leftarrow\mathcal{P}_{t}\cup\{P\}`$.

31:   else

32:    Leave $`P`$ Provisional {support below $`k`$: search continues,
state unchanged}

33:   end if

34:  end if

35:  {Standing re-verification: every new admissible record is also
scored against each active}

36:  {principle it bears on, so an Accepted principle is retracted by
later contradiction,}

37:  {not only at proposal time (cf. the mechanism-reversal trace,
Appendix [E.4](#A5.SS4 "E.4 Protocol and Case Trace for Dynamic Mechanism Reversal ‣ Appendix E Controlled Stress Tests and Detailed Per-Condition Protocols ‣ Triage-PiEvo: Evidence Admission for Principle-Evolving Scientific Agents")).}

38:  for all $`P^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{t}`$ with
$`\mathrm{state}(P^{\prime})\neq\textsc{Retracted}`$ do

39:   if a record $`e`$ admitted this round is linked to $`P^{\prime}`$
and $`\mathrm{contradict}(e,P^{\prime})=1`$ then

40:    Set state of $`P^{\prime}`$ to Retracted; recursively retract
every principle listing $`P^{\prime}`$ as parent.

41:   end if

42:  end for

43:  return $`\mathcal{P}_{t+1}`$.

## Appendix H Extended Benchmark Protocols and Failure Metric Definitions

#### AutoResearchEval Benchmark.

AutoResearchEval ([Fei et al., 2026](#bib.bib42)) evaluates multi-step
targeted optimization across molecular properties, material bandgaps,
and algorithmic code generation. Each harness is run across 5
independent seeds with an identical per-task monetary cap (\$2.00) and
time budget.

#### Failure Metric Definitions.

- •
  Official Score: Raw target optimization metric reported by the host
  framework upon run termination.
- •
  Target Attainment: Binary indicator indicating whether the best
  discovered candidate achieves $`\geq 90\%`$ of the task-specific
  global target optimum.
- •
  Verified Score: Score evaluated independently by an external,
  sandboxed ground-truth testbed.
- •
  Valid Runs (%): Percentage of runs that complete all execution rounds
  without unhandled runtime crashes, tool timeouts, or syntax failure.
- •
  Critical Failure (%): Percentage of runs in which unverified,
  hallucinated tool outputs or corrupted feedback poisoned the agent’s
  internal memory, leading to permanent loop collapse or degenerate
  repeating actions.
````
