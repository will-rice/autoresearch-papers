# Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement via Hierarchical Coordination for Multi-Agent Scientific Discovery

###### Abstract

Multi-agent scientific discovery with large language models raises a
coupled learning problem: research assignments determine both which
experiments are pursued and the experiences from which agents adapt. We
introduce Meld, a unified hierarchical test-time training framework that
jointly adapts a team-level Coordinator policy and agent-level
individual Scientist policies within a single discovery process. The
Coordinator generates a joint portfolio of search branches and research
directives over a shared derivation tree, while Scientists develop
hypotheses and execution plans implemented by a shared frozen Executor.
Both levels receive alternating GRPO-style parameter updates under a
shared smooth best-of-budget objective, allowing team organization and
individual research behavior to co-evolve in a recursive
self-improvement loop. Our analysis establishes objective alignment for
the hierarchy, characterizes conditions for coordination gains over
independent parallel discovery and the first-order benefit of private
adaptation, and derives a finite-budget regret bound for adapted models.
On 22 MLGym and MLE-bench tasks, Meld is best on 20 of them and achieves
the highest aggregate normalized scores on MLGym, using a 4B backbone
for coordination and research. Most baselines use proprietary
Scientists, and all systems share one frozen Executor.

## 1 Introduction

Scientific discovery requires judiciously allocating a limited
exploration/evaluation budget across competing hypotheses. Large
language models (LLMs) increasingly support this process by proposing,
testing, and refining candidates ([Romera-Paredes et al.,
2024](#bib.bib1); [Novikov et al., 2025](#bib.bib2)), while test-time
training (TTT) uses discovery feedback to adapt their research
policies ([Wang et al., 2026b](#bib.bib3); [Yuksekgonul et al.,
2026](#bib.bib4); [Juneja et al., 2026](#bib.bib6)). For a team of
collaborative agents, each local research assignment determines both the
team’s overall progress and the learning experience that shapes the
team’s subsequent research strategy. Agent coordination in scientific
discovery, such as focusing collective effort on promising research
lineages while maintaining sufficient exploration, becomes part of the
learning process rather than merely a scheduling mechanism.

Existing approaches provide complementary capabilities. TTT-Discover and
ARTS adapt task-local proposal or research policies ([Yuksekgonul et
al., 2026](#bib.bib4); [Juneja et al., 2026](#bib.bib6)), while CORAL
coordinates parallel discovery through shared persistent memory ([Qu et
al., 2026](#bib.bib8)). Agent Q-Mix learns communication and
coordination across a training distribution ([Jiang et al.,
2026](#bib.bib14); [Rashid et al., 2018](#bib.bib24)), without jointly
adapting its coordination strategy and underlying LLM researchers to
each new discovery task. These advances leave a central joint learning
problem unresolved: _how can a team-level coordination policy and
individual agent-level research policies be learned together within a
single discovery process?_ We address this problem through a unified
framework that jointly adapts both levels via task-local parameter
updates, allowing team organization and individual research behavior to
co-evolve.

We introduce Meld, a unified hierarchical TTT framework for multi-agent
scientific discovery. A team-level Coordinator generates a joint
portfolio of parent branches and research directives over a shared
derivation tree. Initially identical Scientists, each with a private
adapter, develop their assignments into hypotheses and execution plans
implemented by a shared frozen Executor. The Coordinator and Scientists
learn through alternating GRPO-style parameter updates ([Shao et al.,
2024](#bib.bib18)) under a shared smooth best-of-budget objective. Thus,
Meld creates a recursive self-improvement (RSI) loop: Joint portfolio
coordination organizes research across agents and shapes the team’s
learning experience, while private agents’ evolving capabilities and
outcomes guide subsequent coordination.

Our theoretical analysis establishes objective alignment for the
proposed hierarchy, characterizes conditions for coordination gains over
independent parallel discovery at matched individual outcome
distributions and budgets, and quantifies the first-order benefit of
private adaptation at a common model. We further derive a finite-budget
regret bound for adapted models and a conditional optimization rate.
Across 12 MLGym ([Nathani et al., 2025](#bib.bib13)) and 10
MLE-bench ([Chan et al., 2025](#bib.bib40)) tasks, Meld attains the best
three-seed mean score among the compared methods on 20 tasks
(Table [1](#S6.T1 "Table 1 ‣ 6.2 Main Results ‣ 6 Experiments ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"))
and the highest aggregate normalized scores on MLGym, with an
interquartile mean of $`0.748`$ versus $`0.684`$ for ARTS^(∗). These
system-level comparisons use a 4B backbone for Meld’s coordination and
research, proprietary Scientist models for most baselines, and the same
frozen Executor throughout.

Our contributions are threefold:

- •
  A unified framework for task-local RSI. We jointly learn a team-level
  portfolio coordination policy and agent-level Scientist research
  policies within a shared-objective hierarchy in a single discovery
  task.
- •
  Theory of coordination and private adaptation. We quantify the
  benefits of Meld and relate surrogate optimization to finite-budget
  discovery regret at adapted policy.
- •
  Strong empirical performance. We report the best score on 20 of 22
  MLGym and MLE-bench tasks and leading aggregate performance on MLGym
  with a compact research backbone, complemented by search trajectories
  and adaptation ablations.

## 2 Related Work

##### Test-Time Training for Discovery.

TTT updates model parameters using a signal from the test instance or
task ([Sun et al., 2020](#bib.bib19)), whereas test-time scaling
allocates additional inference computation without updating them ([Snell
et al., 2025](#bib.bib20)). Early discovery systems such as
FunSearch ([Romera-Paredes et al., 2024](#bib.bib1)) and
AlphaEvolve ([Novikov et al., 2025](#bib.bib2)) iteratively generate and
select candidates with a fixed proposal model, while recent methods
center adaptation on a single task-local proposal policy ([Wang et al.,
2026b](#bib.bib3); [Yuksekgonul et al., 2026](#bib.bib4)), strategic
advisor ([Yan et al., 2026](#bib.bib5)), uncertainty-guided exploration
component ([Riaz et al., 2026](#bib.bib7)), or tree-search
Scientist ([Juneja et al., 2026](#bib.bib6)). Meld instead organizes the
experiences of multiple concurrently adapting research policies.

##### Multi-Agent Systems and Learned Orchestration.

In discovery, CORAL ([Qu et al., 2026](#bib.bib8)) and
SwarmResearch ([Virk et al., 2026](#bib.bib9)) coordinate parallel
agents through shared memory or centralized orchestration.
Parameter-trained systems ([Zhang et al., 2026](#bib.bib10); [Wang et
al., 2026a](#bib.bib11)) typically learn across a training distribution,
while TacoMAS ([Xu et al., 2026](#bib.bib12)) co-evolves role-specific
capabilities and topology at inference. Related MARL work learns
emergent role representations during training ([Wang et al.,
2020](#bib.bib32)), whereas Meld induces persistent task-local
specialization through assignment-conditioned private adapter updates.
GPTSwarm ([Zhuge et al., 2024](#bib.bib21)), ADAS ([Hu et al.,
2025](#bib.bib22)), and the RL-trained Conductor ([Nielsen et al.,
2026](#bib.bib23)) establish that orchestration can be learned. Meld
differs by alternating task-local weight updates to the team-level
coordination policy and multiple agent-level Scientist policies within
the same unseen discovery run, under one shared best-of-budget
objective, so that team organization and individual research behavior
can co-evolve.

## 3 Preliminaries

##### Executable Discovery Task.

Following [Juneja et al. (2026)](#bib.bib6), we study test-time
discovery on a single previously unseen executable task
$`\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{W}_{0},m,s_{0},T)`$, where $`\mathcal{W}_{0}`$
contains the task specification, data, baseline artifact $`c_{0}`$, and
evaluation harness; $`m`$ is the task-specific metric;
$`s_{0}=s(c_{0})`$ is the normalized baseline utility; and $`T`$ is a
system-level budget shared by all agents. Given the raw evaluator metric
$`q_{m}(c)`$, we define the normalized utility
$`s(c)=g_{m}\!\left(q_{m}(c)\right)\in[0,1]`$, where $`g_{m}`$ is fixed
for each task and monotone in the preferred direction of $`m`$, so that
larger $`s(c)`$ always indicates a better artifact. In our
implementation, $`g_{m}(q)=\sigma(z_{m}(q))`$, where $`z_{m}`$ performs
metric-specific standardization and direction correction, and $`\sigma`$
is the logistic sigmoid.

Let $`\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{val}}_{T}(\Pi)`$ denote the successfully
evaluated candidates produced within budget by an adaptive multi-agent
policy $`\Pi`$. We optimize expected best-of-budget utility,
corresponding to a simple-regret objective in fixed-budget pure
exploration ([Bubeck et al., 2009](#bib.bib29)),

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
J_{T}(\Pi;\mathcal{P})=\mathbb{E}_{\Pi}\!\left[\max_{c\in\{c_{0}\}\cup\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{val}}_{T}(\Pi)}s(c)\right],\qquad\mathfrak{R}_{T}=s^{\star}-J_{T}(\Pi;\mathcal{P}),
``` |  | (1) |

where $`s^{\star}`$ is the optimal feasible score. All parameter updates
use only interaction data from this task.

##### Shared Discovery Process.

At round $`t`$, the shared history is represented by a rooted derivation
tree $`\mathcal{G}_{t}=(V_{t},E_{t})`$, whose root stores
$`(c_{0},s_{0})`$. Together with the remaining budget $`b_{t}`$, it
defines the discovery state
$`\mathcal{S}_{t}=(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{G}_{t},b_{t})`$. Conditioned on
$`\mathcal{S}_{t}`$, policy $`\Pi`$ proposes $`n_{t}\leq N`$ concurrent
expansions, where $`N`$ is the maximum number of active research units,
with expansion $`i`$ given by

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\xi_{t}^{i}=(p_{t}^{i},h_{t}^{i},e_{t}^{i}),\qquad c_{t}^{i}=\operatorname{Exec}(c_{p_{t}^{i}};h_{t}^{i},e_{t}^{i}),\qquad(\ell_{t}^{i},s_{t}^{i})=\operatorname{Eval}_{m}(c_{t}^{i};\mathcal{W}_{0}),
``` |  | (2) |

where $`p_{t}^{i}\in V_{t}`$ selects a parent node, $`h_{t}^{i}`$ is a
testable hypothesis, and $`e_{t}^{i}`$ is an execution plan. The
evaluator returns feedback $`\ell_{t}^{i}`$ and utility $`s_{t}^{i}`$,
with $`s_{t}^{i}=\bot`$ on failure. The Executor remains frozen,
confining parameter adaptation to the policies that select and develop
research directions.

Each successful evaluation adds
$`v_{t}^{i}=(h_{t}^{i},e_{t}^{i},c_{t}^{i},\ell_{t}^{i},s_{t}^{i})`$ to
the tree. Defining $`V_{t}^{+}=\{v_{t}^{i}:s_{t}^{i}\neq\bot\}`$, we
update $`V_{t+1}=V_{t}\cup V_{t}^{+}`$ and
$`E_{t+1}=E_{t}\cup\{(p_{t}^{i},v_{t}^{i}):v_{t}^{i}\in V_{t}^{+}\}`$.
Failed attempts consume budget and provide learning signals but are not
added as expandable nodes.
Section [4](#S4 "4 Meld: Hierarchical Test-Time Training for Self-Improving Multi-Agent Discovery ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")
instantiates $`\Pi`$ hierarchically: the team-level Coordinator assigns
$`z_{t}^{i}=(p_{t}^{i},d_{t}^{i})`$, and each agent-level Scientist
$`i`$ produces $`u_{t}^{i}=(h_{t}^{i},e_{t}^{i})`$; their alternating
adaptation forms Meld’s multi-agent RSI loop.

## 4 Meld: Hierarchical Test-Time Training for Self-Improving Multi-Agent Discovery

Meld couples joint research assignment with private policy adaptation. A
learnable *Coordinator* assigns research directives to $`N`$ agents
(*research units*), each pairing a private Scientist with the shared
frozen Executor. The two policy levels, the team-level Coordinator and
the agent-level Scientists, receive alternating task-local updates under
a shared smooth best-of-budget objective. Assignments thus shape both
current exploration and the experience available for private adaptation.

### 4.1 Hierarchical Policy

At round $`t`$, the coordination policy $`\pi^{\mathrm{coord}}_{\phi}`$
observes the shared discovery state $`\mathcal{S}_{t}`$ and generates a
*portfolio* $`Z_{t}=\{z_{t}^{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{t}}`$ for active
units $`\mathcal{I}_{t}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}`$. Each assignment
$`z_{t}^{i}=(p_{t}^{i},d_{t}^{i})`$ specifies a parent node
$`p_{t}^{i}`$ from a retrieved tree shortlist and a research directive
$`d_{t}^{i}`$: a schema-constrained natural-language statement of the
research question, the parent’s bottleneck, and the constraints to
respect. The portfolio is emitted as one structured completion, allowing
assignments to depend on one another before candidates are generated.

Given $`(\mathcal{S}_{t},z_{t}^{i})`$, Scientist $`i`$ samples a
hypothesis and execution plan
$`u_{t}^{i}=(h_{t}^{i},e_{t}^{i})\sim\pi^{\mathrm{sci}}_{\theta_{i}}(\cdot\mid\mathcal{S}_{t},z_{t}^{i})`$.
The frozen Executor implements the plan as
$`c_{t}^{i}=\operatorname{Exec}(c_{p_{t}^{i}};h_{t}^{i},e_{t}^{i})`$,
which is evaluated as in
Eq. ([2](#S3.E2 "Equation 2 ‣ Shared Discovery Process. ‣ 3 Preliminaries ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")).
Scientists sample independently conditional on the state and portfolio.
With $`U_{t}=\{u_{t}^{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{t}}`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\pi_{\Theta}(Z_{t},U_{t}\mid\mathcal{S}_{t})=\pi^{\mathrm{coord}}_{\phi}(Z_{t}\mid\mathcal{S}_{t})\prod_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{t}}\pi^{\mathrm{sci}}_{\theta_{i}}(u_{t}^{i}\mid\mathcal{S}_{t},z_{t}^{i}),\qquad\Theta=(\phi,\theta_{1:N}).
``` |  | (3) |

The Coordinator decides *where and what* to research; each Scientist
decides *how*. Coordination resides in the joint assignment policy, even
though Scientists sample independently once assigned.

The Coordinator and Scientists share one frozen backbone, with a LoRA
adapter ([Hu et al., 2022](#bib.bib25)) $`\phi`$ for coordination and a
private $`\theta_{i}`$ for each Scientist. All Scientists start
identically, $`\theta_{1,0}=\cdots=\theta_{N,0}`$, and every adapter
resets at task start; neither the backbone nor the Executor is updated.
The tree shares artifacts and feedback, while private adapters retain
assignment-conditioned updates without forcing identical policy changes.

### 4.2 A Shared Best-of-Budget Objective

Equation ([1](#S3.E1 "Equation 1 ‣ Executable Discovery Task. ‣ 3 Preliminaries ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"))
values the best validated artifact within budget. Parent-relative gains
can reward branch improvements that leave the best-so-far utility
unchanged, while scoring candidates separately ignores their
combination. We therefore evaluate each round against the shared
discovery state, using a smooth potential whose terminal value
approximates best-of-budget utility.

##### Smooth potential.

Let $`K`$ be the evaluator-call budget allowed by $`T`$, and $`V`$ the
derivation-tree node set in state $`\mathcal{S}`$, including the root.
Define

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\Phi_{\tau,K}(\mathcal{S})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\tau\log\!\Big(\tfrac{1}{K+1}\Big[\textstyle\sum_{v\in V}e^{s_{v}/\tau}+(K+1-|V|)\,e^{s_{\min}/\tau}\Big]\Big),`$ |  | (4) |
|  | $`\displaystyle J_{\tau,K}(\Theta)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\Pi_{\Theta}}\big[\Phi_{\tau,K}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{end}})\big],`$ |  |  |

where $`\tau>0`$ is a temperature, $`\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{end}}`$ is the
state at budget exhaustion, and failed or unused slots carry
$`s_{\min}=0`$. Padding fixes the normalization as the tree grows: each
validated candidate replaces a sentinel slot rather than changing the
denominator. This log-sum-exp lies below the best utility by at most
$`\tau\log(K+1)`$
(Proposition [5.2](#S5.Thmtheorem2 "Proposition 5.2 (One objective across the hierarchy). ‣ Notation. ‣ 5.1 One Objective Across the Hierarchy ‣ 5 Theoretical Analysis ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")(i)),
making $`J_{\tau,K}`$ a shared surrogate of $`J_{T}`$ for both policy
levels.

##### Potential increments as shared credit.

A round receives
$`r_{t}^{\Phi}=\Phi_{\tau,K}(\mathcal{S}_{t+1})-\Phi_{\tau,K}(\mathcal{S}_{t})`$
for its collective outcome. The temperature sets how closely the
potential tracks the maximum while retaining credit for near-frontier
candidates. Joint assignments are thus evaluated through realized
candidate utilities, not an explicit diversity bonus. The increments
telescope,
$`\sum_{t}r_{t}^{\Phi}=\Phi_{\tau,K}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{end}})-\Phi_{\tau,K}(\mathcal{S}_{0})`$;
the exact score-function gradient ([Williams, 1992](#bib.bib28)) credits
each decision with the full downstream sum
(Proposition [5.2](#S5.Thmtheorem2 "Proposition 5.2 (One objective across the hierarchy). ‣ Notation. ‣ 5.1 One Objective Across the Hierarchy ‣ 5 Theoretical Analysis ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")).
Direct Monte Carlo estimation requires continuations to budget
exhaustion, so Meld limits adaptation to a *one-round horizon*.

At snapshot $`\mathcal{S}`$, a policy block
$`\beta\in\{\mathrm{coord},\mathrm{sci}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{sci}_{N}\}`$
takes action $`a`$ in context $`x`$: $`a=Z`$, $`x=\mathcal{S}`$ for the
Coordinator, and $`a=u^{i}`$, $`x=(\mathcal{S},z^{i})`$ for Scientist
$`i`$. Its local objective is

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle J^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\beta}(\mathcal{S})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{\beta}(\cdot\mid x)}\big[\Phi_{\tau,K}(\mathcal{S}\oplus X(a))-\Phi_{\tau,K}(\mathcal{S})\big],`$ |  | (5) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\nabla_{\beta}J^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\beta}(\mathcal{S})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big[r^{\Phi}(a)\,\nabla_{\beta}\log\pi_{\beta}(a\mid x)\big].`$ |  |  |

Here $`X(a)`$ contains the round’s validated candidates,
$`\mathcal{S}\oplus X(a)`$ extends the snapshot with their nodes, and
$`r^{\Phi}(a)`$ is the realized potential increment; the gradient
identity is established in
Proposition [5.2](#S5.Thmtheorem2 "Proposition 5.2 (One objective across the hierarchy). ‣ Notation. ‣ 5.1 One Objective Across the Hierarchy ‣ 5 Theoretical Analysis ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery").
The expectation includes execution and evaluation randomness and the
other policies’ responses at the fixed behavior model. For a Scientist
update, the full portfolio is also fixed. This supplies round-local
credit rather than an unbiased full-return gradient: the Coordinator is
credited through its portfolio, each Scientist through its response, and
both against the same team outcome.

### 4.3 Alternating Test-Time Adaptation

##### Matched-snapshot groups.

Because the tree and policies evolve, relative feedback is compared at a
fixed context. We combine GRPO-style sampling ([Shao et al.,
2024](#bib.bib18)) with a leave-one-out baseline ([Ahmadian et al.,
2024](#bib.bib26)). Each group contains $`G\geq 2`$ independent *forks*
of one snapshot, with the discovery state, remaining budget, parent
shortlist, and behavior model $`\Theta_{\mathrm{old}}`$ fixed. The $`N`$
research units provide parallel exploration within a fork, while the
$`G`$ forks provide matched-context samples for adaptation. In a
*Coordinator group*, each fork samples a complete portfolio
$`Z^{(g)}\sim\pi^{\mathrm{coord}}_{\phi_{\mathrm{old}}}(\cdot\mid\mathcal{S})`$
and executes its units using the current Scientist policies. In a
*Scientist group*, one portfolio $`Z`$ is fixed across forks, each
Scientist independently samples
$`u_{i}^{(g)}\sim\pi^{\mathrm{sci}}_{\theta_{i,\mathrm{old}}}(\cdot\mid\mathcal{S},z^{i})`$,
and their responses are executed together. Fork $`g`$ yields validated
candidates $`X^{(g)}`$, reward
$`r_{g}^{\Phi}=\Phi_{\tau,K}(\mathcal{S}\oplus X^{(g)})-\Phi_{\tau,K}(\mathcal{S})`$,
and advantage
$`\widehat{A}_{g}=r^{\Phi}_{g}-\frac{1}{G-1}\sum_{g^{\prime}\neq g}r^{\Phi}_{g^{\prime}}`$,
whose baseline excludes fork $`g`$ and, by conditional independence,
preserves the sequence-score estimator’s expectation in
Eq. ([5](#S4.E5 "Equation 5 ‣ Potential increments as shared credit. ‣ 4.2 A Shared Best-of-Budget Objective ‣ 4 Meld: Hierarchical Test-Time Training for Self-Improving Multi-Agent Discovery ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")).
Scientists share this team advantage but update only through their own
response tokens, without pooling samples across snapshots or private
policies.

##### Token-level update.

For the policy being updated, let $`y_{g}=(y_{g,1},\ldots,y_{g,L_{g}})`$
be its structured response in fork $`g`$ and
$`\rho_{g,j}=\pi_{\beta}(y_{g,j}\mid x,y_{g,<j})/\pi_{\beta,\mathrm{old}}(y_{g,j}\mid x,y_{g,<j})`$
the token importance ratio. Broadcasting $`\widehat{A}_{g}`$ to its
response tokens gives the token-averaged PPO-style surrogate ([Schulman
et al., 2017](#bib.bib27)),

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{\beta}=-\frac{1}{G}\sum_{g=1}^{G}\frac{1}{L_{g}}\sum_{j=1}^{L_{g}}\min\!\big(\rho_{g,j}\widehat{A}_{g},\ \operatorname{clip}(\rho_{g,j},1-\epsilon_{\mathrm{lo}},1+\epsilon_{\mathrm{hi}})\,\widehat{A}_{g}\big)+\beta_{\mathrm{KL}}\,\widehat{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(\pi_{\beta}\,\|\,\pi_{\beta}^{\mathrm{ref}}\big),
``` |  | (6) |

where $`\pi_{\beta}^{\mathrm{ref}}`$ is the frozen task-start policy,
distinct from the per-group behavior policy
$`\pi_{\beta,\mathrm{old}}`$, and $`\widehat{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}`$ is
token-averaged on the same responses. Token-length normalization,
clipping, and KL regularization define the implemented surrogate rather
than the exact sequence-score gradient of
Eq. ([5](#S4.E5 "Equation 5 ‣ Potential increments as shared credit. ‣ 4.2 A Shared Best-of-Budget Objective ‣ 4 Meld: Hierarchical Test-Time Training for Self-Improving Multi-Agent Discovery ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"))
(Appendix [A.3](#A1.SS3 "A.3 Proof of Proposition ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")).

##### Update and commit.

Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ Update and commit. ‣ 4.3 Alternating Test-Time Adaptation ‣ 4 Meld: Hierarchical Test-Time Training for Self-Improving Multi-Agent Discovery ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")
summarizes a complete group with sufficient remaining calls. Coordinator
and Scientist groups alternate using fresh samples, with no replay,
until the budget no longer admits a full group, after which the current
policies are rolled out without further updates
(Appendix [B.3](#A2.SS3 "B.3 Models, Harness, and Candidate Execution ‣ Appendix B Experimental Details ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")).

the merged potential increment. Because updated policies collect the
next group, Coordinator updates change subsequent learning assignments
while Scientist updates change the outcomes informing subsequent

$`\Theta_{\mathrm{old}}`$ during collection.

2: Sample independent portfolios (Coordinator group) or one shared

them, and compute $`r_{g}^{\Phi}`$.

5: Take one step of

on $`\phi`$ (Coordinator) or on all private $`\theta_{i}`$ in parallel

## 5 Theoretical Analysis

This section shows that both levels of Meld follow partial gradients of

and bounds the discovery regret after adaptation
(Section [5.3](#S5.SS3 "5.3 From Co-Adaptation to Discovery Regret ‣ 5 Theoretical Analysis ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")),

### 5.1 One Objective Across the Hierarchy

Although the team-level Coordinator and the agent-level Scientists act
on different objects,

### 5.2 Coordination and Private Adaptation versus Parallel Discovery

Parallel discovery runs $`N`$ units, each choosing its assignment
independently given the shared state, with frozen agents ([Qu et al.,
2026](#bib.bib8)) or a single adapted policy ([Yuksekgonul et al.,
2026](#bib.bib4)), whereas Meld draws the assignments jointly and adapts
each Scientist privately. We compare the two designs at equal individual
quality and equal budget, so that any difference is attributable to the
design rather than to stronger units or additional evaluations. Fix a
snapshot $`\mathcal{S}`$ with at least $`N`$ evaluator calls remaining
and fix the Scientist models. A *portfolio law*
$`\pi(Z\mid\mathcal{S})`$ draws a full portfolio
$`Z=(z^{1},\ldots,z^{N})`$, after which unit $`i`$ produces and
evaluates its candidate independently of the other units, using only
$`(\mathcal{S},z^{i})`$, and returns a utility $`Y_{i}\in[0,1]`$, with
$`0`$ for a failed call. Let $`q_{i}`$ be the $`i`$th marginal of
$`\pi`$ and $`\pi^{\mathrm{par}}=\prod_{i}q_{i}`$ its *independent
parallel* counterpart. The two laws induce the same unit marginals
$`H=(H_{1},\ldots,H_{N})`$,
$`H_{i}(y)=\Pr(Y_{i}\leq y\mid\mathcal{S})`$, and differ only in the
dependence among assignments, which is all that coordination controls.
Write $`B_{\mathrm{new}}=\max\{B(\mathcal{S}),Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{N}\}`$,
$`H_{\pi}(y)=\Pr_{\pi}(\max_{i}Y_{i}\leq y\mid\mathcal{S})`$, and
$`\Delta\mathcal{A}=\sum_{i}(e^{Y_{i}/\tau}-1)`$ for the soft-mass added
to $`\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})=\sum_{v\in V}e^{s_{v}/\tau}+(K+1-|V|)`$,
the bracket in
Eq. ([4](#S4.E4 "Equation 4 ‣ Smooth potential. ‣ 4.2 A Shared Best-of-Budget Objective ‣ 4 Meld: Hierarchical Test-Time Training for Self-Improving Multi-Agent Discovery ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")),
so that $`r^{\Phi}=\psi(\Delta\mathcal{A})`$ for the strictly concave
and increasing function
$`\psi(w)=\tau\log(1+w/\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}))`$. Let

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathrm{MN}(H)=\min_{c\in[B(\mathcal{S}),1]}\Big\{c+\textstyle\sum_{i}\int_{c}^{1}\big(1-H_{i}(y)\big)\,dy\Big\}
``` |  | (8) |

be the Meilijson–Nádas bound on $`\mathbb{E}[B_{\mathrm{new}}]`$ given
$`H`$ ([Meilijson and Nádas, 1979](#bib.bib36)), with
$`c\geq B(\mathcal{S})`$ because $`B_{\mathrm{new}}`$ includes the
incumbent.

###### Theorem 5.3 (Value of coordination at matched marginals).

In the setting above, for every portfolio law $`\pi`$: (i)
$`\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[B_{\mathrm{new}}]-\mathbb{E}_{\pi^{\mathrm{par}}}[B_{\mathrm{new}}]=\int_{B(\mathcal{S})}^{1}\big(\prod_{i}H_{i}(y)-H_{\pi}(y)\big)\,dy\leq\mathrm{MN}(H)-\mathbb{E}_{\pi^{\mathrm{par}}}[B_{\mathrm{new}}]`$;
(ii)
$`\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[r^{\Phi}]\leq\psi(\mathbb{E}[\Delta\mathcal{A}])`$,
where $`\mathbb{E}[\Delta\mathcal{A}]`$ depends on $`H`$ only, with
equality if and only if $`\Delta\mathcal{A}`$ is $`\pi`$-a.s. constant;
under $`\pi^{\mathrm{par}}`$ the inequality is strict unless every
$`Y_{i}`$ is degenerate; (iii) if $`(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{N})`$ is negatively
associated under $`\pi`$ ([Joag-Dev and Proschan, 1983](#bib.bib38)),
then
$`\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[r^{\Phi}]\geq\mathbb{E}_{\pi^{\mathrm{par}}}[r^{\Phi}]`$.

Theorem [5.3](#S5.Thmtheorem3 "Theorem 5.3 (Value of coordination at matched marginals). ‣ 5.2 Coordination and Private Adaptation versus Parallel Discovery ‣ 5 Theoretical Analysis ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")
shows that an independent law can change
$`\mathbb{E}[B_{\mathrm{new}}]`$ and $`\mathbb{E}[r^{\Phi}]`$ only
through the marginals $`H`$, whereas a joint law with the same marginals
adds the dependence term in (i), up to the coupling bound
$`\mathrm{MN}(H)`$. By strict concavity of $`\psi`$, the training reward
at fixed marginals is largest when $`\Delta\mathcal{A}`$ is
deterministic, which no independent law with non-degenerate units
achieves (ii), and it is at least the parallel reward under negative
association (iii). When one of $`N`$ directions has utility
$`s_{\mathrm{hi}}>B(\mathcal{S})`$ and the others
$`s_{\mathrm{lo}}\leq B(\mathcal{S})`$, assigning them by a uniformly
random permutation attains the bounds in (i) and (ii) and exceeds
independent assignment in expected best-of-round utility by
$`(s_{\mathrm{hi}}-B(\mathcal{S}))(1-1/N)^{N}`$
(Appendix [A.5.2](#A1.SS5.SSS2 "A.5.2 A Complementary-Direction Construction ‣ A.5 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")).
Since the Coordinator emits the portfolio as one completion
(Section [4.1](#S4.SS1 "4.1 Hierarchical Policy ‣ 4 Meld: Hierarchical Test-Time Training for Self-Improving Multi-Agent Discovery ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"))
and is trained on $`r^{\Phi}`$, such laws are expressible and rewarded
by its objective.

##### Private adaptation.

To isolate the learning signal that coordination gives each Scientist,
fix $`\mathcal{S}`$ and a full portfolio $`Z`$, let all Scientists start
from a common model with weights $`W_{0}`$, and let
$`\bar{r}^{\Phi}(W_{1},\ldots,W_{N})=\mathbb{E}[r^{\Phi}\mid\mathcal{S},Z]`$
be the expected round reward when Scientist $`i`$ responds from weights
$`W_{i}=W_{0}+\gamma U_{i}V_{i}`$, with LoRA scaling $`\gamma>0`$ and
factors $`\theta_{i}=(U_{i},V_{i})`$ starting from the standard
zero-output initialization $`(0,V_{0})`$ ([Hu et al.,
2022](#bib.bib25)). Write
$`\Gamma_{i}=(\nabla_{W_{i}}\bar{r}^{\Phi})V_{0}^{\top}`$ at the common
model, $`\bar{\Gamma}=N^{-1}\sum_{i}\Gamma_{i}`$, and
$`\mathcal{D}(Z;\mathcal{S})=N^{-2}\sum_{i<j}\|\Gamma_{i}-\Gamma_{j}\|_{F}^{2}`$
for the dispersion of these assignment-conditioned gradients. We compare
one population gradient step of size $`\eta`$ on each private adapter
with the same step on a single shared adapter, whose population gradient
is the average of the $`N`$ Scientist gradients because
Eq. ([6](#S4.E6 "Equation 6 ‣ Token-level update. ‣ 4.3 Alternating Test-Time Adaptation ‣ 4 Meld: Hierarchical Test-Time Training for Self-Improving Multi-Agent Discovery ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"))
averages over the $`GN`$ pooled responses.

###### Proposition 5.4 (Private adaptation retains dispersion).

For differentiable $`\bar{r}^{\Phi}`$, the first-order change of
$`\bar{r}^{\Phi}`$ divided by $`N`$ is
$`\eta\gamma^{2}\big(\|\bar{\Gamma}\|_{F}^{2}+\mathcal{D}(Z;\mathcal{S})\big)`$
for private adapters and $`\eta\gamma^{2}\|\bar{\Gamma}\|_{F}^{2}`$ for
the shared adapter. If every Scientist receives the same assignment,
then $`\mathcal{D}(Z;\mathcal{S})=0`$.

Proposition [5.4](#S5.Thmtheorem4 "Proposition 5.4 (Private adaptation retains dispersion). ‣ Private adaptation. ‣ 5.2 Coordination and Private Adaptation versus Parallel Discovery ‣ 5 Theoretical Analysis ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")
shows that a shared adapter follows only the mean signal
$`\bar{\Gamma}`$, whereas private adapters also retain the dispersion
$`\mathcal{D}(Z;\mathcal{S})`$. Because the Coordinator sets the law of
$`Z`$, it also sets the expected dispersion available to the Scientists,
which is the mechanism behind the co-adaptation loop of
Section [4.3](#S4.SS3 "4.3 Alternating Test-Time Adaptation ‣ 4 Meld: Hierarchical Test-Time Training for Self-Improving Multi-Agent Discovery ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery").
Pooling forgoes exactly this term, since a shared adapter cannot move
along $`\Gamma_{i}-\bar{\Gamma}`$, in exchange for the lower estimation
variance of averaging $`GN`$ responses.

We now show that adaptation converges, in the sense that the discovery

(A1) $`\nabla F`$ is $`L`$-Lipschitz on $`\Omega`$ and

### 6.3 Ablations

We test whether adapting both policy levels improves candidate
generation beyond either update alone. On Iterated Prisoner’s Dilemma
(PD) and Colonel Blotto (CB), full Meld, Coordinator-only,
Scientist-only, and Frozen each generate a fresh 24-candidate batch
after one Coordinator–Scientist cycle
(Appendix [B.5](#A2.SS5 "B.5 Ablation Protocol ‣ Appendix B Experimental Details ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")).
Over three seeds, full Meld raises the PD batch median from $`2.35`$ to
$`2.57`$ and threshold hits from $`2`$ to $`10`$ per 24 attempts
(Table [4](#A2.T4 "Table 4 ‣ Reported statistics. ‣ B.5 Ablation Protocol ‣ Appendix B Experimental Details ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")),
versus only $`5`$ and $`6`$ hits for Coordinator-only and
Scientist-only. On CB, medians stay near zero, while full Meld raises
hits from $`2`$ to $`6`$, versus $`3`$, $`4`$, and $`2`$ for
Coordinator-only, Scientist-only, and Frozen. Joint adaptation thus
yields stronger post-cycle generation than either update alone in these
experiments, improving median and hits on PD but only hits on CB, a gain
that neither the batch median nor a cumulative best score reveals. With
one research unit ($`N{=}1`$) at the same budget, Meld loses nearly half
of its CHAMPS margin over ARTS^(∗), as log-MAE rises from $`1.1592`$ to
$`1.1899`$, so the full gain requires multiple units.

## 7 Conclusion

We introduced Meld, a hierarchical TTT framework that jointly adapts
team-level Coordinator and agent-level Scientist policies on one unseen
task. Updated policies generate the experience and feedback for later
adaptation, forming a recursive self-improvement loop with team-level
coordination and agent-level research exploration. Our analysis
establishes objective alignment, characterizes conditions for
coordination gains beyond independent parallel discovery and the
first-order benefit of private adaptation, and bounds the discovery
regret of adapted models. Across 12 MLGym and 10 MLE-bench tasks, Meld
is best on 20 and achieves the highest aggregate normalized scores on
MLGym with a 4B backbone. Current limitations are round-local credit and
task-local adaptation within a fixed research hierarchy, and cross-task
transfer could be natural future work.

### A.1 Notation and Elementary Properties of the Potential

##### Matrices and blocks.

For matrices $`A,B`$ of equal shape,
$`\langle A,B\rangle_{F}=\operatorname{tr}(A^{\top}B)`$ and
$`\|A\|_{F}^{2}=\langle A,A\rangle_{F}`$. When a parameter block
consists of several matrices, inner products and squared norms are
summed over the matrices, and every identity below holds matrix by
matrix. The trainable blocks are
$`\beta\in\{\mathrm{coord},\mathrm{sci}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{sci}_{N}\}`$
with policies $`\pi_{\beta}`$, actions $`a^{\beta}`$, and contexts
$`x^{\beta}`$ as in
Section [4.2](#S4.SS2 "4.2 A Shared Best-of-Budget Objective ‣ 4 Meld: Hierarchical Test-Time Training for Self-Improving Multi-Agent Discovery ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"):
$`a^{\mathrm{coord}}_{t}=Z_{t}`$,
$`x^{\mathrm{coord}}_{t}=\mathcal{S}_{t}`$, and
$`a^{\mathrm{sci}_{i}}_{t}=u_{t}^{i}`$,
$`x^{\mathrm{sci}_{i}}_{t}=(\mathcal{S}_{t},z_{t}^{i})`$.

##### States and the potential.

A state $`\mathcal{S}`$ carries the derivation tree with node set
$`V(\mathcal{S})`$ and utilities $`s_{v}\in[0,1]`$; the root carries
$`s_{0}`$, every other node is a validated candidate, and
$`B(\mathcal{S})=\max_{v\in V(\mathcal{S})}s_{v}`$. Since every
validated candidate consumed one evaluator call,
$`n_{\mathrm{val}}(\mathcal{S}):=|V(\mathcal{S})|-1\leq K`$, and the
number of padded slots

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
n_{\mathrm{pad}}(\mathcal{S}):=K+1-|V(\mathcal{S})|=K-n_{\mathrm{val}}(\mathcal{S})\ \geq\ 0
``` |  | (10) |

counts evaluator calls that failed or have not been used. Failed calls
therefore do not appear in $`V(\mathcal{S})`$ but are accounted for by
Eq. ([10](#A1.E10 "Equation 10 ‣ States and the potential. ‣ A.1 Notation and Elementary Properties of the Potential ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")).
With $`s_{\min}=0`$, define

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})=\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{S})}e^{s_{v}/\tau}+n_{\mathrm{pad}}(\mathcal{S})\,e^{s_{\min}/\tau},\qquad\Phi_{\tau,K}(\mathcal{S})=\tau\log\frac{\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})}{K+1},
``` |  | (11) |

which is
Eq. ([4](#S4.E4 "Equation 4 ‣ Smooth potential. ‣ 4.2 A Shared Best-of-Budget Objective ‣ 4 Meld: Hierarchical Test-Time Training for Self-Improving Multi-Agent Discovery ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"));
$`\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})`$ is a sum of exactly $`K+1`$ positive terms,
one per evaluator slot including the root. For a state
$`\mathcal{S}^{\prime}`$ and a collection $`X`$ of new validated
candidates, $`\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\oplus X`$ denotes the state extended
by their call-indexed nodes; repeated utilities remain separate entries.
Failed calls leave $`n_{\mathrm{pad}}`$ unchanged because it counts
failed and unused slots together.

###### Lemma A.1 (Slot bookkeeping).

Let $`\mathcal{S}^{\prime}`$ be a state and $`X`$ the validated
candidates produced within its remaining evaluator-call budget. Then

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\oplus X)=\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})+\sum_{c\in X}\big(e^{s(c)/\tau}-e^{s_{\min}/\tau}\big)\ \geq\ \mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}).
``` |  | (12) |

In particular, failed calls leave $`\mathcal{A}`$ unchanged, and for a
single candidate $`c`$ with utility $`s`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Phi_{\tau,K}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\oplus\{c\})-\Phi_{\tau,K}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})=\tau\log\Big(1+\frac{e^{s/\tau}-e^{s_{\min}/\tau}}{\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})}\Big).
``` |  | (13) |

###### Proof.

Each validated candidate $`c\in X`$ adds the node term $`e^{s(c)/\tau}`$
to the first sum in
Eq. ([11](#A1.E11 "Equation 11 ‣ States and the potential. ‣ A.1 Notation and Elementary Properties of the Potential ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"))
and, having consumed one evaluator call, reduces $`n_{\mathrm{pad}}`$ by
one, removing one term $`e^{s_{\min}/\tau}`$; a failed call reduces
neither $`|V|`$ nor the number of remaining slots as counted in
Eq. ([10](#A1.E10 "Equation 10 ‣ States and the potential. ‣ A.1 Notation and Elementary Properties of the Potential ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")),
since $`n_{\mathrm{pad}}`$ counts failed and unused slots alike. This
gives the equality in
Eq. ([12](#A1.E12 "Equation 12 ‣ Lemma A.1 (Slot bookkeeping). ‣ States and the potential. ‣ A.1 Notation and Elementary Properties of the Potential ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")).
The inequality follows from $`s(c)\geq 0=s_{\min}`$. Dividing
Eq. ([12](#A1.E12 "Equation 12 ‣ Lemma A.1 (Slot bookkeeping). ‣ States and the potential. ‣ A.1 Notation and Elementary Properties of the Potential ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"))
with $`X=\{c\}`$ by $`\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})`$ and taking
$`\tau\log`$ gives
Eq. ([13](#A1.E13 "Equation 13 ‣ Lemma A.1 (Slot bookkeeping). ‣ States and the potential. ‣ A.1 Notation and Elementary Properties of the Potential ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")).
∎

###### Lemma A.2 (Monotone, telescoping increments).

Along any run, the round rewards
$`r_{t}^{\Phi}=\Phi_{\tau,K}(\mathcal{S}_{t+1})-\Phi_{\tau,K}(\mathcal{S}_{t})`$
satisfy $`r_{t}^{\Phi}\geq 0`$, and for any
$`0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq R`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{t^{\prime}=t_{1}}^{t_{2}-1}r_{t^{\prime}}^{\Phi}=\Phi_{\tau,K}(\mathcal{S}_{t_{2}})-\Phi_{\tau,K}(\mathcal{S}_{t_{1}});\qquad\text{in particular}\qquad\sum_{t^{\prime}=0}^{R-1}r_{t^{\prime}}^{\Phi}=\Phi_{\tau,K}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{end}})-\Phi_{\tau,K}(\mathcal{S}_{0}).
``` |  | (14) |

Moreover,
$`R^{\Phi}_{t:\mathrm{end}}=r^{\Phi}_{t}+R^{\Phi}_{t+1:\mathrm{end}}`$
for every round $`t`$.

###### Proof.

Nonnegativity follows from
Lemma [A.1](#A1.Thmtheorem1 "Lemma A.1 (Slot bookkeeping). ‣ States and the potential. ‣ A.1 Notation and Elementary Properties of the Potential ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")
applied to the validated candidates of round $`t`$, since $`\log`$ is
increasing. The sums telescope term by term. The last identity is the
case $`t_{1}=t`$, $`t_{2}=t+1`$ combined with the definition
$`R^{\Phi}_{t:\mathrm{end}}=\Phi_{\tau,K}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{end}})-\Phi_{\tau,K}(\mathcal{S}_{t})`$.
∎

### A.2 Trajectory Factorization and Regularity

A run of $`\Pi_{\Theta}`$ with $`\Theta=(\phi,\theta_{1:N})`$ held fixed
produces the trajectory

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\zeta=(\mathcal{S}_{0},Z_{0},U_{0},\mathcal{S}_{1},Z_{1},U_{1},\ldots,Z_{R-1},U_{R-1},\mathcal{S}_{R}),\qquad\mathcal{S}_{R}=\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{end}},
``` |  | (15) |

where $`R\leq K`$ is the number of nonempty rounds until the budget is
exhausted, $`Z_{t}=\{z_{t}^{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{t}}`$, and
$`U_{t}=\{u_{t}^{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{t}}`$. Let
$`\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{S}_{t+1}\mid\mathcal{S}_{t},Z_{t},U_{t})`$ be the
kernel comprising execution by the frozen Executor, evaluation, the tree
update of
Section [3](#S3 "3 Preliminaries ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"),
and budget accounting, and let $`\nu`$ be the law of the initial state.
By the factorization of
Eq. ([3](#S4.E3 "Equation 3 ‣ 4.1 Hierarchical Policy ‣ 4 Meld: Hierarchical Test-Time Training for Self-Improving Multi-Agent Discovery ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")),

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
p_{\Theta}(\zeta)=\nu(\mathcal{S}_{0})\prod_{t=0}^{R-1}\Big[\pi^{\mathrm{coord}}_{\phi}(Z_{t}\mid\mathcal{S}_{t})\prod_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{t}}\pi^{\mathrm{sci}}_{\theta_{i}}(u_{t}^{i}\mid\mathcal{S}_{t},z_{t}^{i})\;\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{S}_{t+1}\mid\mathcal{S}_{t},Z_{t},U_{t})\Big].
``` |  | (16) |

Throughout we work under
Assumption [5.1](#S5.Thmtheorem1 "Assumption 5.1 (Regularity). ‣ Notation. ‣ 5.1 One Objective Across the Hierarchy ‣ 5 Theoretical Analysis ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"),
whose conditions (R1)–(R3) refer to the factorization in
Eq. ([16](#A1.E16 "Equation 16 ‣ A.2 Trajectory Factorization and Regularity ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")):
(R1) $`\nu`$ and $`\mathcal{Q}`$ do not depend on $`\Theta`$; (R2) each
policy density is differentiable in its own block with
parameter-independent support; (R3) differentiation with respect to any
block may be interchanged with the expectation over $`\zeta`$.

###### Remark A.3 (The assumptions for LLM policies).

At a fixed context, bounded-length token actions over a finite
vocabulary have finite support. Softmax probabilities on a
parameter-independent admissible support are differentiable, giving
(R2); any decoding mask is held fixed when taking derivatives. For the
full trajectory law, however, contexts depend on random execution
feedback, so finite action spaces alone do not imply (R3). A sufficient
condition is an integrable envelope $`D_{\beta}(\zeta)`$ such that
$`\sup_{\Theta\in\mathcal{U}}\|\nabla_{\beta}p_{\Theta}(\zeta)\|\leq D_{\beta}(\zeta)`$
in a neighborhood $`\mathcal{U}`$ of the differentiated model. The
bounded potential then permits dominated differentiation. We impose (R3)
on the trajectory law and its fixed-snapshot conditional laws.
Assumption (R1) records the parameter independence of the Executor,
evaluator, and state transition given actions.

We define the filtrations under which actions are drawn. Let
$`\mathcal{E}_{t}=\sigma(\mathcal{S}_{0},Z_{0},U_{0},\ldots,\mathcal{S}_{t})`$
be the history up to and including $`\mathcal{S}_{t}`$, and
$`\mathcal{E}_{t}^{+}=\mathcal{E}_{t}\vee\sigma(Z_{t})`$ the history
after the portfolio of round $`t`$ is drawn. For
$`\beta=\mathrm{coord}`$ write
$`\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\beta}=\mathcal{E}_{t}`$; for
$`\beta=\mathrm{sci}_{i}`$ write
$`\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\beta}=\mathcal{E}_{t}^{+}`$. In both cases
$`x_{t}^{\beta}`$ is $`\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\beta}`$-measurable, and
conditionally on $`\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\beta}`$ the action is drawn as
$`a_{t}^{\beta}\sim\pi_{\beta}(\cdot\mid x_{t}^{\beta})`$; for
$`\beta=\mathrm{sci}_{i}`$ this uses that, given $`\mathcal{S}_{t}`$ and
$`Z_{t}`$, the responses of different Scientists are conditionally
independent by
Eq. ([3](#S4.E3 "Equation 3 ‣ 4.1 Hierarchical Policy ‣ 4 Meld: Hierarchical Test-Time Training for Self-Improving Multi-Agent Discovery ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")).

###### Lemma A.4 (Score identity).

Under (R2), for every block $`\beta`$ and every context $`x`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{\beta}(\cdot\mid x)}\big[\nabla_{\beta}\log\pi_{\beta}(a\mid x)\big]=0.
``` |  | (17) |

###### Proof.

Let $`\mathcal{X}`$ be the parameter-independent support of
$`\pi_{\beta}(\cdot\mid x)`$, which is finite for bounded-length token
actions
(Remark [A.3](#A1.Thmtheorem3 "Remark A.3 (The assumptions for LLM policies). ‣ A.2 Trajectory Factorization and Regularity ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")).
Then
$`\mathbb{E}[\nabla_{\beta}\log\pi_{\beta}(a\mid x)]=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{X}}\pi_{\beta}(a\mid x)\,\frac{\nabla_{\beta}\pi_{\beta}(a\mid x)}{\pi_{\beta}(a\mid x)}=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{X}}\nabla_{\beta}\pi_{\beta}(a\mid x)=\nabla_{\beta}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{X}}\pi_{\beta}(a\mid x)=\nabla_{\beta}1=0`$,
where the interchange of $`\nabla_{\beta}`$ and the finite sum uses only
(R2), since $`\mathcal{X}`$ does not move with the parameters. ∎

###### Lemma A.5 (Baseline lemma).

Let $`\beta`$ act at round $`t`$, and let $`\chi`$ be an integrable,
$`\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\beta}`$-measurable random variable such that
$`\chi\,\nabla_{\beta}\log\pi_{\beta}(a_{t}^{\beta}\mid x_{t}^{\beta})`$
is integrable. Then
$`\mathbb{E}\big[\chi\,\nabla_{\beta}\log\pi_{\beta}(a_{t}^{\beta}\mid x_{t}^{\beta})\big]=0`$.

###### Proof.

By the tower property and $`\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\beta}`$-measurability of
$`\chi`$ and of $`x_{t}^{\beta}`$,
$`\mathbb{E}[\chi\,\nabla_{\beta}\log\pi_{\beta}(a_{t}^{\beta}\mid x_{t}^{\beta})]=\mathbb{E}\big[\chi\,\mathbb{E}[\nabla_{\beta}\log\pi_{\beta}(a_{t}^{\beta}\mid x_{t}^{\beta})\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\beta}]\big]`$,
and the inner conditional expectation is
$`\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{\beta}(\cdot\mid x_{t}^{\beta})}[\nabla_{\beta}\log\pi_{\beta}(a\mid x_{t}^{\beta})]=0`$
by
Lemma [A.4](#A1.Thmtheorem4 "Lemma A.4 (Score identity). ‣ A.2 Trajectory Factorization and Regularity ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery").
∎

###### Lemma A.6 (Block score decomposition).

Under (R1)–(R2), for every block $`\beta`$ and every trajectory
$`\zeta`$ in the support of $`p_{\Theta}`$,

#### A.3.1 The Implemented Surrogate: Rejection, Length Normalization, and Standardization

##### Rejection of malformed responses.

The judge discards malformed hypotheses and regenerates them without
consuming a scored candidate
(Appendix [B.3](#A2.SS3 "B.3 Models, Harness, and Candidate Execution ‣ Appendix B Experimental Details ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")).
Let $`\alpha(a)\in[0,1]`$ be the probability that the judge accepts
$`a`$, which does not depend on the parameters (a deterministic judge is
the case $`\alpha\in\{0,1\}`$), so the effective policy of a block is
the accept-conditioned law
$`\tilde{\pi}_{\beta}(a\mid x)=\pi_{\beta}(a\mid x)\alpha(a)/P_{\beta}(\mathrm{acc}\mid x)`$
with
$`P_{\beta}(\mathrm{acc}\mid x)=\sum_{a}\pi_{\beta}(a\mid x)\alpha(a)`$,
whose support $`\{\alpha>0\}\cap\mathcal{X}`$ is parameter independent;
(R2) and all statements above hold for $`\tilde{\pi}_{\beta}`$. The
implemented score, however, is
$`\nabla_{\beta}\log\pi_{\beta}(a\mid x)=\nabla_{\beta}\log\tilde{\pi}_{\beta}(a\mid x)+\nabla_{\beta}\log P_{\beta}(\mathrm{acc}\mid x)`$,
whose second term is constant in $`a`$ and equals
$`\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{\pi}_{\beta}}[\nabla_{\beta}\log\pi_{\beta}(a\mid x)]`$.
In the leave-one-out estimator this term is multiplied by
$`\widehat{A}_{g}=r^{\Phi}_{g}-b_{g}`$ with
$`\mathbb{E}[b_{g}]=\mathbb{E}[r^{\Phi}_{g}]`$, so its contribution has
expectation
$`\big(\mathbb{E}[r^{\Phi}_{g}]-\mathbb{E}[b_{g}]\big)\nabla_{\beta}\log P_{\beta}(\mathrm{acc}\mid x)=0`$;
part (iv) therefore holds verbatim for the accept-conditioned objective.
The un-baselined identity in
Eq. ([26](#A1.E26 "Equation 26 ‣ Proof of Proposition . ‣ A.3 Proof of Proposition ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"))
does not: it acquires the bias
$`\mathbb{E}[r^{\Phi}_{g}]\nabla_{\beta}\log P_{\beta}(\mathrm{acc}\mid x)`$,
which is one more reason for the baseline.

##### Relation to token-length normalization.

The estimator in part (iv) uses the score of the entire generated
action. Let $`L(a)\geq 1`$ be its response length and
$`h_{\beta}(a)=\nabla_{\beta}\log\pi_{\beta}(a\mid x)=\sum_{j=1}^{L(a)}\nabla_{\beta}\log\pi_{\beta}(y_{j}\mid x,y_{<j})`$.
With advantages held fixed, at the behavior model the negative gradient
of the unregularized, unclipped loss in
Eq. ([6](#S4.E6 "Equation 6 ‣ Token-level update. ‣ 4.3 Alternating Test-Time Adaptation ‣ 4 Meld: Hierarchical Test-Time Training for Self-Improving Multi-Agent Discovery ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"))
is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\widehat{g}_{\mathrm{tok}}=\frac{1}{G}\sum_{g=1}^{G}\frac{\widehat{A}_{g}}{L(a_{g})}h_{\beta}(a_{g}),\qquad\widehat{g}_{\mathrm{seq}}=\frac{1}{G}\sum_{g=1}^{G}\widehat{A}_{g}h_{\beta}(a_{g}).
``` |  | (29) |

Indeed, each importance ratio equals one there, and its derivative is
the corresponding token score. Condition throughout on the snapshot, the
behavior model, and, for a Scientist group, the full fixed portfolio.
Write $`\bar{r}(a)=\mathbb{E}[r_{g}^{\Phi}\mid a_{g}=a]`$ and
$`\bar{r}_{0}=\mathbb{E}[r_{g}^{\Phi}]`$. Since the leave-one-out
baseline is independent of $`a_{g}`$ and has mean $`\bar{r}_{0}`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}[\widehat{g}_{\mathrm{tok}}]=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{\beta,\mathrm{old}}}\left[\frac{\bar{r}(a)-\bar{r}_{0}}{L(a)}\,h_{\beta}(a)\right]_{\beta=\beta_{\mathrm{old}}}.
``` |  | (30) |

In particular, the baseline contribution is
$`\bar{r}_{0}\mathbb{E}[h_{\beta}(a)/L(a)]`$, which need not vanish for
action-dependent lengths. If $`L(a)=L_{0}`$ throughout the support, then
$`\mathbb{E}[\widehat{g}_{\mathrm{tok}}]=L_{0}^{-1}\nabla_{\beta}J^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\beta}(\mathcal{S})`$;
otherwise part (iv) establishes unbiasedness for
$`\widehat{g}_{\mathrm{seq}}`$, not for $`\widehat{g}_{\mathrm{tok}}`$.
The implementation further divides $`\widehat{A}_{g}`$ by the
within-group standard deviation of
$`\{r^{\Phi}_{g^{\prime}}\}_{g^{\prime}=1}^{G}`$
(Appendix [B.3](#A2.SS3 "B.3 Models, Harness, and Candidate Execution ‣ Appendix B Experimental Details ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")).
This factor is a function of all $`G`$ forks, including fork $`g`$, so
the resulting estimator is a data-dependent rescaling of
$`\widehat{g}_{\mathrm{tok}}`$ whose expectation is likewise not covered
by part (iv); as $`G`$ grows the factor concentrates around the
population standard deviation and the rescaling becomes a constant.
Length normalization, advantage standardization, clipping, and KL
regularization thus define the implemented surrogate; none of them
alters the potential alignment of part (i) or the block structure of
part (ii).

### A.4 Exact Surrogate Gap and Frontier-Seeking Increments

The proof of
Proposition [5.2](#S5.Thmtheorem2 "Proposition 5.2 (One objective across the hierarchy). ‣ Notation. ‣ 5.1 One Objective Across the Hierarchy ‣ 5 Theoretical Analysis ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")(i)
shows that
$`\Phi_{\tau,K}(\mathcal{S})=B(\mathcal{S})-\tau\log\frac{K+1}{K_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{S})}`$
with $`K_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{S})\in[1,K+1]`$ the number of slots
counted in frontier units: the surrogate undercounts the best utility by
exactly $`\tau`$ times the log-ratio of nominal to frontier-equivalent
slots, most when a single node stands far above every other slot and not
at all when all slots tie. The same bookkeeping characterizes the
per-round increment earned by one candidate.

###### Proposition A.7 (Frontier-seeking increments).

Let $`\mathcal{S}^{\prime}`$ be a state with
$`B=B(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})`$ and
$`K_{\mathrm{eff}}=K_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})`$, and let
$`c`$ be a validated candidate of utility $`s`$ produced by one of its
remaining evaluator calls. Then

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Phi_{\tau,K}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\oplus\{c\})-\Phi_{\tau,K}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})=\tau\log\Big(1+\frac{e^{(s-B)/\tau}-e^{-B/\tau}}{K_{\mathrm{eff}}}\Big),
``` |  | (31) |

which is strictly increasing in $`s`$. If $`s\leq B`$, the increment is
at most $`\tau\log(1+1/K_{\mathrm{eff}})\leq\tau\log 2`$; if
$`s=B+\omega`$ with $`\omega>0`$, it is at least
$`\tau\log\big(1+(e^{\omega/\tau}-1)/K_{\mathrm{eff}}\big)`$, which
exceeds the former bound as soon as $`\omega>\tau\log 2`$ and is at
least $`\omega-\tau\log(2K_{\mathrm{eff}})`$ whenever
$`\omega\geq\tau\log 2`$.

At any state the credit for a candidate is thus strictly increasing in
its realized utility: tying or trailing the frontier earns a bounded,
$`\tau`$-scaled reward, whereas advancing it by $`\omega`$ earns a
reward within $`\tau\log 2`$ of $`\omega`$ when few nodes sit near the
frontier ($`K_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx 1`$) and less in a crowded frontier,
where repeated confirmation of a known direction is discounted; the
temperature interpolates between the maximum ($`\tau\to 0`$) and the
average utility over slots ($`\tau\to\infty`$). Increments of $`B`$
itself would vanish on every round without a new best; the potential
yields a dense signal that still rewards the frontier most.

Let $`\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})`$,
$`B=B(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})`$, and
$`K_{\mathrm{eff}}=K_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})`$, so that
$`\mathcal{A}=e^{B/\tau}K_{\mathrm{eff}}`$ by the proof of

By

``` math

``` |  | (32) |

which is strictly increasing in $`s`$ because $`e^{s/\tau}`$ is.

*Non-frontier bound.* If $`s\leq B`$, then $`e^{(s-B)/\tau}\leq 1`$ and

*Frontier bound.* Let $`s=B+\omega`$ with $`\omega>0`$. Since

The first expression exceeds the non-frontier bound

Replacing $`K_{\mathrm{eff}}`$ by its upper bound $`K+1`$ gives

### A.5 Proof of Theorem [5.3](#S5.Thmtheorem3 "Theorem 5.3 (Value of coordination at matched marginals). ‣ 5.2 Coordination and Private Adaptation versus Parallel Discovery ‣ 5 Theoretical Analysis ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")

#### A.5.1 Matched-Marginal Comparison and Proof

Fix the snapshot $`\mathcal{S}`$, the $`N`$ Scientist models
$`\theta_{1:N}`$, and the shared frozen Executor and evaluator, and
write $`b=B(\mathcal{S})`$ for the incumbent utility throughout this
subsection. The Scientist models may differ across units but are the
same in the two systems being compared. Both systems make exactly $`N`$
new evaluator calls, including failed attempts, from this snapshot;
$`K`$ remains the common system-level budget. For a portfolio law
$`\pi`$ with marginals $`q_{i}`$, the joint assignment–response policies
are

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\pi(Z,U\mid\mathcal{S})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\pi(Z\mid\mathcal{S})\prod_{i=1}^{N}\pi^{\mathrm{sci}}_{\theta_{i}}(u_{i}\mid\mathcal{S},z^{i}),`$ |  | (34) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\pi^{\mathrm{par}}(Z,U\mid\mathcal{S})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\big[q_{i}(z^{i}\mid\mathcal{S})\,\pi^{\mathrm{sci}}_{\theta_{i}}(u_{i}\mid\mathcal{S},z^{i})\big],`$ |  |  |

so that the parallel comparison retains every Scientist model and
matches each unit’s assignment distribution. Let
$`\kappa_{i}(dy\mid\mathcal{S},z^{i})`$ be the conditional law of unit
$`i`$’s utility after its Scientist responds, the Executor implements
the response, and the evaluator scores it; a failed call returns utility
$`0`$. The conditional independence and per-unit dependence assumptions
of
Section [5.2](#S5.SS2 "5.2 Coordination and Private Adaptation versus Parallel Discovery ‣ 5 Theoretical Analysis ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")
give

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{P}_{\pi}(dy_{1:N}\mid\mathcal{S})=\int\prod_{i=1}^{N}\kappa_{i}(dy_{i}\mid\mathcal{S},z^{i})\,\pi(dZ\mid\mathcal{S}),
``` |  | (35) |

and likewise for $`\pi^{\mathrm{par}}`$: the same kernels occur in both
laws, and only the coupling of the assignments changes. All
probabilities and expectations below condition on this fixed snapshot
and these fixed models.

###### Proof of Theorem [5.3](#S5.Thmtheorem3 "Theorem 5.3 (Value of coordination at matched marginals). ‣ 5.2 Coordination and Private Adaptation versus Parallel Discovery ‣ 5 Theoretical Analysis ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery").

*Step 1: marginal matching and joint failure.* For any measurable
$`A\subseteq[0,1]`$, integrating
Eq. ([35](#A1.E35 "Equation 35 ‣ A.5.1 Matched-Marginal Comparison and Proof ‣ A.5 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"))
over the other utilities yields
$`\Pr_{\pi}(Y_{i}\in A\mid\mathcal{S})=\int\kappa_{i}(A\mid\mathcal{S},z^{i})\,q_{i}(dz^{i}\mid\mathcal{S})=\Pr_{\pi^{\mathrm{par}}}(Y_{i}\in A\mid\mathcal{S})`$,
so the unit marginals, including failure probabilities, agree; setting
$`A=[0,y]`$ defines their common CDF $`H_{i}(y)`$. For the parallel law
the product assignment measure gives

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
H_{\pi^{\mathrm{par}}}(y)=\int\prod_{i=1}^{N}\kappa_{i}([0,y]\mid\mathcal{S},z^{i})\prod_{i=1}^{N}q_{i}(dz^{i}\mid\mathcal{S})=\prod_{i=1}^{N}H_{i}(y),
``` |  | (36) |

whereas for $`\pi`$ the same integral against $`\pi(dZ\mid\mathcal{S})`$
equals $`H_{\pi}(y)`$.

*Step 2: from threshold coverage to expected best utility.* Put
$`M_{Y}=\max_{i}Y_{i}`$. For each realized vector,
$`\max\{b,M_{Y}\}=b+\int_{b}^{1}\mathbf{1}\{M_{Y}>y\}\,dy`$, since the
integral is the length of $`[b,M_{Y})`$ when $`M_{Y}>b`$ and zero
otherwise; the integrand is nonnegative and bounded, so expectation and
integration interchange and

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}_{\pi^{\prime}}[B_{\mathrm{new}}\mid\mathcal{S}]=b+\int_{b}^{1}\big(1-H_{\pi^{\prime}}(y)\big)\,dy,\qquad\pi^{\prime}\in\{\pi,\pi^{\mathrm{par}}\}.
``` |  | (37) |

With
Eq. ([36](#A1.E36 "Equation 36 ‣ Proof of Theorem . ‣ A.5.1 Matched-Marginal Comparison and Proof ‣ A.5 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"))
this gives the closed form of
$`\mathbb{E}_{\pi^{\mathrm{par}}}[B_{\mathrm{new}}]`$ and, by
subtraction, the identity in part (i). Atoms in the utility
distributions cause no difficulty because the pointwise identity uses
the strict event $`M_{Y}>y`$.

*Step 3: the ceiling.* For any $`c\in[b,1]`$ and any realization,
$`\max\{b,Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{N}\}\leq c+\sum_{i}(Y_{i}-c)^{+}`$, because
$`b\leq c`$ and $`Y_{i}\leq c+(Y_{i}-c)^{+}`$ for each $`i`$. Taking
$`\mathbb{E}_{\pi}`$ and using
$`\mathbb{E}(Y_{i}-c)^{+}=\int_{c}^{1}(1-H_{i}(y))\,dy`$, which depends
on the marginals only, gives
$`\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[B_{\mathrm{new}}]\leq c+\sum_{i}\int_{c}^{1}(1-H_{i}(y))\,dy`$;
minimizing over $`c`$ yields
$`\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[B_{\mathrm{new}}]\leq\mathrm{MN}(H)`$ and hence the
inequality in part (i). This is the Meilijson–Nádas bound for the
$`N+1`$ variables $`b,Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{N}`$, whose minimization over all
real $`c`$ is attained in $`[b,1]`$ (for $`c<b`$ the term $`(b-c)^{+}`$
raises the expression to at least its value at $`c=b`$, and for $`c>1`$
it equals $`c`$); it is attained by some coupling of the
marginals ([Meilijson and Nádas, 1979](#bib.bib36)), as [Lai and Robbins
(1976)](#bib.bib35) showed for identically distributed marginals, hence
sharp over all matched laws, but it need not be attained by a law of the
form
([35](#A1.E35 "Equation 35 ‣ A.5.1 Matched-Marginal Comparison and Proof ‣ A.5 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")),
whose dependence is created through the assignments alone.

*Step 4: the potential.* By
Lemma [A.1](#A1.Thmtheorem1 "Lemma A.1 (Slot bookkeeping). ‣ States and the potential. ‣ A.1 Notation and Elementary Properties of the Potential ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"),
the validated candidates $`X`$ of the round satisfy
$`\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}\oplus X)=\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})+\sum_{c\in X}(e^{s(c)/\tau}-1)=\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})+\Delta\mathcal{A}`$,
since a failed unit has $`Y_{i}=0`$ and contributes $`e^{0}-1=0`$; hence
$`r^{\Phi}=\tau\log\frac{\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})+\Delta\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})}=\psi(\Delta\mathcal{A})`$.
The function $`\psi`$ is strictly concave and increasing on
$`[0,\infty)`$, and
$`\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\Delta\mathcal{A}]=\sum_{i}\int_{0}^{1}(e^{y/\tau}-1)\,dH_{i}(y)`$
is determined by the marginals, so it is the same under every matched
law. Jensen’s inequality gives
$`\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\psi(\Delta\mathcal{A})]\leq\psi(\mathbb{E}[\Delta\mathcal{A}])`$,
with equality if and only if $`\Delta\mathcal{A}`$ is $`\pi`$-a.s.
constant by strict concavity. Under $`\pi^{\mathrm{par}}`$ the summands
of $`\Delta\mathcal{A}`$ are independent, so $`\Delta\mathcal{A}`$ is
degenerate only if every summand is, that is, only if every $`Y_{i}`$ is
degenerate, and otherwise the inequality is strict.

*Step 5: negative association.* Suppose $`(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{N})`$ is
negatively associated under $`\pi`$. Negative association is preserved
by coordinatewise increasing maps ([Joag-Dev and Proschan,
1983](#bib.bib38), Property P6), so $`(e^{Y_{i}/\tau}-1)_{i}`$ is
negatively associated, and for negatively associated summands the sum is
dominated in the convex order by the sum of independent copies with the
same marginals ([Shao, 2000](#bib.bib37), Theorem 1):
$`\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\varphi(\Delta\mathcal{A})]\leq\mathbb{E}_{\pi^{\mathrm{par}}}[\varphi(\Delta\mathcal{A})]`$
for every convex $`\varphi`$, since under $`\pi^{\mathrm{par}}`$ the
summands are exactly such independent copies. Taking $`\varphi=-\psi`$
gives part (iii). Permutation distributions are negatively
associated ([Joag-Dev and Proschan, 1983](#bib.bib38), Theorem 2.11);
the utility vector is one whenever $`N`$ distinct directions are
assigned to the $`N`$ units by a uniformly random permutation and each
unit’s utility is determined by its direction. The property extends to
utilities of the form $`Y_{i}=f_{i}(X_{i},\epsilon_{i})`$ with $`X_{i}`$
the direction-determined value, $`\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{N}`$
independent of each other and of the permutation, and each $`f_{i}`$
coordinatewise increasing, because the union of independent negatively
associated families is negatively associated and increasing functions of
disjoint subsets preserve the property ([Joag-Dev and Proschan,
1983](#bib.bib38), Properties P7 and P6). Without such structure the
condition can fail, for instance when the noise scale depends on the
direction. ∎

###### Remark A.8 (Why part (ii) is stated separately).

The sign of the best-of-round gain in part (i) does not control the sign
of the potential gain. Take $`N=2`$, $`\tau=0.05`$, $`K=48`$, a root of
utility $`b=0.40`$ and no other node, three directions whose utilities
are deterministically $`0.40`$, $`0.45`$, and $`0.50`$, unit marginals
$`(0.32,0.48,0.20)`$ over them, and the assignment coupling that places
mass $`0.04`$ on $`(0.40,0.40)`$, $`0.28`$ on each of $`(0.40,0.45)`$
and $`(0.45,0.40)`$, and $`0.20`$ on each of $`(0.45,0.50)`$ and
$`(0.50,0.45)`$. Then $`\prod_{i}H_{i}(y)-H_{\pi}(y)\geq 0`$ for every
$`y`$ and the best-of-round gain is $`+5.1\times 10^{-3}`$, but the
potential gain is $`-8\times 10^{-5}`$: the coupling reduces joint
failure on the diagonal, yet the law of $`\Delta\mathcal{A}`$ it induces
is not dominated in the convex order by that of $`\pi^{\mathrm{par}}`$
(its variance is even slightly smaller), so the concavity of $`\psi`$
does not favor it. The potential is a function of the whole utility
vector rather than of its maximum, and is governed by the convex order
of $`\Delta\mathcal{A}`$, which parts (ii)–(iii) address directly.

##### Two-unit interpretation.

When $`N=2`$, let $`I_{i}(y)=\mathbf{1}\{Y_{i}\leq y\}`$. Marginal
matching yields
$`\prod_{i}H_{i}(y)-H_{\pi}(y)=-\operatorname{Cov}_{\pi}\big(I_{1}(y),I_{2}(y)\mid\mathcal{S}\big)`$:
negatively correlated threshold failures translate directly into
improved best-of-round utility while preserving both marginal utility
distributions.

#### A.5.2 A Complementary-Direction Construction

Let $`N\geq 2`$ and suppose the snapshot offers $`N`$ feasible research
directions. For every unit, the same direction deterministically yields
a validated utility $`s_{\mathrm{hi}}>b=B(\mathcal{S})`$ and each of the
other $`N-1`$ directions yields utility $`s_{\mathrm{lo}}\in[0,b]`$.
Under the parallel law, every unit independently chooses a uniformly
random direction. Under the coordinated law, draw a uniformly random
permutation of the $`N`$ directions and assign one to each unit. Every
unit has the same uniform assignment marginal under both laws, and both
make exactly $`N`$ evaluator calls.

For $`y\in[b,s_{\mathrm{hi}})`$ each marginal CDF is $`(N-1)/N`$. The
parallel law misses the successful direction with probability
$`(1-1/N)^{N}`$, whereas the permutation always assigns it to one unit;
for $`y\geq s_{\mathrm{hi}}`$ both joint CDFs equal $`1`$. Hence

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\prod_{i}H_{i}(y)-H_{\mathrm{perm}}(y)`$ | $`\displaystyle=(1-1/N)^{N}\,\mathbf{1}\{b\leq y<s_{\mathrm{hi}}\},`$ |  | (38) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{perm}}[B_{\mathrm{new}}]-\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{par}}[B_{\mathrm{new}}]`$ | $`\displaystyle=(s_{\mathrm{hi}}-b)(1-1/N)^{N}.`$ |  |  |

In particular, for $`N=2`$ the probability of finding utility
$`s_{\mathrm{hi}}`$ is $`3/4`$ under independent assignments and $`1`$
under coordination, although each unit finds it with probability $`1/2`$
in both systems. The permutation law attains the ceiling of part (i):
each unit’s utility is $`s_{\mathrm{hi}}`$ with probability $`1/N`$ and
$`s_{\mathrm{lo}}`$ otherwise, so for $`c\in[b,s_{\mathrm{hi}}]`$ the
expression $`c+\sum_{i}\mathbb{E}(Y_{i}-c)^{+}`$ equals
$`c+(s_{\mathrm{hi}}-c)=s_{\mathrm{hi}}`$, whence
$`\mathrm{MN}(H)=s_{\mathrm{hi}}=\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{perm}}[B_{\mathrm{new}}]`$.

It also attains the ceiling of part (ii) for every $`\tau>0`$. Let
$`R_{\mathrm{hi}}`$ count the units assigned to the successful
direction; then
$`\Delta\mathcal{A}=N(e^{s_{\mathrm{lo}}/\tau}-1)+R_{\mathrm{hi}}\,(e^{s_{\mathrm{hi}}/\tau}-e^{s_{\mathrm{lo}}/\tau})`$.
Under the permutation law $`R_{\mathrm{hi}}=1`$, so
$`\Delta\mathcal{A}`$ is constant and
$`\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{perm}}[\psi(\Delta\mathcal{A})]=\psi(\mathbb{E}[\Delta\mathcal{A}])`$;
under independent assignments
$`R_{\mathrm{hi}}\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(N,1/N)`$ has mean $`1`$ and
positive variance, so strict concavity gives
$`\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{par}}[\psi(\Delta\mathcal{A})]<\psi(\mathbb{E}[\Delta\mathcal{A}])`$,
and the potential gap
$`\psi(\mathbb{E}[\Delta\mathcal{A}])-\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{par}}[\psi(\Delta\mathcal{A})]`$
is strictly positive.

A full-support version is obtained from the assignment law
$`\pi^{\lambda}=(1-\lambda)\pi^{\mathrm{par}}+\lambda\pi^{\mathrm{perm}}`$
with $`0<\lambda<1`$. This mixture preserves every uniform marginal and
assigns positive probability to every one of the $`N^{N}`$ portfolios,
and both the best-of-round and the potential gains over
$`\pi^{\mathrm{par}}`$ equal $`\lambda`$ times their values under
$`\pi^{\mathrm{perm}}`$, since expectations are affine in the law. In
particular, the expected round reward of the Coordinator increases
linearly in the weight $`\lambda`$ it places on the complementary law.

###### Lemma A.9 (Pairwise variance identity).

For matrices $`A_{1},\ldots,A_{N}`$ with mean
$`\bar{A}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}A_{i}`$,
$`\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\|A_{i}\|_{F}^{2}-\|\bar{A}\|_{F}^{2}=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i<j}\|A_{i}-A_{j}\|_{F}^{2}`$.

###### Proof.

Expanding the squares,
$`\sum_{i<j}\|A_{i}-A_{j}\|_{F}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}(\|A_{i}\|_{F}^{2}+\|A_{j}\|_{F}^{2}-2\langle A_{i},A_{j}\rangle_{F})=N\sum_{i}\|A_{i}\|_{F}^{2}-\|\sum_{i}A_{i}\|_{F}^{2}`$.
Divide by $`N^{2}`$ and use $`\sum_{i}A_{i}=N\bar{A}`$. ∎

###### Lemma A.10 (Private-adapter specialization).

In the setting above, one population-gradient step gives
$`\Delta W_{i}^{+}=\eta\gamma^{2}G_{i}P_{V}`$ for private adapters and
$`\Delta W_{\mathrm{sh}}^{+}=\eta\gamma^{2}\bar{G}P_{V}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\Delta W_{i}^{+}`$
for the shared adapter, so
$`\Delta W_{i}^{+}-\Delta W_{j}^{+}=\eta\gamma^{2}(\Gamma_{i}-\Gamma_{j})V_{0}`$.
If $`\bar{r}^{\Phi}(\cdot;\mathcal{S},Z)`$ has an $`L_{r}`$-Lipschitz
gradient on the update segments, then

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{priv}}(Z)-\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{sh}}(Z)=\eta\gamma^{2}\mathcal{D}(Z;\mathcal{S})+R_{\eta}(Z),\qquad|R_{\eta}(Z)|\leq L_{r}\eta^{2}\gamma^{4}\|V_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\,\tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_{i}\|\Gamma_{i}\|_{F}^{2}.
``` |  | (41) |

If moreover
$`\mathbb{E}_{Z}[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\|\Gamma_{i}\|_{F}^{2}\mid\mathcal{S}]<\infty`$,
then
$`\mathbb{E}_{Z}[\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{priv}}-\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{sh}}\mid\mathcal{S}]=\eta\gamma^{2}\mathcal{D}_{\phi}(\mathcal{S})+O(\eta^{2})`$,
which is strictly positive for all sufficiently small $`\eta>0`$
whenever $`\mathcal{D}_{\phi}(\mathcal{S})>0`$; if
$`\mathcal{D}_{\phi}(\mathcal{S})=0`$, the two schemes produce the same
weights almost surely.

###### Proof.

*Step 1: effective updates.* For any differentiable $`\bar{r}^{\Phi}`$,
the differential $`dW=\gamma\,dU\,V+\gamma\,U\,dV`$ gives
$`\nabla_{U}\bar{r}^{\Phi}(W(U,V))=\gamma\nabla_{W}\bar{r}^{\Phi}(W(U,V))V^{\top}`$
and
$`\nabla_{V}\bar{r}^{\Phi}(W(U,V))=\gamma U^{\top}\nabla_{W}\bar{r}^{\Phi}(W(U,V))`$,
by cyclicity of the trace. At $`(0,V_{0})`$, Scientist $`i`$ therefore
has factor gradients $`(\gamma\Gamma_{i},0)`$, so
$`U_{i}^{+}=\eta\gamma\Gamma_{i}`$, $`V_{i}^{+}=V_{0}`$, and

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Delta W_{i}^{+}=\gamma U_{i}^{+}V_{i}^{+}=\eta\gamma^{2}G_{i}P_{V},\qquad\Delta W_{\mathrm{sh}}^{+}=\eta\gamma^{2}\bar{G}P_{V}=\tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_{i}\Delta W_{i}^{+},
``` |  | (42) |

and
$`\Delta W_{i}^{+}-\Delta W_{j}^{+}=\eta\gamma^{2}(\Gamma_{i}-\Gamma_{j})V_{0}`$.

*Step 2: first-order gains and remainders.* For any matrix $`A`$ of the
shape of $`G_{i}`$, cyclicity of the trace and the spectral-norm
inequality give

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\langle A,AP_{V}\rangle_{F}=\|AV_{0}^{\top}\|_{F}^{2},\qquad\|AP_{V}\|_{F}=\|(AV_{0}^{\top})V_{0}\|_{F}\leq\|AV_{0}^{\top}\|_{F}\|V_{0}\|_{2}.
``` |  | (43) |

For a joint displacement $`\Delta=(\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{N})`$, the
fundamental theorem of calculus yields

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\bar{r}^{\Phi}(W_{0}+\Delta_{1},\ldots,W_{0}+\Delta_{N})-\bar{r}^{\Phi}(W_{0},\ldots,W_{0})=\textstyle\sum_{i}\langle G_{i},\Delta_{i}\rangle_{F}+\varrho(\Delta),\ \ |\varrho(\Delta)|\leq\tfrac{L_{r}}{2}\textstyle\sum_{i}\|\Delta_{i}\|_{F}^{2},
``` |  | (44) |

by integrating the Lipschitz bound along the segment and applying
Cauchy–Schwarz. For the private step,
$`\sum_{i}\langle G_{i},\Delta W_{i}^{+}\rangle_{F}=\eta\gamma^{2}\sum_{i}\|\Gamma_{i}\|_{F}^{2}`$
by
Eq. ([43](#A1.E43 "Equation 43 ‣ Proof. ‣ A.6.1 Setting ‣ A.6 Private Adaptation and Proof of Proposition ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"));
for the shared step,
$`\sum_{i}\langle G_{i},\Delta W_{\mathrm{sh}}^{+}\rangle_{F}=\eta\gamma^{2}N\langle\bar{G},\bar{G}P_{V}\rangle_{F}=\eta\gamma^{2}N\|\bar{\Gamma}\|_{F}^{2}`$.
Dividing by $`N`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{priv}}(Z)=\eta\gamma^{2}\tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_{i}\|\Gamma_{i}\|_{F}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{N}\varrho(\Delta W^{+}_{1:N}),\qquad\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{sh}}(Z)=\eta\gamma^{2}\|\bar{\Gamma}\|_{F}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{N}\varrho(\Delta W^{+}_{\mathrm{sh}},\ldots,\Delta W^{+}_{\mathrm{sh}}),
``` |  | (45) |

and
Lemma [A.9](#A1.Thmtheorem9 "Lemma A.9 (Pairwise variance identity). ‣ A.6.1 Setting ‣ A.6 Private Adaptation and Proof of Proposition ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")
turns the difference of the first-order terms into
$`\eta\gamma^{2}\mathcal{D}(Z;\mathcal{S})`$, which is
Eq. ([41](#A1.E41 "Equation 41 ‣ Lemma A.10 (Private-adapter specialization). ‣ A.6.1 Setting ‣ A.6 Private Adaptation and Proof of Proposition ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"))
with $`R_{\eta}(Z)`$ the difference of the two remainders. By
Eqs. ([43](#A1.E43 "Equation 43 ‣ Proof. ‣ A.6.1 Setting ‣ A.6 Private Adaptation and Proof of Proposition ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"))–([44](#A1.E44 "Equation 44 ‣ Proof. ‣ A.6.1 Setting ‣ A.6 Private Adaptation and Proof of Proposition ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"))
and
$`\|\bar{\Gamma}\|_{F}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\|\Gamma_{i}\|_{F}^{2}`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
|R_{\eta}(Z)|\leq\frac{L\eta^{2}\gamma^{4}}{2}\Big[\tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_{i}\|G_{i}P_{V}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\bar{G}P_{V}\|_{F}^{2}\Big]\leq L_{r}\eta^{2}\gamma^{4}\|V_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\,\tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_{i}\|\Gamma_{i}\|_{F}^{2}.
``` |  | (46) |

*Step 3: averaging over portfolios.* Taking conditional expectations in
Eq. ([41](#A1.E41 "Equation 41 ‣ Lemma A.10 (Private-adapter specialization). ‣ A.6.1 Setting ‣ A.6 Private Adaptation and Proof of Proposition ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"))
and using
Eq. ([46](#A1.E46 "Equation 46 ‣ Proof. ‣ A.6.1 Setting ‣ A.6 Private Adaptation and Proof of Proposition ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"))

$`\mathbb{E}_{Z}[\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{priv}}-\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{sh}}\mid\mathcal{S}]\geq\eta\gamma^{2}\mathcal{D}_{\phi}(\mathcal{S})-C_{\phi}(\mathcal{S})\eta^{2}`$
with

which is positive for
$`0<\eta<\gamma^{2}\mathcal{D}_{\phi}(\mathcal{S})/C_{\phi}(\mathcal{S})`$.

$`\Gamma_{i}=\bar{\Gamma}`$ for every $`i`$ almost surely, and
Eq. ([42](#A1.E42 "Equation 42 ‣ Proof. ‣ A.6.1 Setting ‣ A.6 Private Adaptation and Proof of Proposition ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"))

#### A.7.1 Decomposition and Coverage

###### Lemma A.11 (Regret decomposition).

For every $`\Theta\in\Omega`$,
$`s^{\star}-J_{T}(\Pi_{\Theta};\mathcal{P})\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cov}}+\tau\log(K+1)+F^{\star}-F(\Theta)`$.

###### Proof.

By
Proposition [5.2](#S5.Thmtheorem2 "Proposition 5.2 (One objective across the hierarchy). ‣ Notation. ‣ 5.1 One Objective Across the Hierarchy ‣ 5 Theoretical Analysis ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")(i),
for every $`\Theta^{\prime}\in\Omega`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
F(\Theta^{\prime})\leq J_{T}(\Pi_{\Theta^{\prime}};\mathcal{P})\leq F(\Theta^{\prime})+\tau\log(K+1).
``` |  | (47) |

Taking the supremum over $`\Theta^{\prime}\in\Omega`$ in the right
inequality gives $`J_{T}^{\mathcal{H}}\leq F^{\star}+\tau\log(K+1)`$,
and the left inequality at $`\Theta^{\prime}=\Theta`$ gives
$`J_{T}(\Pi_{\Theta};\mathcal{P})\geq F(\Theta)`$. Hence
$`s^{\star}-J_{T}(\Pi_{\Theta};\mathcal{P})=\big(s^{\star}-J_{T}^{\mathcal{H}}\big)+\big(J_{T}^{\mathcal{H}}-J_{T}(\Pi_{\Theta};\mathcal{P})\big)\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cov}}+\tau\log(K+1)+F^{\star}-F(\Theta)`$.
∎

###### Lemma A.12 (Coverage).

Suppose some $`\Pi^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{H}`$ produces at every evaluator
call, conditioned on any history, a valid candidate of utility at least
$`s^{\star}-\epsilon`$ with probability at least $`p_{\epsilon}>0`$.
Then
$`\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cov}}\leq\epsilon+(s^{\star}-s_{0})e^{-Kp_{\epsilon}}`$.

###### Proof.

Index the evaluator calls of a run of $`\Pi^{\dagger}`$ by
$`j=1,\ldots,K`$, ordering the calls of one round arbitrarily, let
$`\mathcal{K}_{j-1}`$ be the history before call $`j`$, including the
outcomes of earlier calls of the same round, let $`\mathsf{S}_{j}`$ be
the event that call $`j`$ returns a validated candidate with utility at
least $`s^{\star}-\epsilon`$, and let
$`\mathsf{E}_{j}=\bigcap_{j^{\prime}\leq j}\mathsf{S}_{j^{\prime}}^{c}`$,
with $`\mathsf{E}_{0}`$ the sure event. The hypothesis states
$`\mathbb{P}(\mathsf{S}_{j}\mid\mathcal{K}_{j-1})\geq p_{\epsilon}`$.
Since $`\mathsf{E}_{j-1}\in\mathcal{K}_{j-1}`$ and
$`\mathsf{E}_{j}=\mathsf{E}_{j-1}\cap\mathsf{S}_{j}^{c}`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{P}(\mathsf{E}_{j})=\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbf{1}\{\mathsf{E}_{j-1}\}\,\mathbb{P}(\mathsf{S}_{j}^{c}\mid\mathcal{K}_{j-1})\big]\leq(1-p_{\epsilon})\,\mathbb{P}(\mathsf{E}_{j-1}),\qquad\text{so}\qquad\mathbb{P}(\mathsf{E}_{K})\leq(1-p_{\epsilon})^{K}\leq e^{-Kp_{\epsilon}}.
``` |  | (48) |

On $`\mathsf{E}_{K}^{c}`$ some validated candidate has utility at least
$`s^{\star}-\epsilon`$, so
$`s^{\star}-B(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{end}})\leq\epsilon`$; on
$`\mathsf{E}_{K}`$ the root remains, so
$`B(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{end}})\geq s_{0}`$. Therefore
$`s^{\star}-J_{T}(\Pi^{\dagger};\mathcal{P})=\mathbb{E}[s^{\star}-B(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{end}})]\leq\epsilon\,\mathbb{P}(\mathsf{E}_{K}^{c})+(s^{\star}-s_{0})\,\mathbb{P}(\mathsf{E}_{K})\leq\epsilon+(s^{\star}-s_{0})e^{-Kp_{\epsilon}}`$,
and
$`\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cov}}\leq s^{\star}-J_{T}(\Pi^{\dagger};\mathcal{P})`$
because $`\Pi^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{H}`$. ∎

#### A.7.2 Two-Block Ascent with Biased Estimates

Write $`\vartheta=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{N})`$ for the stacked
Scientist block. As in

$`\widehat{\nabla}_{m}`$ is supported on the block $`\mathsf{b}_{m}`$
updated at step $`m`$ and is zero outside it, and
$`\nabla_{\mathsf{b}}F`$ denotes the gradient of $`F`$ restricted to
block $`\mathsf{b}`$ and padded with zeros likewise, so that
$`\|\nabla F\|^{2}=\|\nabla_{\phi}F\|^{2}+\|\nabla_{\vartheta}F\|^{2}`$.
The updates of
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ Update and commit. ‣ 4.3 Alternating Test-Time Adaptation ‣ 4 Meld: Hierarchical Test-Time Training for Self-Improving Multi-Agent Discovery ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")

|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Theta^{(m+1)}=\Theta^{(m)}+\eta\,\widehat{\nabla}_{m},\qquad m=0,\ldots,2C-1,\qquad\mathsf{b}_{2j}=\phi,\quad\mathsf{b}_{2j+1}=\vartheta,
``` |  | (49) |

where $`\Theta^{(m)}`$ is $`\mathcal{F}_{m}`$-measurable and the
filtrations are nested. Since $`\Omega`$ is convex and almost surely
contains every iterate, all update segments lie in $`\Omega`$, and the

$`\eta\leq 1/(4L)`$; this condition is automatic whenever $`\Omega`$

omitted downstream terms $`R^{\Phi}_{t+1:\mathrm{end}}`$ of
Eq. ([7](#S5.E7 "Equation 7 ‣ Proposition 5.2 (One objective across the hierarchy). ‣ Notation. ‣ 5.1 One Objective Across the Hierarchy ‣ 5 Theoretical Analysis ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")),

$`\delta`$ bounds their sum. The variance bound $`\sigma^{2}`$ is that
of a group estimate, which averages $`G`$ forks.

###### Proposition A.13 (Two-block ascent with biased estimates).

Under (A1)–(A2), for $`0<\eta\leq 1/(4L)`$ and $`q=1-\eta\mu/4`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}(2C)\leq q^{C}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}(0)+\frac{5L\eta\sigma^{2}}{\mu}+\frac{8\delta^{2}}{\mu}.
``` |  | (50) |

For an odd number of updates,
$`\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}(2C+1)\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}(2C)+\frac{3}{4}\eta\delta^{2}+\frac{1}{2}L\eta^{2}\sigma^{2}`$.

###### Proof.

*Step 1: one block update.* Condition on $`\mathcal{F}_{m}`$, write
$`v_{m}=\nabla_{\mathsf{b}_{m}}F(\Theta^{(m)})`$, and decompose
$`\widehat{\nabla}_{m}=v_{m}+e_{m}+\xi_{m}`$ with
$`e_{m}=\bar{\nabla}_{m}-v_{m}`$,
$`\bar{\nabla}_{m}=\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\nabla}_{m}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m}]`$,
so that $`\|e_{m}\|\leq\delta`$,
$`\mathbb{E}[\xi_{m}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m}]=0`$, and
$`\mathbb{E}[\|\xi_{m}\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m}]\leq\sigma^{2}`$ by
(A2). By (A1) and since $`\widehat{\nabla}_{m}`$ is supported on
$`\mathsf{b}_{m}`$,
$`F(\Theta^{(m+1)})\geq F(\Theta^{(m)})+\eta\langle v_{m},\widehat{\nabla}_{m}\rangle-\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2}\|\widehat{\nabla}_{m}\|^{2}`$.
Now
$`\mathbb{E}[\langle v_{m},\widehat{\nabla}_{m}\rangle\mid\mathcal{F}_{m}]=\|v_{m}\|^{2}+\langle v_{m},e_{m}\rangle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|v_{m}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}`$
and
$`\mathbb{E}[\|\widehat{\nabla}_{m}\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m}]=\|v_{m}+e_{m}\|^{2}+\mathbb{E}[\|\xi_{m}\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m}]\leq 2\|v_{m}\|^{2}+2\delta^{2}+\sigma^{2}`$,
hence

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[F(\Theta^{(m+1)})\mid\mathcal{F}_{m}]`$ | $`\displaystyle\geq F(\Theta^{(m)})+\eta\Big(\frac{1}{2}-L\eta\Big)\|v_{m}\|^{2}-\eta\delta^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2}+L\eta\Big)-\frac{L\eta^{2}\sigma^{2}}{2}`$ |  | (51) |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\geq F(\Theta^{(m)})+\frac{\eta}{4}\|v_{m}\|^{2}-\frac{3}{4}\eta\delta^{2}-\frac{L\eta^{2}\sigma^{2}}{2},`$ |  |  |

where the last step uses $`\eta\leq 1/(4L)`$.

*Step 2: a complete cycle.* Write $`v=\nabla_{\phi}F(\Theta^{(2j)})`$,
$`w_{0}=\nabla_{\vartheta}F(\Theta^{(2j)})`$, and
$`w=\nabla_{\vartheta}F(\Theta^{(2j+1)})`$. By (A1),
$`\|w-w_{0}\|\leq L\eta\|\widehat{\nabla}_{2j}\|`$, and
$`\|a\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\|a^{\prime}\|^{2}-\|a-a^{\prime}\|^{2}`$
gives

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\|w\|^{2}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\|w_{0}\|^{2}-L^{2}\eta^{2}\|\widehat{\nabla}_{2j}\|^{2},\qquad\mathbb{E}[\|\widehat{\nabla}_{2j}\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{2j}]\leq 2\|v\|^{2}+2\delta^{2}+\sigma^{2}.

###### Remark A.14 (Rate, noise, bias, and parallelism).

With $`\delta=\sigma=0`$,
Eq. ([50](#A1.E50 "Equation 50 ‣ Proposition A.13 (Two-block ascent with biased estimates). ‣ A.7.2 Two-Block Ascent with Biased Estimates ‣ A.7 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"))
gives geometric decay to zero; the two floors are the price of gradient
noise and bias under a Polyak–Łojasiewicz condition (see [Karimi et al.,
2016](#bib.bib30) for the stochastic case). The cross-block term in
Eq. ([52](#A1.E52 "Equation 52 ‣ Proof. ‣ A.7.2 Two-Block Ascent with Biased Estimates ‣ A.7 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"))
is what the Gauss–Seidel order pays for evaluating the Scientist
gradient after the Coordinator has moved; it costs a constant factor,
not a dependence on $`N`$. Parallel Scientist updates avoid an explicit
$`1/(N+1)`$ block-selection factor because the exponent counts
Coordinator–Scientist cycles rather than individual blocks; $`L`$,
$`\mu`$, $`\sigma^{2}`$, and $`\delta`$ may still depend on $`N`$.

###### Remark A.15 (Private-gradient energy decomposition).

At any Scientist update, write $`g_{i}=\nabla_{\theta_{i}}F`$,
$`\bar{g}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}g_{i}`$, and
$`\mathcal{D}^{\vartheta}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i<j}\|g_{i}-g_{j}\|^{2}`$.
Lemma [A.9](#A1.Thmtheorem9 "Lemma A.9 (Pairwise variance identity). ‣ A.6.1 Setting ‣ A.6 Private Adaptation and Proof of Proposition ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")
gives
$`\|\nabla_{\vartheta}F\|^{2}=\sum_{i}\|g_{i}\|^{2}=N\|\bar{g}\|^{2}+\mathcal{D}^{\vartheta}`$,
so dispersion contributes additively to the gradient energy that drives
Eq. ([51](#A1.E51 "Equation 51 ‣ Proof. ‣ A.7.2 Two-Block Ascent with Biased Estimates ‣ A.7 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")).
At a common model $`\theta_{1}=\cdots=\theta_{N}=\theta`$, a shared
adapter with the mean-reduced pooled loss moves every Scientist by
$`\eta\bar{g}`$ while private steps move Scientist $`i`$ by
$`\eta g_{i}`$; their first-order ascents are $`\eta N\|\bar{g}\|^{2}`$
and $`\eta(N\|\bar{g}\|^{2}+\mathcal{D}^{\vartheta})`$, the
parameter-space counterpart of
Lemma [A.10](#A1.Thmtheorem10 "Lemma A.10 (Private-adapter specialization). ‣ A.6.1 Setting ‣ A.6 Private Adaptation and Proof of Proposition ‣ Appendix A Proofs for Section ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery").
A uniform PL constant, however, lower-bounds the ratio of gradient
energy to objective gap along all iterates; a larger dispersion at one
snapshot does not by itself yield a larger $`\mu`$.

### B.2 Private Evaluators

We score all candidates with private evaluators that run outside the
agent workspace. Test labels, evaluator state, and protected scoring
files are inaccessible to the research agents and are restored before
each validation. For Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, and MNLI, we hold out a
test split from the labeled data and keep its labels in the private
evaluator. For House Prices, we use MLGym’s split of the 1460 labeled
rows (1022 train, 146 validation, 292 test) with test labels moved to
the private directory. Image and language candidates run in a sandbox
with network access disabled and task data exposed through a
permission-enforcing local filesystem.

##### Game-theory evaluators.

Each scoring plays 10 rounds $`\times`$ 10,000 Monte-Carlo simulations
and reports the mean payoff per round. The evaluator isolates opponent
randomness from the candidate, protects the opponent and scorer files,
and rebuilds a clean evaluation directory for each scoring.

##### Control-task evaluators.

MountainCar, Breakout, and MetaMaze share one evaluator, byte-identical
to MLGym’s up to the environment id and episode cap. A scoring loads the
submission’s five checkpoints and rolls each out on five fixed seeds
with 256 vectorized environments (6,400 rollouts), reporting the mean
reward. Rollouts are stochastic but fully determined by the fixed seeds
within one software build; because frozen seeds produce different
networks under different jax/flax versions, the baseline anchors in
Table [2](#A2.T2 "Table 2 ‣ B.1 Tasks, Metrics, and Reporting Anchors ‣ Appendix B Experimental Details ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")
are re-measured on our stack.

##### Language Modeling evaluator.

The score is cross-entropy on a fixed 10,485,760-token FineWeb
validation shard (320 deterministic batches of $`32\times 1024`$ tokens,
GPT-2 BPE). The training script never reads the validation shard; inside
the sandbox the shard path is blanked, and the private scorer, which
runs outside the sandbox, reads the real one.

### B.3 Models, Harness, and Candidate Execution

##### Executor and judge.

All methods share the same Executor, Claude Haiku 4.5 operating a tool
loop with read, write, run, and validate actions inside the candidate
workspace. The Executor is frozen throughout; it implements the plan it
is given and returns the candidate together with execution and
validation feedback. A per-batch judge, also Haiku 4.5, discards
malformed hypotheses (empty, off-schema, or inexecutable plans) before
execution in every arm; a discarded hypothesis is regenerated and does
not consume a scored candidate.

##### Policies and adapters.

Meld uses Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 as the frozen backbone for both the
Coordinator and the Scientists, served locally in bfloat16, with four
independent LoRA adapters: one coordination adapter $`\phi`$ and one
private Scientist adapter $`\theta_{i}`$ per unit ($`N{=}3`$). Each
adapter has rank $`r{=}32`$, scaling $`\alpha{=}64`$, no dropout, and is
applied to the seven linear projections of every transformer block (q,
k, v, o, gate, up, down); each adapter has 66M parameters. The
Coordinator emits the complete portfolio in one schema-constrained JSON
completion of at most 1200 tokens containing, for each unit, a parent
identifier chosen from a retrieved shortlist of at most 16 tree nodes
(at most two nodes from the same family) and a free-text directive with
three fields (research question, observed bottleneck, constraints). Each
Scientist emits a hypothesis and an execution plan of at most 3400
tokens for its assignment. All adapters are reset to their initial
checkpoint at the start of every task.

the updated serving adapters are used for the next group. When the

### B.4 Baseline Implementations

The three baselines are re-implemented in the same harness as Meld while
preserving the core search mechanisms of their cited methods.
AIRA ([Toledo et al., 2025](#bib.bib15)) formulates research as
graph-based search over candidate solutions: it selects previously
evaluated nodes, applies LLM-driven Draft, Debug, and Improve operators,
and adds the evaluated artifact back to the search graph. MLEvolve ([Du
et al., 2026](#bib.bib16)) uses Progressive MCGS, augmenting tree search
with graph-based reference edges for cross-branch information flow, a
progressive exploration-to-exploitation schedule, and retrospective
memory for task-specific experience reuse. CORAL ([Qu et al.,
2026](#bib.bib8)) runs multiple autonomous research agents
asynchronously; agents explore and reflect in parallel, communicate
discoveries through shared persistent memory, and use periodic
heartbeat-style interventions to sustain and redirect the search. All
three use Haiku 4.5 as a frozen Scientist together with the shared
Executor, evaluator, tree bookkeeping, and judge. We additionally run
ARTS^(∗) ([Juneja et al., 2026](#bib.bib6)), the test-time-training
variant of ARTS, in which a single reasoning Scientist inspects prior
execution logs, diagnoses whether failures stem from faulty
implementations or weak hypotheses, selects which tree node to expand,
and is periodically fine-tuned with LoRA to distill the search tree into
its weights. ARTS^(∗) uses the Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 backbone of Meld
together with the shared Executor, evaluator, tree bookkeeping, and
judge.

### B.5 Ablation Protocol

##### Variants and budget.

We compare full Meld, Coordinator-only, Scientist-only, and Frozen on
Iterated Prisoner’s Dilemma (PD) and Colonel Blotto (CB), using three
independent seeds per variant. The variants share the task-start policy
initialization, backbone, prompts, sampling settings, Executor, judge,
and tree bookkeeping. They differ only in which optimizer steps are
enabled: full Meld updates both policy levels, Coordinator-only updates
the Coordinator, Scientist-only updates the private Scientist adapters,
and Frozen disables both updates while retaining the same tree-based
search. Each run uses 72 candidate evaluations, comprising two
24-candidate adaptation groups and a final 24-candidate evaluation batch
reserved before the run. The reserved batch is excluded from training.
Timeouts and validation failures consume budget in every variant. Model
and optimization settings follow
Appendix [B.3](#A2.SS3 "B.3 Models, Harness, and Candidate Execution ‣ Appendix B Experimental Details ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery").

##### Adaptation schedule.

The first group, denoted $`b_{\mathrm{pre}}`$, is collected from the
task-start snapshot before any parameter update. Following the
Coordinator-group protocol of
Section [4.3](#S4.SS3 "4.3 Alternating Test-Time Adaptation ‣ 4 Meld: Hierarchical Test-Time Training for Self-Improving Multi-Agent Discovery ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"),
it consists of $`G=8`$ independent forks, each sampling a fresh joint
portfolio for $`N=3`$ research units and executing their Scientists’
responses. The snapshot and behavior policies remain fixed during
collection. Full Meld and Coordinator-only then take one optimizer step
on the Coordinator adapter; Scientist-only and Frozen skip this step.
Validated candidates are committed to the discovery tree in every
variant.

The second group, denoted $`b_{\mathrm{mid}}`$, follows the
Scientist-group protocol. From the resulting snapshot, the current
Coordinator samples one portfolio, which is held fixed across eight
forks. Each Scientist independently samples its response in each fork,
producing another 24 candidate evaluations. Full Meld and Scientist-only
then take one optimizer step on each private Scientist adapter, with
these updates performed in parallel; Coordinator-only and Frozen skip
this step. Validated candidates are again committed to the tree. After
each enabled update, the updated serving adapters are used for
subsequent generation. Full Meld therefore completes one
Coordinator–Scientist adaptation cycle before the final batch is
collected.

##### Post-cycle evaluation.

After the second adaptation group has been processed and all enabled
updates applied, we generate a fresh 24-candidate batch
$`b_{\mathrm{post}}`$ using the resulting policies. This batch consists
of eight independent forks of the post-adaptation snapshot. Each fork
samples a new joint portfolio and fresh responses from all three
Scientists. Thus, $`b_{\mathrm{pre}}`$ and $`b_{\mathrm{post}}`$ use the
same portfolio-resampling scheme, whereas $`b_{\mathrm{mid}}`$ holds one
portfolio fixed for Scientist training.

All policy parameters, the discovery state, and the parent shortlist
remain fixed throughout collection of $`b_{\mathrm{post}}`$. Candidates
and feedback from one evaluation fork are not exposed to the others, and
no optimizer step is taken on this batch. Each variant is evaluated from
the tree produced by its own adaptation groups, so the comparison
includes how the enabled updates shape the subsequent discovery process.

##### Reported statistics.

A valid candidate counts as a threshold hit when its raw
private-evaluator score
(Appendix [B.2](#A2.SS2 "B.2 Private Evaluators ‣ Appendix B Experimental Details ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery"))
is at least $`2.62`$ on PD or $`0.245`$ on CB, and the same thresholds
apply to all variants and seeds. For each seed and each reported batch,
we compute the median raw score over valid candidates and the number of
threshold hits among the 24 attempted evaluations. Failed evaluations
count as non-hits.

### B.7 Protocol and Compute

##### Budget accounting.

Within each task, all methods receive the same nominal scored-candidate
budget, so comparisons are matched by scored candidates rather than
wall-clock time. Every candidate is executed under the task training
timeout and scored by the private evaluator. A candidate that exceeds
the timeout or fails validation consumes one evaluator call, receives
failure utility $`s_{\min}{=}0`$ for the training potential, and is not
added to the derivation tree.

##### Scores and seeds.

Main benchmark results and adaptation ablations are averaged over three
independent seeds.
Table [1](#S6.T1 "Table 1 ‣ 6.2 Main Results ‣ 6 Experiments ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")
reports the mean of each seed’s best held-out score within budget, and
Figure [5](#S6.F5 "Figure 5 ‣ Broad gains across task families. ‣ 6.2 Main Results ‣ 6 Experiments ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery")
aggregates the corresponding anytime best-so-far trajectories.

##### Compute.

Experiments run on four NVIDIA A100 GPUs. Each method run takes
approximately 9 hours, with test-time adaptation updating only the LoRA
blocks described in
Table [3](#A2.T3 "Table 3 ‣ Adaptation. ‣ B.3 Models, Harness, and Candidate Execution ‣ Appendix B Experimental Details ‣ Meld: Recursive Self-Improvement viaHierarchical Coordination for Multi-AgentScientific Discovery").

##### Broader impact.

More capable automated discovery systems can reduce the cost of
exploring large hypothesis spaces and accelerate iterative scientific
and machine-learning experimentation. The same autonomy can also
propagate erroneous hypotheses or be applied in sensitive domains;
practical deployments should therefore retain task-appropriate
evaluation and human oversight.
````
