###### Abstract

Autonomous research seeks sustained model improvements through iterative
experimentation and feedback. LLM agents show promise in automating
machine learning and language-model post-training, but their ability to
sustain multimodal improvement remains unclear. We introduce
MMPostTrainBench, a benchmark spanning eight tasks in image, audio,
video, and joint audio-video understanding and image-grounded software
repair. Agents operate from a common base model within fixed budgets,
using development feedback before independent evaluation of their
submitted models. Evaluation covers target and non-target model
outcomes, iterative model improvement and selection, and research
integrity. Across all eight tasks, 52.1% of model–task means fall below
the base, and evaluated submissions also exhibit non-target regressions.
Model performance does not consistently improve across research
iterations, and agents do not reliably select the best evaluated
candidate for submission; final submissions trail that candidate by up
to 5.38 percentage points. Extending autonomous research from text-only
to multimodal tasks introduces additional sources of error in
perception, cross-modal alignment, and temporal grounding. The observed
regressions and selection gaps highlight the need to balance targeted
improvements with non-target capability preservation and to retain gains
across research iterations. These requirements motivate MMResearch, a
multimodal research framework that connects media-grounded evidence to
hypotheses and interventions, carries findings across rounds through
hierarchical memory, and retains candidates using development
evaluation. Added to existing code-agent runtimes, it improves
submitted-model accuracy by up to 7.75 percentage points for Claude Opus
4.8 with Claude Code and 2.33 points for GPT-5.6-sol with Codex.

## 1 Introduction

A central challenge in autonomous machine-learning research is to
translate experimental feedback into effective model improvements and
accumulate useful knowledge across successive experiments. Research
agents tackle this challenge with code, tools, and feedback for
experiments, language-model post-training, and training-data
research ([Huang et al., 2024](#bib.bib6); [Chan et al.,
2025](#bib.bib1); [Wijk et al., 2024](#bib.bib17); [Lu et al.,
2024](#bib.bib11); [Rank et al., 2026](#bib.bib14); [Meng et al.,
2026](#bib.bib13)). Beyond text-only post-training, agents must connect
evidence in source media with language and other modalities. Clutter,
background sounds, and incomplete descriptions in media collected in the
wild make relevant evidence difficult to isolate. This raises a broader
question: _Can research agents autonomously improve how models see,
hear, and reason about multimodal inputs—and sustain those improvements
as experimentation continues?_

Autonomous multimodal post-training couples three challenges. (1)
Multimodal Error Diagnosis. In visual reasoning, chain-of-thought
prompting can degrade performance on perception-heavy tasks ([Jiang
et al., 2025a](#bib.bib7)). Agents must inspect source media to
distinguish missed or misinterpreted evidence from faulty inference
before choosing an intervention. (2) Data Curation and Modality
Alignment. Selecting visual instructions for vulnerable sample groups
can improve robustness ([Yang et al., 2025b](#bib.bib23)). Agents must
also preserve semantic and temporal alignment: labels should match
observable content, and paired audio-video segments should retain their
timing. (3) Budgeted Multimodal Experimentation. Autonomous
language-model post-training evaluations report early termination
without valid final checkpoints ([Rank et al., 2026](#bib.bib14)).
Multimodal runs must also budget for frame sampling and audio processing
alongside training, candidate evaluation, and final delivery. Effective
research must therefore connect media evidence and alignment decisions
to the improvements ultimately delivered.

We introduce MMPostTrainBench (Multimodal Post-Training Benchmark)¹¹ 1
Benchmark code and evaluation setup:
[https://github.com/sod1010/MMPostTrainBench](https://github.com/sod1010/MMPostTrainBench).,
which evaluates autonomous research on eight tasks spanning image,
audio, video, and joint audio-visual understanding, as well as
multimodal code tasks
(Table [1](#S3.T1 "Table 1 ‣ Autonomous research. ‣ 3.1 Task Definition and Research Process ‣ 3 Method ‣ MMPostTrainBench: Benchmarking Autonomous Research for Multimodal Post-Training");
Figure [2](#S3.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 3 Method ‣ MMPostTrainBench: Benchmarking Autonomous Research for Multimodal Post-Training")).
Task queries specify a target capability and benchmark, such as audio
reasoning on MMAR, and require training pipelines to process the
corresponding media. Under a common resource budget, agents select data
sources, modality mixtures, supervision, and training strategies in
response to development feedback. Harbor-compatible task packages
provide this feedback during research and reserve separate held-out
tests for final evaluation. Target and non-target evaluations assess
whether a targeted update preserves other capabilities.

We assess model outcomes, iterative model improvement, and research
integrity. Across six research-agent configurations on eight tasks,
52.1% of model–task means fall below the base, and submissions also
exhibit non-target regressions.
Figure [1](#S0.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ MMPostTrainBench: Benchmarking Autonomous Research for Multimodal Post-Training")
summarizes final outcomes and best-so-far candidate improvement. Final
submissions trail the best evaluated candidates by up to 5.38 percentage
points across recorded candidate pools. Integrity checks flag suspected
misuse of evaluation data during training. Producing a strong candidate,
selecting it reliably, and retaining other multimodal capabilities thus
remain distinct requirements.

These findings motivate our proposed multimodal research framework,
MMResearch. Building on persistent research orchestration ([Jin et al.,
2026](#bib.bib9)), it augments existing code-agent runtimes with
multimodal evidence reasoning, hierarchical memory, and
evaluation-guided model selection. Observations are anchored to source
media and linked to hypotheses, interventions, and development outcomes;
memory carries successful recipes and failure records across rounds;
model selection retains a candidate for final delivery. These mechanisms
connect diagnosis, accumulated knowledge, and submission decisions
within a seven-step research loop. In the evaluated configurations,
adding MMResearch improves submitted-model accuracy by up to 7.75
percentage points for Claude Opus 4.8 with Claude Code and 2.33 points
for GPT-5.6-sol with Codex.

Our contributions are threefold: (1) We introduce MMPostTrainBench,
which evaluates autonomous post-training across eight multimodal tasks
through model outcomes, iterative model improvement, and research
integrity. (2) We characterize non-target regressions, candidate
degradation, and submission-selection failures in current research
agents. (3) We propose MMResearch, which combines multimodal evidence
reasoning, hierarchical memory, and evaluation-guided model selection to
improve submitted models within existing code-agent runtimes.

## 2 Related Work

#### Autonomous Research Benchmarks.

Autonomous research benchmarks evaluate progress at different levels of
an AI system. Task-level evaluations measure agents’ ability to conduct
ML experiments and solve AI R&D problems ([Huang et al.,
2024](#bib.bib6); [Chan et al., 2025](#bib.bib1); [Wijk et al.,
2024](#bib.bib17)). Other evaluations focus on the supporting harness
and infrastructure ([Wu et al., 2026c](#bib.bib20); [Ding et al.,
2026](#bib.bib3)), or on the agent’s ability to learn from broad goals
and interaction feedback ([Wu et al., 2026b](#bib.bib19); [Shi et al.,
2026](#bib.bib16)). For autonomous post-training, the object of
improvement is the target model: PostTrainBench permits end-to-end
language-model post-training, whereas RSIBench-Data fixes the learning
stack to isolate training-data research ([Rank et al.,
2026](#bib.bib14); [Meng et al., 2026](#bib.bib13)). MMPostTrainBench
extends this setting to multimodal tasks, assessing final target and
non-target performance, iterative candidate quality and submission
choice, and research integrity.

#### Research Agent Harnesses.

Research agent harnesses must organize experiments and make accumulated
evidence useful for subsequent decisions. End-to-end workflows
coordinate idea generation, implementation, and training ([Lu et al.,
2024](#bib.bib11); [Zhang et al., 2026a](#bib.bib25)), while
search-based systems structure the choice of experiments ([Jiang et al.,
2025b](#bib.bib8)). As experimentation continues, preserving and reusing
research state becomes equally important. Hierarchical knowledge
retrieval and persistent hypothesis trees connect earlier findings to
later decisions ([Zhang et al., 2026b](#bib.bib26); [Jin et al.,
2026](#bib.bib9)); discovery replay and reusable environment memory
serve a related role in autonomous exploration ([Zheng et al.,
2026](#bib.bib27); [Zhu et al., 2026](#bib.bib28)). ModularRSI refactors
Terminus-2 into behavioral modules and evolves their source
functions ([Wu et al., 2026a](#bib.bib18)). Building on research
orchestration and knowledge reuse, MMResearch focuses on improving
multimodal models through post-training, connecting multimodal
evidence-based diagnosis with training interventions and model
selection.

## 3 Method

MMPostTrainBench evaluates autonomous multimodal post-training through a
controlled research environment and three complementary evaluation
dimensions
(Figure [2](#S3.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 3 Method ‣ MMPostTrainBench: Benchmarking Autonomous Research for Multimodal Post-Training")).
We describe the task setting and evaluation protocol before introducing
our proposed research framework, MMResearch.

Figure 2: MMPostTrainBench benchmark overview. Agents conduct
post-training from a common base model on eight multimodal tasks within
fixed budgets. To limit evaluation leakage, execution and verification
run in separate containers, research feedback is restricted to
authorized development data, and final tests remain sealed. Submitted
models, candidate trajectories, and audits support evaluation of model
outcomes, iterative model improvement, and research integrity.

### 3.1 Task Definition and Research Process

#### Research task.

A task assigns the agent a capability to improve, a starting model, and
a bounded research environment. Following the artifact-oriented setup of
autonomous post-training and research benchmarks ([Rank et al.,
2026](#bib.bib14); [Meng et al., 2026](#bib.bib13); [Jin et al.,
2026](#bib.bib9)), we represent task $`b`$ as

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
\mathcal{T}_{b}=(q_{b},\theta_{0},\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{adm}}_{b},F_{b}^{\mathrm{dev}},E_{b}^{\mathrm{test}},\mathcal{B}_{b}).
``` |  | (1) |

Here $`q_{b}`$ specifies the target capability and success criterion;
$`\theta_{0}`$ is the common base model;
$`\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{adm}}_{b}`$ specifies permitted training-data
sources and uses; $`F_{b}^{\mathrm{dev}}`$ returns authorized
development feedback; $`E_{b}^{\mathrm{test}}`$ independently evaluates
frozen models; and $`\mathcal{B}_{b}`$ specifies compute allocation and
the research time limit. Research configuration $`a`$ consists of a
frontier model and its execution runtime.

#### Autonomous research.

Within loop $`r`$, the agent chooses interventions from its accumulated
history rather than following a prescribed training recipe. Suppressing
$`a,b,r`$ for clarity, candidate production and feedback can be written
as

|  |  |  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle u_{t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\pi_{a}(q_{b},\mathcal{H}_{<t}),`$ | $`\displaystyle\theta_{t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\operatorname{Execute}(u_{t};\theta_{0},\mathcal{C}_{<t}),`$ |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle f_{t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=F_{b}^{\mathrm{dev}}(\theta_{t}),`$ | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\leq t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{<t}\cup\{(u_{t},\theta_{t},f_{t},\ell_{t})\}.`$ |  | (2) |

An intervention $`u_{t}`$ specifies data construction, a training
recipe, or a model-side operation using permitted prior candidates
$`\mathcal{C}_{<t}`$; $`\ell_{t}`$ records execution diagnostics and
provenance. The initial history contains permitted task materials, and
later observations include only information available during research.
The agent decides how many experiments to attempt and which completed
model $`S_{a,b,r}=\theta^{\mathrm{sub}}_{a,b,r}`$ to submit before the
budget expires.

### 3.2 Research Environment and Protocol

#### Task environment.

Each Harbor-compatible task package specifies the task objective,
designated base model, data-use rules, tool interfaces, and resource
limits. The research environment provides a writable workspace and the
dependencies required for model training and evaluation. Within the
task’s permissions, agents can inspect multimodal media, construct
training examples, modify training code and configurations, execute
experiments, and retain candidate models. Harbor’s separate-verifier
interface and the Docker driver separate research execution from
independent verification.

#### Development feedback.

Agents query $`F_{b}^{\mathrm{dev}}`$ to obtain candidate scores,
permitted evaluation examples, and execution diagnostics. These
observations support error diagnosis and subsequent decisions about data
construction, training configurations, and candidate selection. The
task’s data-use rules determine which materials may serve as training
supervision; access to evaluation examples for diagnosis does not itself
authorize their use for training. Development feedback excludes final
held-out test instances, reference answers, and scores, which remain
withheld during research.

#### Submission and verification.

At the end of research, the selected model’s weights, configuration, and
loading dependencies are frozen and transferred to the independent
verifier. The final evaluator $`E_{b}^{\mathrm{test}}`$ assesses the
base and submitted models using the same fixed held-out test instances,
preprocessing, inference interface, and scoring procedure. This matched
evaluation provides the basis for measuring changes in model
performance. Data provenance, tool traces, and candidate records connect
the submitted model to its research process and support the integrity
checks defined in
Section [3.3](#S3.SS3 "3.3 Evaluation Dimensions ‣ 3 Method ‣ MMPostTrainBench: Benchmarking Autonomous Research for Multimodal Post-Training").

### 3.3 Evaluation Dimensions

We evaluate three complementary dimensions: model outcomes, iterative
model improvement, and research integrity. Let
$`S=\theta^{\mathrm{sub}}_{a,b}`$ denote the submitted model and
$`H_{b}(\theta)\in[0,1]`$ its raw task score: answer accuracy for
understanding tasks and resolved-instance rate for SWE-bench Multimodal.

Model outcomes. We compare submissions with the base on the target task
and two prespecified non-target probes from other categories. Target
change is $`\Delta_{a,b}=100[\widehat{s}_{a,b}-H_{b}(\theta_{0})]`$
percentage points, where $`\widehat{s}_{a,b}`$ is the mean of three loop
scores, with flagged loops reset to the base before averaging.
Non-target changes assess transfer and capability retention. Aggregate
comparisons weight all eight tasks equally. For SWE-bench Multimodal,
Table [2](#S4.T2 "Table 2 ‣ Model outcomes. ‣ 4.2 Main Results ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ MMPostTrainBench: Benchmarking Autonomous Research for Multimodal Post-Training")
reports submitted-model means relative to a base resolved-instance rate
of 2.29%.

Iterative model improvement. We assess iterative model improvement from
two perspectives. (1) Iterative progress: whether further experiments
improve candidate quality or lead to stagnation, degradation, or
fluctuations, as reflected by candidate trajectories evaluated
retrospectively on the same held-out test set. (2) Final selection:
whether the final submission retains the best performance among the
evaluated candidates produced before submission. Let
$`\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{rec}}_{a,b}`$ denote this candidate set.
Submission loss is defined as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
R^{\mathrm{rec}}_{a,b}=100\!\left[\max_{\theta\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{rec}}_{a,b}\cup\{S\}}H_{b}(\theta)-H_{b}(S)\right].
``` |  | (3) |

A positive value indicates that the final submission underperforms the
best evaluated candidate, with the gap measured in percentage points.

Research integrity. Benchmark scores may be inflated by contamination or
misuse of evaluation information. Audit categories cover training-data
contamination, unauthorized workspace or data access, prohibited split
use, and misuse of evaluation information. Retained code, data
provenance, and research trajectories provide evidence for these checks.
Results are summarized by the number of runs flagged for suspected
violations.

### 3.4 MMResearch: Multimodal Research Framework

MMResearch adds a research controller to existing code-agent runtimes,
retaining their native tools and action loops while coordinating
experiments, memory, and model delivery.
Figure [3](#S3.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 3.4 MMResearch: Multimodal Research Framework ‣ 3 Method ‣ MMPostTrainBench: Benchmarking Autonomous Research for Multimodal Post-Training")
shows how hierarchical memory (a) and multimodal evidence (c) support
the seven-step research loop (b), which concludes with model delivery
(d). The evidence-anchor and graph design in (c) specifies how localized
media observations connect to research hypotheses and experimental
outcomes.

![Refer to caption](2610.05398v1/reference_harness.png)

Figure 3: MMResearch framework. MMResearch coordinates a seven-step
research loop (b), using hierarchical memory (a) to guide
experimentation and multimodal evidence (c) to connect observations with
experimental decisions, then freezes the retained candidate for delivery
(d).

Seven-Step Research Loop. Each round turns a research direction into an
evaluated candidate and a decision
(Figure [3](#S3.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 3.4 MMResearch: Multimodal Research Framework ‣ 3 Method ‣ MMPostTrainBench: Benchmarking Autonomous Research for Multimodal Post-Training")b).
Select chooses a data- or model-side direction from prior hypotheses and
failure records. Diagnose inspects authorized multimodal inputs to
investigate the suspected failure. Debate reviews the diagnosis,
intervention, feasibility, and risks. Implement executes the
intervention and saves a candidate. Evaluate screens and confirms
candidates on development data. Record stores configurations, results,
and rationales in hierarchical memory. Decide uses confirmation results
to promote the candidate, refine the approach, or pivot to another
direction; a proxy improvement alone does not justify promotion. A
separate checkpoint tracks the retained model, initially the base.
Before the budget expires, the controller checks required records and
freezes the retained model’s weights and configuration for delivery.

Multimodal Evidence. The agent inspects authorized image, audio, video,
and audio-video inputs through native perception or configured tools. In
the *multimodal evidence graph* design
(Figure [3](#S3.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 3.4 MMResearch: Multimodal Research Framework ‣ 3 Method ‣ MMPostTrainBench: Benchmarking Autonomous Research for Multimodal Post-Training")c),
each *evidence anchor* identifies a source sample, its modality, and a
relevant region or time span. When finer localization is unavailable,
the anchor refers to the whole image or clip. Direct observations are
stored separately from the hypotheses inferred from them. Links connect
observations, hypotheses, training interventions, and
development-evaluation outcomes, making diagnoses traceable to the
underlying media. Diagnose and Debate use the linked observations to
review explanations; Record preserves the evidence and outcome links for
subsequent rounds. Later rounds can follow these links back to the
source media and prior interventions to reassess an earlier diagnosis in
light of development results. Held-out test results remain outside this
research feedback loop.

Hierarchical Memory. MMResearch preserves research context across rounds
in three memory tiers
(Figure [3](#S3.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 3.4 MMResearch: Multimodal Research Framework ‣ 3 Method ‣ MMPostTrainBench: Benchmarking Autonomous Research for Multimodal Post-Training")a).
*Global* maintains the active research plan and a summary of the
retained model. *Core* stores task and evaluation rules and reusable
training recipes. *Archive* indexes hypotheses, outcomes, failures, and
media artifacts. Select retrieves relevant records at the start of a
round, and Record updates them with its results and lessons, allowing
later experiments to build on earlier findings.

## 4 Experiments

### 4.1 Experimental Setup

Tasks and Resources. All loops start from
Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct ([Xu et al., 2025](#bib.bib21)) on the
eight-task suite spanning the Image, Audio, Video, Omni, and Code
domains
(Table [1](#S3.T1 "Table 1 ‣ Autonomous research. ‣ 3.1 Task Definition and Research Process ‣ 3 Method ‣ MMPostTrainBench: Benchmarking Autonomous Research for Multimodal Post-Training")).
Training uses eight GPUs, with a default limit of 24 hours per loop.

Research Agents. We evaluate six research-model/runtime configurations:
GPT-5.6-sol and GPT-5.6-terra with Codex; Claude Opus 5 and Claude Opus
4.8 with Claude Code; Gemini 3.8 Flash with Gemini CLI; and Qwen 3.8
Omni Flash with Qwen Code. These models direct experiments through their
native runtimes, while Qwen3-Omni is the shared model being
post-trained.

Evaluation Protocol. For each model–benchmark pair, we run three
independent loops with fresh histories, the same base model, and the
same budget. Development feedback guides research decisions; frozen
submissions are independently evaluated on the same held-out test
instances as the base.
Table [2](#S4.T2 "Table 2 ‣ Model outcomes. ‣ 4.2 Main Results ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ MMPostTrainBench: Benchmarking Autonomous Research for Multimodal Post-Training")
reports the three-loop mean, with identified audit-triggering loops
reset to the base before averaging. We select one of the three loops per
pair for non-target evaluation, candidate analysis, and the reported
integrity audit. Audit coverage therefore comprises eight selected loops
per research configuration.

## 5 Conclusion

We introduced MMPostTrainBench, a benchmark for autonomous post-training
across image, audio, video, and joint audio-video understanding, as well
as image-grounded software repair. It evaluates model outcomes,
iterative model improvement, and research integrity. The results reveal
unreliable target gains, non-target capability regressions, candidate
degradation and selection failures, and suspected protocol violations.
These findings motivate MMResearch, which combines multimodal evidence
reasoning, hierarchical memory, and evaluation-guided model selection to
support more reliable research. Compared with unaugmented harnesses,
MMResearch improves submission accuracy in seven of eight model–task
comparisons across four modalities. Future work should investigate how
agents can use multimodal evidence to improve target capabilities while
preserving other modalities, and develop research strategies that
transfer across tasks.

#### Development feedback and final evaluation.

Harbor-compatible task packages separate research execution from
verification. Development feedback provides candidate scores, permitted
examples, and execution diagnostics. Examples available for diagnosis
are not thereby authorized as training supervision. Final test
instances, reference answers, and scores remain unavailable to the
research agent. At submission, weights, configuration, and loading
dependencies are frozen. The verifier evaluates base and submitted
models on the same fixed test instances with matched preprocessing,
inference, and scoring procedures.

#### Research configurations.

The six configurations pair GPT-5.6-sol and GPT-5.6-terra with Codex,
Claude Opus 5 and Claude Opus 4.8 with Claude Code, Gemini 3.8 Flash
with Gemini CLI, and Qwen 3.8 Omni Flash with Qwen Code.

#### Scoring and aggregation.

Let $`\widehat{s}_{a,b}`$ be configuration $`a`$’s mean target score
after applying the per-loop audit rule in
Appendix [C](#A3 "Appendix C Integrity Analysis ‣ MMPostTrainBench: Benchmarking Autonomous Research for Multimodal Post-Training"),
and let $`s_{b}^{0}`$ be the base score. For the eight tasks
$`\mathcal{B}`$, the mean task score and mean change are

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
A_{a}=\frac{100}{|\mathcal{B}|}\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\widehat{s}_{a,b},\qquad\overline{\Delta}_{a}=\frac{100}{|\mathcal{B}|}\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}(\widehat{s}_{a,b}-s_{b}^{0}).
``` |  | (4) |

Tasks receive equal weight, and differences are calculated before
rounding. SWE-bench Multimodal uses a 480-item test set with a base
resolved-instance rate of 2.29%. Its three-loop means are included in
the eight-task aggregate. Non-target evaluation and candidate analysis
use one selected loop from the three for each model–benchmark pair.
These analyses and auxiliary comparisons are separate from the target
means.

## Appendix D MMResearch Implementation

MMResearch coordinates experiments within existing code-agent runtimes
while retaining their native tools. It supplies research instructions
and memory, validates round artifacts, and manages delivery. Debate
combines Researcher, Engineer, Critic, and Artifact Analyst reviews,
using independent Claude Code/Codex subagents or sequential reviews in
other adapters. The procedures below expand
Figure [3](#S3.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 3.4 MMResearch: Multimodal Research Framework ‣ 3 Method ‣ MMPostTrainBench: Benchmarking Autonomous Research for Multimodal Post-Training").

### D.1 Seven-Step Controller Procedure

The retained model $`\theta^{\star}`$ starts from the base model. Each
round produces a candidate and a decision without automatically
replacing it. Screening and confirmation belong to Evaluate; promotion
belongs to Decide.

Input: Task contract $`\mathcal{T}`$, base model $`\theta_{0}`$,
development evaluator $`\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{dev}}`$, budget $`B`$

Output: Retained model $`\theta^{\star}`$ and its loading configuration

1 $`\theta^{\star}\leftarrow\theta_{0}`$; initialize or restore
checkpoint and Global/Core/Archive memory

2 while *remaining budget permits another round* do

    3 $`m_{t}\leftarrow`$ read Global and Core; retrieve relevant
Archive records

    4 $`d_{t}\leftarrow\textsc{Select}(\mathcal{T},m_{t})`$

    5 $`(e_{t},h_{t})\leftarrow\textsc{Diagnose}(d_{t})`$
$`\triangleright`$ anchor observations; separate hypotheses

    6 $`p_{t}\leftarrow\textsc{Debate}(e_{t},h_{t},m_{t})`$

    7 $`(\theta_{t},c_{t})\leftarrow\textsc{Implement}(p_{t})`$
$`\triangleright`$ save candidate and configuration

    8
$`s_{t}\leftarrow\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{dev}}^{\mathrm{screen}}(\theta_{t})`$
$`\triangleright`$ Evaluate: screening

    9 $`v_{t}\leftarrow\mathrm{not\ confirmed}`$

    10 if *screening supports a proposed improvement* then

       11
$`v_{t}\leftarrow\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{dev}}^{\mathrm{confirm}}(\theta_{t},\theta^{\star})`$
$`\triangleright`$ Evaluate: confirmation

    12 end if

    13
$`r_{t}\leftarrow\textsc{Record}(e_{t},h_{t},p_{t},\theta_{t},c_{t},s_{t},v_{t})`$

    14 Index round artifacts and lessons in memory

    15 if *records are valid and $`v_{t}`$ confirms improvement* then

       16 $`\theta^{\star}\leftarrow\theta_{t}`$ $`\triangleright`$
Decide: retain the confirmed candidate

    17 else

       18 Retain $`\theta^{\star}`$; choose refinement or a new
direction $`\triangleright`$ Decide

    19 end if

    20 Finalize the decision and checkpoint; refresh Global

21 end while

22 Freeze $`\theta^{\star}`$ and its loading configuration before the
budget expires

23 return *$`\theta^{\star}`$ and its loading configuration*

Algorithm 1 MMResearch seven-step research loop

#### Confirmation and record validation.

Screening provides inexpensive evidence for choosing which directions
warrant further evaluation. Confirmation re-evaluates a proposed
improvement using authorized development data held apart from the
screening feedback; it is distinct from the benchmark’s sealed final
test. Both results are recorded with the candidate they evaluate. The
controller checks for the required round artifacts and confirmation
evidence before accepting a promotion record. A missing record can
trigger a bounded repair request; an execution failure is recorded as a
failure and does not supply a candidate score. Candidate retention
follows development evidence throughout the loop.

### D.2 Memory Organization and Record Structure

The three memory tiers separate a compact working summary from stable
task knowledge and detailed research history. A single checkpoint stores
the dynamic run state, including the current round and retained model.
Global summarizes this checkpoint; a Core entry can point to it without
maintaining a second, independently edited copy.

| Store |  Stored information |  Read and write points |
|----|----|----|
| Global |  Current plan, retained-model summary, and critical lessons. |  Read at startup and Select; refresh after the round decision. |
| Core |  Task rules, evaluator interface, environment facts, and reusable configurations. |  Read the index at Select; load relevant values and add stable knowledge when established. |
| Archive |  Hypotheses, round outcomes, failures, detailed lessons, and media references. |  Retrieve relevant entries during research; index new evidence and outcomes at Record. |
| Checkpoint |  Authoritative round, phase, retained candidate, and progress state. |  Restore on resume; update after Decide and at finalization. |
````
