# Verifier strength does not buy discovery quality: a pre-registered two-domain test of LLM-based automated research, with post-hoc evidence pointing to proposer-pool composition

Journal: rsos

Peter Chen^(1,†)    Ni Zhang^(1,†) and Siyuan Zhang^(1,∗)
Address: ¹Shenyang Institute of Technology, Shenyang, China
Email: <lelandsy28@163.com>

###### Abstract

Automated research pipelines pair an LLM proposer with a verifier on an
implicit bet: discovery quality rises with verification strength. Under
a pre-registered protocol we test this bet in two real-data domains –
job-shop scheduling and vehicular edge offloading on Beijing T-Drive
trajectories – across 100 matched LLM/random pairs per domain and three
verifier tiers. The manipulation binds – a 429-case mutation battery
shows scores rising across tiers – yet the paired LLM-over-random
advantage does not scale with it (A, one-sided $`p{=}0.1115`$; D,
$`p{=}0.9994`$), and in D shrinks under the strict tier (margin contrast
$`-0.040`$, $`p{=}0.0013`$). The two lower gates promote identical
candidates in 100/100 and 99/100 pairs, so the strict order is
untestable when they coincide. Constructively, LLM proposals carry a
real paired advantage over random controls, but a post-hoc union-pool
analysis shows the fastest path to better discovery is widening the
pool, not tightening the gate: mixing the two pools adds $`+1.52`$
holdout-value units in domain A (one-sided Wilcoxon, $`n{=}100`$,
$`p{=}0.0002`$) – evidence that pool composition, not verification
strength, is the lever. Criteria, ledgers and scripts are archived on
Zenodo (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23308866).

## 1 Introduction

Automated scientific discovery pipelines built on large language models
\[[1](#bib.bib1)\] increasingly pair an LLM proposer with an automated
verifier: the model generates candidate decision rules, heuristics, or
hypotheses; a programmatic verifier screens them against constraints;
the surviving candidate is scored on held-out data. The scaling behavior
of such pipelines is usually discussed in terms of proposer capability.
There is, however, a second lever that is cheaper to turn and is widely
assumed to monotonically improve outcomes: _verification strength_. If
screening is what stands between noisy proposals and discovery quality,
then a stricter verifier – more constraints, plus adversarial stress
testing – should buy more quality. This assumption shapes how
practitioners build such systems: add checks, add stress tests, trust
the filter.

This paper tests the assumption directly rather than assuming it. We
decompose a minimal automated-research pipeline into a proposer $`P`$
(an LLM under iterative verifier feedback) and a verifier $`V`$, and ask
a causal question: _holding $`P`$ fixed, does increasing the strength of
$`V`$ improve the quality of what is discovered?_ Verification strength
is operationalized as three frozen tiers: S⁻ (minimal screening), S⁰
(the full constraint verifier), and S⁺ (S⁰ plus a worst-case stress
test). Discovery quality is the one-shot held-out value of the promoted
rule under a frozen scoring function.

Answering this credibly requires three design commitments. First,
_pre-registration_: the criteria, the protocol, and the analysis were
frozen (v1.0) before any main-domain data were collected, with
changelogs disclosing every deviation, including a manipulation-check
population disclosure (v1.2) made before unblinding. Second, a
_manipulation check before the main data_: a mutation battery must
establish that the three tiers genuinely differ in what they filter
(E1-D2); a main experiment with an ineffective manipulation is
uninterpretable. Third, a _matched control arm_: every LLM session is
paired with a random-proposal session of equal size, so that quality is
measured as the paired advantage of structured proposals over random
ones, not as an absolute score.

We ran 100 such pairs in each of two real-data domains: job-shop
scheduling on instances derived from real records (domain A), and
vehicular edge offloading on Beijing T-Drive taxi trajectories
\[[2](#bib.bib2)\] (domain D). The proposer is a small open-weights
model (Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct \[[3](#bib.bib3)\]) running fully offline
on CPU – a deliberately modest proposer, so that any conclusion applies
to the pipeline structure rather than to frontier-model capability, and
so that the whole study is reproducible on commodity hardware.

The main hypothesis fails in both domains. The manipulation check passes
(the 429-case mutation battery yields scores that strictly increase
across tiers), yet the paired quality advantage of LLM proposals over
random ones does not increase with verifier strength: it is
statistically indistinguishable across tiers in domain A and _decreases_
under the strict tier in domain D. The failure is informative rather
than null. Three additional analyses give it structure: (i) the
intermediate feasibility gate is _inert_ – in 100/100 and 99/100 pairs
S⁻ and S⁰ promote the identical candidate, because an LLM session’s
proposals are near-determinately all-feasible or all-infeasible; (ii)
the LLM pool has a large _coverage gap_ – the best of twenty random
proposals beats the best of twenty LLM proposals in roughly half the
pairs, and adding the random pool would have significantly improved
held-out quality in domain A ($`+1.52`$, $`p{=}0.0002`$), a gain that
stricter verification failed to deliver; (iii) a post-hoc _tension
gradient_ shows the value of the stress-test tier crossing zero with
domain tension – negative when the deployment regime leaves constraints
slack, peaking at medium utilization. Together these suggest a
separation result: verifier tiers that separate cleanly on the mutation
battery do not buy discovery quality; the post-hoc evidence points
instead to proposer-pool composition as the lever, and that evidence is
now under pre-registered test as an intervention.

We therefore close with two pre-registered follow-ups that treat this
conclusion as an experimental hypothesis: a diversity dose–response
experiment (pool composition manipulated at fixed verifier) and a
tension transfer experiment (utilization manipulated at fixed verifier).
All criteria, ledgers, and seed-to-figure reproduction scripts are
archived on Zenodo (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23308866).

## 2 Related work

#### Automated research systems

End-to-end systems such as The AI Scientist \[[1](#bib.bib1)\] chain an
LLM proposer with automated execution and evaluation, and evaluate the
output with human or AI review; domain pipelines have produced
autonomous chemistry laboratories \[[4](#bib.bib4)\] and mathematical
conjectures via program search \[[5](#bib.bib5)\], and a large-scale
human study finds LLM research ideas rated as novel while LLM
self-evaluation remains unreliable \[[6](#bib.bib6)\], and a recent
survey argues the field’s bottleneck is shifting from task completion to
verifying what agents claim \[[7](#bib.bib7)\]. Our contribution is
orthogonal: we hold the proposer fixed and manipulate the verifier,
under pre-registration, to isolate the causal contribution of
verification strength – making that proposed bottleneck directly
measurable. The proposer-heavy line treats filtering as plumbing; we
show the plumbing is where a central assumption fails.

#### LLM-as-judge and programmatic verifiers

The alignment literature studies whether LLM judges match human
preferences \[[8](#bib.bib8)\]. Our verifiers are programmatic and exact
(constraint checkers, stress tests), removing judge-noise as an
explanation: when quality fails to track verification strength here, it
is not because the verifier is noisy. Selection pipelines that assume
stronger filtering buys quality – outcome and process verifiers
\[[9](#bib.bib9), [10](#bib.bib10)\], self-consistency
\[[11](#bib.bib11)\], and verbal self-refinement \[[12](#bib.bib12),
[13](#bib.bib13)\] – inherit precisely this assumption, and resampling
against imperfect verifiers is already known to be capped by false
positives \[[14](#bib.bib14)\]; our randomized evidence extends that
concern from pass-rate contamination to discovery quality. The
generator–verifier gap literature \[[15](#bib.bib15)\] shows pass-rate
gaps between generation and verification; we show a complementary gap –
between verification strength and downstream quality. Two concurrent
audits in adjacent domains reinforce this audit premise: agents
answering scientific questions often ignore the supplied evidence
\[[16](#bib.bib16)\], and LLM-generated kernels that pass numerical
checks can fail under scale shift \[[17](#bib.bib17)\]. Neither
manipulates verifier strength under pre-registration or measures
discovery quality, which is our focus.

#### Pool composition and diversity

Quality-diversity methods \[[18](#bib.bib18), [19](#bib.bib19)\],
descendants of novelty search \[[20](#bib.bib20)\], make archive
diversity the explicit optimization target; repeated-sampling results
\[[15](#bib.bib15)\] show coverage growing steeply with draw count.
Adjacent to our negative result, a budget-sensitive scoring study finds
no marginal discovery value from LLM proposers over selection alone in a
drug-candidate pipeline \[[21](#bib.bib21)\]. Our union-pool analysis
is, to our knowledge, the first to quantify the LLM-vs-random coverage
gap inside a verifier-bound discovery pipeline, and the first to show it
widening under stricter verification.

#### Pre-registration and negative results

Our protocol follows clinical-trial norms transplanted to ML evaluation:
frozen criteria with changelogs, a manipulation check gate before the
main data, and a negative clause committing to publication of failure.
These norms respond to replication failures across disciplines
\[[22](#bib.bib22), [23](#bib.bib23)\] and to reproducibility gaps
specific to AI and ML-based science \[[24](#bib.bib24),
[25](#bib.bib25), [26](#bib.bib26)\]. The S$`{}^{0}\equiv`$S⁻ degeneracy
adds a criterion-reachability lesson to this practice: check that a
criterion can be satisfied under plausible degenerate outcomes before
freezing it.

## 3 Framework and frozen criteria

### 3.1 Proposer–verifier decomposition

An automated-research pipeline is a proposer $`P`$ (an LLM under
iterative feedback, 20 proposals per session) and a verifier $`V`$
mapping a candidate to {pass, fail} plus a frozen score. Discovery
quality on a held-out instance set $`H`$ is the holdout value of the
promoted candidate
$`\arg\max_{c:\,V(c)=\mathrm{pass}}\mathrm{train}(c)`$, evaluated
exactly once on $`H`$ (HoldoutGate: one-shot semantics, no
re-evaluation). We write $`J(c)`$ for the frozen holdout score of
candidate $`c`$ and $`\overline{J}(G)`$ for its mean over a group $`G`$
of promoted rules (used in

$`\Delta(\cdot)`$ denotes the paired LLM-over-random margin of a tier,
and $`\mathrm{MS}(\cdot)`$ the mutation score of a tier
(Section [4](#S4 "4 Experimental setup ‣ Verifier strength does not buy discovery quality: a pre-registered two-domain test of LLM-based automated research, with post-hoc evidence pointing to proposer-pool composition")).

### 3.2 Three verifier tiers

- •
  S⁻ (weak): minimal screening. Domain A promotes any rule that executes
  (INFEASIBLE outcomes score the frozen floor $`-10^{9}`$); domain D
  promotes any rule without deadline violations.
- •
  S⁰ (standard): the full frozen constraint verifier (A:
  feasibility-gated scoring; D: safety isolation, per-task deadline,
  per-task energy allowance, per-RSU capacity).
- •
  S⁺ (strict): S⁰ plus a worst-case stress test (A: non-degeneracy of
  promoted weights; D: re-verification with every task’s channel quality
  lowered to the instance minimum).

### 3.3 Frozen criteria (v1.0)

E1-D1 (main): the paired advantage of the LLM arm over the random
control, $`\Delta(\cdot)`$, satisfies
$`\Delta(\mathrm{S}^{+})>\Delta(\mathrm{S}^{0})>\Delta(\mathrm{S}^{-})`$
with one-sided Wilcoxon $`p<0.05`$ for S⁺ vs. S⁻, in both main domains
(domain A mandatory). E1-D2 (manipulation validity): strictly ordered
mutation scores
$`\mathrm{MS}(\mathrm{S}^{-})<\mathrm{MS}(\mathrm{S}^{0})<\mathrm{MS}(\mathrm{S}^{+})`$
in both domains. Negative clause: if either criterion fails, the
manipulation is recorded as ineffective or the main hypothesis as
falsified, and all outcomes are reported as such.

## 4 Experimental setup

### 4.1 Domains and protocol

Domain A (scheduling). Priority rules over 7 normalized numerical
features of the job-shop instance (frozen feature list in the archived
protocol). Instances are bootstrap resamples of a real production-order
pool from the prior suture experiments of this program: all listed
orders in a fixed due window (2026-08-15 to 2026-09-02), deduplicated
across 10 business fields – 1,791 rows collapse to 154 unique orders,
because the same batch was re-imported under different run identifiers.
Pair $`i`$ (seed $`1000{+}i`$) draws a 60-order train instance without
replacement and a disjoint 60-order holdout instance from the remaining
94 orders, redrawing if the baseline schedule is infeasible or exceeds
40 days. The pool is small and pairs are strongly correlated by
construction, so domain-A claims are limited to within-distribution
perturbation robustness – not across-factory generalization. Domain D
(offloading). Offloading-ratio rules over 8 normalized task features;
instances built from Beijing T-Drive taxi trajectories
\[[2](#bib.bib2)\] around 3 Road Side Units (RSUs) by the frozen
instance generator of the archived pipeline: pair $`i`$ uses a frozen
training-instance seed ($`9000{+}i`$) and a disjoint holdout-instance
seed ($`10000{+}i`$). T-Drive is a public research dataset released by
Microsoft Research; trajectories were aggregated into per-pair task
arrival instances at frozen timestamps, with no identifier or
trip-purpose information retained (Section *Ethics*). Holdout instances
for both domains are held out by construction: frozen at protocol time,
never scored during training, and evaluated exactly once per promoted
rule.

#### Session and gating design.

Each domain runs 100 matched pairs. Per pair: _one_ fresh LLM session
(20 iterative proposals under standard-tier S⁰ prompt and verifier
feedback) and one random control (20 uniform weight vectors over the
same rule family, per-pair seed). The _same_ 20-proposal pool is then
re-gated independently by each verifier tier – S⁻, S⁰, S⁺ – and each
tier promotes the passing candidate with the best training value; the
random pool is gated identically. Tiers therefore differ _only_ in the
filter applied to a fixed proposal pool, which is what makes the
manipulation a clean test of verification strength. Promotion gaps (an
arm has no passing candidate in a tier) occurred in 0/100 pairs in
domain A and in 3 pairs in domain D; those 3 pairs are excluded from
analyses involving the affected arm (n=99/97 where applicable, stated
per analysis).

1: draw frozen train instance and holdout instance (frozen per-pair
seeds)

2: _LLM arm_: run one fresh session of 20 iterative proposals under the
S⁰ prompt and verifier feedback

3: _random arm_: draw 20 proposals from the frozen random distribution
(per-pair seed, same budget)

4: for each tier
$`T\in\{\mathrm{S}^{-},\mathrm{S}^{0},\mathrm{S}^{+}\}`$ do

5:   gate _both_ fixed proposal pools by tier $`T`$

6:   promote the passing candidate with the best training value

7: end for

8: evaluate each promoted rule on the holdout instance exactly once
(HoldoutGate: one-shot semantics)

9: return per-tier promotions and holdout values

Algorithm 1 Tier-1 paired session with three-tier re-gating (per pair)

#### Proposer and compute.

Proposer: Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct \[[3](#bib.bib3)\] (0.49B parameters,
32K context), served locally through Hugging Face transformers on
PyTorch with CPU inference, fully offline (hub and local-restore
environment flags set). Decoding is frozen: sampling with temperature
$`0.8`$, top-$`p`$ $`0.9`$; up to 220 new tokens per proposal in domain
A (Chinese prompt template with two fixed few-shot examples) and 180 in
domain D (template with per-category verifier feedback); sampling is not
seeded, so the unit of replication is the 20-proposal session, and the
archived ledgers record every proposal verbatim. The frozen prompt and
generation configuration are reproduced verbatim in the archived
protocol and ledgers. All experiments ran offline on a consumer laptop
CPU (no GPU); per-session LLM wall-clock is minutes, and domain D is
dominated by trajectory simulation (on the order of half a day for the
full 100-pair run).

### 4.2 Manipulation battery (E1-D2)

Domain A: 119 mutants (100 uniform random over the frozen 7-dim family,
19 structured). Domain D: 310 mutants (300 random rule templates, 4
extreme heuristics, 6 structured mutants matched to the violation mix
observed in a prior instrumented ledger: safety/energy/capacity entries
vs. deadline). Population disclosed before unblinding (v1.2).

Per-tier post-hoc paired tests size the LLM-vs-random advantage directly
(one-sided Wilcoxon, LLM $`>`$ random; post-hoc, not part of the frozen

for the reverse ordering, and the post-hoc margin contrast below is
significantly negative (two-sided $`p{=}0.0013`$). The main hypothesis
is falsified under the frozen criteria, and the falsification is not a
plain null: in domain D, stricter active verification measurably reduced
the LLM-over-random advantage.

Two floor-contaminated pairs in domain A (an INFEASIBLE rule’s value is
floored at $`-10^{9}`$ in both training and holdout; in domain A the
floor is a _scored outcome_ – an executing rule whose schedule is
infeasible – so both gated tiers can promote it) are excluded in a
sensitivity analysis re-run by floor_sensitivity.py: on the $`n{=}98`$
floor-free pairs, $`\Delta(\mathrm{S}^{-}){=}{+}0.367`$,
$`\Delta(\mathrm{S}^{+}){=}{+}0.469`$, and the one-sided Wilcoxon $`p`$

the raw mean – leaving $`\Delta{=}{+}0.28`$ while the SD is

the signed-rank statistic because $`\mathrm{S}^{+}{=}\mathrm{S}^{-}`$
within each floor pair. Excluding them moves the means
($`+0.367/+0.469`$) but not the test ($`0.1115`$). No domain-D pair
involves the floor.

### 5.3 The feasibility gate is inert

| domain    | identical | diff pairs | S⁺ wins | mean diff  |
| --------- | --------- | ---------- | ------- | ---------- |
| A (n=100) | 100       | 14         | 10      | $`+0.714`$ |
| D (n=100) | 99        | 23         | 1       | $`-0.066`$ |

Table 5: Tier degeneracy and stress-test behavior. “identical”: pairs
where S⁻ and S⁰ promote the same candidate. “diff pairs”: pairs where S⁺
and S⁰ promote different candidates, with S⁺ wins and mean holdout
difference among those pairs.

In 100/100 pairs (A) and 99/100 (D), S⁻ and S⁰ promoted the _identical_
candidate: proposals within an LLM session are near-determinately
all-feasible or all-infeasible, so an intermediate gate almost never
catches an intermediate candidate
(Table [5](#S5.T5 "Table 5 ‣ 5.3 The feasibility gate is inert ‣ 5 Results ‣ Verifier strength does not buy discovery quality: a pre-registered two-domain test of LLM-based automated research, with post-hoc evidence pointing to proposer-pool composition")).
The mechanism is exact in domain A: an executing-but-infeasible
candidate scores the frozen training floor $`-10^{9}`$, which is never
the session maximum unless _every_ candidate in the session fails; under
verifier feedback no session was all-infeasible (0 promotion gaps in 100
pairs, both arms and all tiers), so the best training-value candidate is
always S⁰-feasible and both gates promote it. A session with no
S⁰-passing candidate would leave S⁰ without a promotion and break the
degeneracy by construction; the three domain-D promotion gaps are of
this kind. Two consequences: (i) the frozen strict order
S$`{}^{+}>`$S$`{}^{0}>`$S⁻ is structurally unsatisfiable whenever
S$`{}^{0}\equiv`$S⁻ (a criterion-reachability lesson for
pre-registration practice); (ii) in this regime the effect of
verification strength is carried entirely by the stress-test tier S⁺ –
and that effect is positive in domain A (S⁺ wins 10 of its 14 divergent
pairs) but sharply negative in domain D (1 of 23). A concrete domain-D
failure mode: in one pair the LLM session passed 5 of 20 proposals under
S⁰, whose best candidate scored $`0.524`$ on holdout; the S⁺ stress test
(worst-case channel floor) killed two of the five passing candidates and
forced promotion of the second-best, which violated a constraint on the
holdout instance and scored $`0.0`$ – a $`-0.52`$ swing produced purely
by the stress test rejecting the candidate that would have generalized.
This is the false-negative mechanism behind the domain-D margin
shrinkage: in a tense domain, the stress test removes
correct-but-fragile-on-paper candidates and the remaining pool is too
thin to absorb the loss.

### 5.4 The proposer pool has a large coverage gap

Because each arm’s tier promotion is the best passing candidate by train
value, the promotion of the union pool (LLM 20 + random 20 proposals) is
the better of the two arm promotions, and its holdout value is already
known – the analysis is a zero-new-inference derivation (exploratory,
post-hoc). Three findings:

1.  1.  The union-pool promotion comes from the _random_ arm in 48% of
        pairs (A) and 46% (D) at S⁰: the best of twenty LLM proposals is
        beaten by the best of twenty random draws about half the time.
2.  2.  Under S⁺ (domain D) the random arm wins 60% of pairs: the LLM
        coverage gap _widens_ exactly where verification is strictest.
3.  3.  In domain A, adding the random pool to the LLM pool would have
        _improved_ holdout quality: $`+1.52`$ in domain-A holdout-value
        units (one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank on per-pair holdout
        differences, $`n{=}100`$, $`p{=}0.0002`$) – a gain that stricter
        verification failed to deliver. The same derivation at the strict
        tier gives $`+1.44`$ ($`p{=}0.0006`$), and in domain D the merge
        effect is null-to-negative ($`-0.036`$/$`-0.041`$/$`-0.035`$ across
        tiers, $`p{=}0.27`$/$`0.32`$/$`0.07`$): the pool lever has domain
        structure, which is why it is now the object of a pre-registered
        intervention (E2) rather than a settled claim. These are _derived_
        statistics: the union-pool promotion is computed from the two arm
        promotions without new inference, and the union selection itself is
        realizable (best training value over the merged pool) – what is
        post-hoc is our attention to it, not its implementability.

## 6 Discussion

#### A separation result

The combination of E1-D2 passing and E1-D1 failing is the paper’s
central finding, and it is a separation result: verification strength
filters synthetic bad candidates – the mutation battery establishes that
the tiers are real, ordered treatments – while buying no measurable
discovery quality on LLM proposals. The three analyses of Section 5
explain why. First, the feasibility gate is inert because proposals
within an LLM session are strongly correlated – the model either
understands the interface or it does not, and within a session it almost
always does one or the other consistently. Intermediate constraints
therefore almost never catch intermediate candidates, and S⁰ degenerates
to S⁻. Second, the coverage gap means the marginal value of verification
is bounded by what the proposer pool contains: a filter, however strict,
cannot promote a candidate that was never proposed, and in these domains
the best random draw beats the best LLM draw about half the time. Third,
the tension gradient shows that even the one tier with a real effect
(S⁺) pays only when the deployment regime makes its stress test bite; in
a slack regime the stress test is a source of false negatives, not
quality.

#### Implications for AI-scientist pipelines

The results redirect effort. The common escalation – more constraints,
stricter tests, more adversarial screening – targets the wrong
bottleneck in this regime: the feasibility gate is already inert, and
the stress test helps only in tense regimes. The first lever our
evidence points to is _pool composition_: diversity of generation
(temperature, mixed LLM and random pools, multiple proposer models),
because the union-pool analysis shows random proposals would have
improved the merged promotion in domain A. We state the status of that
number precisely: the union selection rule (best training value over the
merged pool) is realizable without any holdout knowledge, and the
$`+1.52`$ is the holdout estimate of exactly that realizable policy,
computed post-hoc from already-evaluated arm promotions under one-shot
semantics. What it lacks is pre-registration as an intervention – which
is precisely what the pre-registered E2 follow-up now supplies.
Verification budget should follow deployment tension: strict tiers where
constraints bind, cheap tiers where they do not.

#### On the negative result

We report the main hypothesis as falsified under frozen criteria, with
the manipulation check passing – the cleanest null this design can
produce: the treatment is real, the effect is absent (and in domain D,
inverted under the strict tier). The exploratory findings (per-tier
margin tests, pool gap, tension gradient) are labelled as such and were
not part of the frozen criteria; they motivate, but do not confirm, the
pre-registered follow-ups.

#### Pre-registered follow-ups

\(1\) _Diversity dose–response:_ hold the verifier fixed at S⁰,
manipulate pool composition (temperature ladder, mixed LLM+random pools,
multi-model), frozen criterion: monotone holdout improvement in domain A
with a domain-D replication contrast. (2) _Tension transfer:_ manipulate
capacity utilization at fixed tier, frozen criterion: sign change of the
S⁺ group contrast near medium utilization, pre-registered before the
next unblinding. Criterion reachability is checked before freezing
(Section [5.3](#S5.SS3 "5.3 The feasibility gate is inert ‣ 5 Results ‣ Verifier strength does not buy discovery quality: a pre-registered two-domain test of LLM-based automated research, with post-hoc evidence pointing to proposer-pool composition")
lesson): a criterion that is structurally unsatisfiable under a
plausible degenerate outcome – here S$`{}^{0}\equiv`$S⁻ – cannot
discriminate hypothesis from design.

## 7 Limitations

One proposer model (Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct); two domains; simulated
instances derived from real trajectories; the tension gradient varies
task density jointly with utilization (composition confound in the
declining right branch); the mix-pool analysis is exploratory (post-hoc)
and the union-pool promotion is derived from already-evaluated arm
promotions, not a pre-registered intervention; the v1.0 strict-order
criterion was structurally unsatisfiable under S$`{}^{0}\equiv`$S⁻,
reported as a pre-registration design lesson. The random-proposal arm is
the only baseline: for the question _does verifier strength buy quality
at a fixed proposer_ this is the appropriate control (any other proposer
would confound pool composition with verification strength), but it
means the paper makes no claim about absolute rule quality relative to
human-designed or classical heuristics; the domain-D mutation battery
separates the tiers only weakly (Section 5.1), so domain-A evidence
carries the manipulation claim; and all findings are established in two
domains, with the cross-domain generalization of the pool-composition
lever left to the pre-registered follow-ups. Three further scope limits:
proposals within an LLM session are strongly correlated, so the
effective sample size of the 100-pair comparisons is smaller than the
pair count suggests; the random arm spans only the frozen uniform weight
family, so the coverage-gap reading does not extend to other proposal
distributions; and the study is deliberately CPU-bound on commodity
hardware, which limits the scale – though not the structure – of what
was evaluated.

## 8 Conclusion

Under frozen criteria across two real-data domains, verification
strength filters bad candidates on the mutation battery but does not buy
discovery quality on LLM proposals – and in domain D it measurably
_shrinks_ the LLM-over-random advantage. The post-hoc evidence
identifies the composition of the proposer pool as the more promising
lever, now under pre-registered interventional test, and the value of
strictness is modulated by deployment tension. A pre-registration design
lesson is recorded: criteria must be checked for reachability under
plausible degenerate outcomes (here S$`{}^{0}\equiv`$S⁻) before
freezing. All ledgers, frozen criteria, mutation batteries, and
reproduction scripts are archived on Zenodo (DOI:
10.5281/zenodo.23308866).

\ethics

This study uses only the public, de-identified T-Drive trajectory
dataset; no personally identifying information, trip-purpose, or
vehicle-operator data are retained in the derived instances, and no new
human-subject data were collected. Automated research pipelines that act
on real infrastructure domains can propose rules whose deployment has
safety and energy consequences; our one-shot holdout semantics and
frozen verifiers are a partial mitigation, and we deploy no discovered
rule. Negative results in automated-research evaluation can be misread
as endorsements of lax verification; we emphasize the opposite reading –
verification is necessary but its marginal value is regime-dependent,
and pool composition deserves at least equal design attention.
