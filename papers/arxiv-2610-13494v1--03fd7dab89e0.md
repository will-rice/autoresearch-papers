###### Abstract

Existing LLM-based agent approaches for long-horizon machine learning
engineering (MLE) often use context engineering, implemented through
custom harnesses that manage tools, skills, and accumulated experience.
However, we find that as single-session execution lengthens, the agent’s
reasoning intensity declines alongside slowing progress, summarized as
_long-horizon deceleration_, and the benefits of these custom harnesses
vanish as the underlying model improves, summarized as _harness
staleness_. These findings suggest that their benefits may not persist
across versions, which motivates a workflow-level approach in which
agents build on one another’s work across sessions. We model these
alternating agent sessions as a context-reset semi-Markov decision
process (CR-SMDP) and establish sufficient conditions for alternation to
exceed single-agent reference limits, derive an explicit
experiment-budget bound, and characterize when session caps preserve
this advantage. Guided by this analysis, we introduce Humanize MLE
Agents (HMA), which alternates two agents on a shared workspace, limits
each session to at most $`k`$ experiments and resets the context between
sessions, without additional task-specific harness or prior knowledge
supplements. Across 75 Kaggle tasks in MLE-bench, HMA reaches a
_state-of-the-art_ any-medal rate of 78.2%, exceeding the strongest
open-source baseline by $`8.0`$ points with a quarter of its time
budget. Moreover, among six configurations spanning five heterogeneous
agent pairs, HMA improves any-medal rate over the constituent baselines
by $`6.4`$ points on average, demonstrating cross-pair generality. A
supplementary case on kernel optimization reaches 878 cycles, within
1.6% of the community leaderboard leader, demonstrating applicability
beyond MLE.

## 1 Introduction

LLM-based agents tackle machine learning engineering (MLE) through
repeated experiments: writing training code, running it, and evaluating
candidate solutions ([Chan et al., 2025](#bib.bib7); [Toledo et al.,
2025](#bib.bib14); [Karpathy, 2026](#bib.bib33)). For long-horizon
tasks, producing a working solution is only the beginning: the agent
must keep finding ways to improve it. As further gains become harder to
find, the agent is expected to spend more tokens reasoning about the
limitations of the current solution and deciding the next action. In our
experiment across six models, however, mean output tokens per response—a
proxy for reasoning intensity—decline over time as score gains diminish
(Figure [2](#S2.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 2 Motivation: Limits of Single-Agent Exploration ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")).
We refer to this phenomenon as _long-horizon deceleration_ and analyze
its implications for prior agent design.

Existing approaches often seek to sustain improvement through context
engineering, supplying Markdown instructions, skills, and accumulated
knowledge to guide an agent’s next steps ([Zhu et al.,
2026](#bib.bib19); [Du et al., 2026](#bib.bib9); [Karpathy,
2026](#bib.bib33); [Rajasekaran et al., 2025](#bib.bib36)). However,
guidance that helps one model need not help its successor. For example,
OpenAI recommends rewriting AGENTS.md, skills, and task prompts when
upgrading models ([Provencher, 2026](#bib.bib35)), while Anthropic
advises removing instructions for checks that newer models can already
perform on their own ([Anthropic, 2026c](#bib.bib29)). As model
capabilities improve, guidance that once compensated for their
limitations can thus become redundant or counterproductive. We observe a
related pattern in our comparison of three custom MLE harnesses—systems
that manage a model’s tools, context, and execution loop ([Zhu et al.,
2026](#bib.bib19); [Du et al., 2026](#bib.bib9); [Zhao et al.,
2026](#bib.bib18)). Nevertheless, our comparison shows that the relative
benefits of specialized harnesses diminish or reverse as the base model
improves, even under longer execution budgets
(Table [2](#S2.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 2 Motivation: Limits of Single-Agent Exploration ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")).
We refer to this decline in relative performance as _harness staleness_,
which indicates the limitations of custom harness design.

These limitations motivate workflow-level coordination across _agent
sessions_, each defined as an agent’s execution from context
initialization to termination. In MLE, code, trained models, and
evaluated candidates persist in a shared workspace, allowing successive
agents to build on prior work ([Zhao et al., 2026](#bib.bib18); [Young,
2025](#bib.bib37)). This persistence enables complementary roles: the
agent that discovers a useful candidate need not be the one best suited
to refine it, and subsequent changes can provide a new starting point
when the first agent returns. We therefore ask: _Can structured
alternation convert complementary agent capabilities into a consistent
advantage over the best single agent under a matched budget?_

transfer of harness improvements across model versions. These

individual model–harness system.

#### Long-horizon deceleration.

We examine six single-session configurations under /goal mode.

#### Harness staleness.

To counter diminishing single-agent exploration, existing harnesses
introduce planning, review, and context engineering ([Young,
2025](#bib.bib37); [Rajasekaran et al., 2025](#bib.bib36)). With
task-specific cold-start knowledge disabled for fairness, our comparison
of recent MLE harnesses ([Zhu et al., 2026](#bib.bib19); [Du et al.,
2026](#bib.bib9); [Zhao et al., 2026](#bib.bib18)) reveals _harness
staleness_: their benefits relative to the official harness diminish as
the backbone improves
(Table [2](#S2.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 2 Motivation: Limits of Single-Agent Exploration ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")).
The stronger performance of the official harness is consistent with a
possible benefit from model–harness alignment acquired during training.
This motivates treating each agent as an integrated model–harness system
and studying how agents from different providers collaborate through
workflow-level coordination. We present detailed configurations and
results in

### 3.1 HMA Preliminaries

#### Workflow policy and task state.

A multi-agent workflow that alternates between agent sessions is
governed by a fixed _policy over options_ $`\mu`$ ([Sutton et al.,
1999](#bib.bib1)). Without loss of generality, we consider two agents,
$`A`$ and $`B`$, working on a shared task. The state $`x=(W,E,c^{(u)})`$
comprises the shared workspace $`W`$, retained result $`E`$, and context
$`c^{(u)}`$ of the active agent $`u\in\{A,B\}`$. Session $`i`$ of the
workflow, executed by agent $`u`$, is modeled as an option
$`o_{i}^{(u)}=(\mathcal{I}_{i}^{(u)},\pi_{i}^{(u)},\beta_{i}^{(u)})`$,
with initiation set
$`\mathcal{I}_{i}^{(u)}:=\{x:\mu(o_{i}^{(u)}\mid x)>0\}`$, internal
policy $`\pi_{i}^{(u)}`$, and termination rule $`\beta_{i}^{(u)}`$.
Termination creates an _option boundary_, where $`\mu`$ selects the next
option.

#### Task initialization and result quality.

For a general MLE task, $`N`$ is the total experiment budget of the
workflow; each primitive step completes one experiment and updates
$`(W,E)`$ within an option. The initial state
$`x_{0}=(W_{0},E_{0},\emptyset)\sim\nu_{0}`$ is drawn from the
task-state distribution $`\nu_{0}`$, with workspace $`W_{0}`$, empty
result $`E_{0}`$, and empty context. Let $`E_{n}`$ be the result
retained after $`n`$ experiments, with normalized quality
$`q(x):=q(E)\in[0,1]`$. Expected quality and target-success probability
are $`\operatorname{Perf}_{\mu}(N):=\mathbb{E}_{\mu,\nu_{0}}q(E_{N})`$
and $`p_{\mu}(N;\tau):=\Pr\nolimits_{\mu,\nu_{0}}(q(E_{N})\geq\tau)`$
for $`\tau\in[0,1]`$.

#### Workflow trajectory, rewards, and values.

The trajectory is
$`(x_{0},o_{0}^{(u_{0})},x_{1},o_{1}^{(u_{1})},\ldots)`$, where session
$`i`$ starts at $`x_{i}`$ and selects
$`o_{i}^{(u_{i})}\sim\mu(\cdot\mid x_{i})`$ with $`u_{i}\in\{A,B\}`$.
The option kernel is
$`P^{(u)}(x,x^{\prime}):=\Pr\nolimits(x_{i+1}=x^{\prime}\mid x_{i}=x,u_{i}=u)`$.
With a discount factor $`\gamma=1`$ and reward
$`r_{n+1}:=q(E_{n+1})-q(E_{n})`$. Let $`\bar{r}^{(u)}(x)`$ be the
expected option reward from $`x`$. Values $`V^{\mu}(x)`$ and
$`Q^{\mu}(x,o_{i}^{(u)})`$ are expected future quality increments, with
$`Q^{\mu}`$ executing $`o_{i}^{(u)}`$ first. The semi-MDP Bellman
recursion ([Bradtke and Duff, 1994](#bib.bib3); [Dietterich,
2000](#bib.bib2)) gives

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
Q^{\mu}(x,o_{i}^{(u)})=\bar{r}^{(u)}(x)+\sum\nolimits_{x^{\prime}}P^{(u)}(x,x^{\prime})V^{\mu}(x^{\prime}),\quad V^{\mu}(x)=\sum\nolimits_{u}\mu(o_{i}^{(u)}\mid x)Q^{\mu}(x,o_{i}^{(u)}).
``` |  | (1) |

#### Context-Reset SMDP.

The HMA workflow is formulated as a *context-reset SMDP* (CR-SMDP),
whose option boundaries preserve $`(W,E)`$ and reset context to
$`\emptyset`$. For integer cap $`k\geq 1`$, remaining experiment budget
$`t`$, and natural duration $`T_{i}^{(u)}(x_{i})`$ measured in
experiments, option $`i`$ completes
$`d_{i}^{(u)}=\min\{T_{i}^{(u)}(x_{i}),k,t\}`$ experiments before
natural termination, fixed-$`k`$ interruption, or budget exhaustion. If
$`T_{i}^{(u)}(x_{i})>k`$ almost surely and $`t\geq k`$, then
$`\beta_{i}^{(u)}(\ell)=\mathbf{1}_{\{\ell\geq k\}}`$ and $`d_{i}=k`$,
where $`\ell`$ counts completed experiments within the option. Further
formulation details and early natural termination analysis appear in
Appendix [A.1](#A1.SS1 "A.1 Model Details and Exact Performance ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Details and Proofs ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits").

### 3.2 Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits

For $`u\in\{A,B\}`$, let $`\mathfrak{B}^{(u)}`$ be a finite set of
single-agent workflow variants of $`u`$ with ordinary or specified
stronger harnesses. Define the reference workflow family
$`\mathfrak{B}_{A,B}:=\mathfrak{B}^{(A)}\cup\mathfrak{B}^{(B)}`$. All
workflows initialize from $`x_{0}\sim\nu_{0}`$ and satisfy the following
retention assumption.

###### Assumption 3.1 (Retention of the best result).

$`q(E_{n+1})\geq q(E_{n})`$ for all $`n`$.

We adopt the fixed-$`k`$ termination rule from
Section [3.1](#S3.SS1 "3.1 HMA Preliminaries ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits").
For simplicity, assume the alternating policy $`\mu_{k}=(A,B,u_{0},k)`$
starts with
$`u_{0}\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{u\in\{A,B\}}\operatorname{Perf}_{(A,B,u,k)}(N)`$,
where $`\bar{A}=B`$ and $`\bar{B}=A`$. With option kernels
$`P_{k}^{(u)}`$, including context reset, define the cycle kernel and
reference ceiling:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
M_{\mu_{k}}=P_{k}^{(u_{0})}P_{k}^{(\bar{u}_{0})},\qquad U_{*}:=\max_{b\in\mathfrak{B}_{A,B}}\lim_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{Perf}_{b}(N).
``` |  | (2) |

On each task, let $`\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{ref}}`$ and
$`\mathcal{X}_{\mu_{k}}^{\mathrm{alt}}`$ contain states whose shared
components $`(W,E)`$ are reachable by the references and alternation,
respectively. For $`\tau>U_{*}`$, the target set
$`\mathcal{T}:=\{x\in\mathcal{X}_{\mu_{k}}^{\mathrm{alt}}\setminus\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{ref}}:q(x)\geq\tau\}`$
contains high-quality states beyond reference reachability; the next
assumption requires persistent cooperative access to them.

###### Assumption 3.2 (Cooperative access and use).

Fix $`\mu_{k}`$ and $`\tau\in(U_{*},1]`$. In cycle $`m`$, agents $`u`$
and $`\bar{u}`$ start at $`x_{2m}`$ and $`x_{2m+1}`$, respectively.
There exist constants $`\alpha,\beta\in(0,1]`$ such that, for every
$`m`$ with $`\Pr\nolimits_{\mu_{k}}(q(x_{2m})<\tau)>0`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr\nolimits_{\mu_{k}}(x_{2m+1}\in G\mid q(x_{2m})<\tau)\geq\alpha,\quad G:=\{x:q(x)<\tau,\ \sum\nolimits_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}}P_{k}^{(\bar{u})}(x,x^{\prime})\geq\beta\}.
``` |  | (3) |

Here $`\alpha`$ bounds $`u`$’s access to states useful to $`\bar{u}`$,
and $`\beta`$ bounds $`\bar{u}`$’s probability of reaching
$`\mathcal{T}`$.

###### Theorem 3.3 (Joint alternation exceeds the reference limits).

Under
Assumptions [3.1](#S3.Thmtheorem1 "Assumption 3.1 (Retention of the best result). ‣ 3.2 Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")
and [3.2](#S3.Thmtheorem2 "Assumption 3.2 (Cooperative access and use). ‣ 3.2 Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits"),
let $`a=\alpha\beta`$ and
$`F_{0}=\Pr\nolimits_{\mu_{k}}(q(x_{0})<\tau)`$. For finite $`k`$ and
integers $`m\geq 0`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\operatorname{Perf}_{\mu_{k}}(2km)\geq\tau[1-F_{0}(1-a)^{m}],\qquad U_{\mu_{k}}:=\lim_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{Perf}_{\mu_{k}}(N)\geq\tau>U_{*}.
``` |  | (4) |

###### Proof sketch.

With $`F_{m}=\Pr\nolimits_{\mu_{k}}(q(x_{2m})<\tau)`$, persistent access
and quality preservation give $`F_{m+1}\leq(1-a)F_{m}`$. Iteration and
$`q\geq 0`$ imply the bound; monotone convergence gives the limit. ∎

###### Corollary 3.4 (Experiment budget).

Under
Theorem [3.3](#S3.Thmtheorem3 "Theorem 3.3 (Joint alternation exceeds the reference limits). ‣ 3.2 Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits"),
with $`F_{0}=1`$, fixed $`p_{0}\in(0,1)`$, and $`0<a\ll 1`$, a
sufficient budget for $`p_{\mu_{k}}(N;\tau)\geq p_{0}`$ has the leading
form

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
N_{\mathrm{suff}}(k,p_{0},\tau)=\frac{2k}{a}\log\frac{1}{1-p_{0}}\,\bigl[1+O(a)\bigr].
``` |  | (5) |

Taking $`p_{0}=(U_{*}+\eta)/\tau`$, $`0<\eta<\tau-U_{*}`$ guarantees
mean quality $`U_{*}+\eta`$. The budget form also applies to a single
agent with a uniform conditional success probability of at least
$`a>0`$.
Appendix [A.2](#A1.SS2 "A.2 Alternation Guarantee and Experiment Budget ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Details and Proofs ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")
gives the proofs, exact budgets, and startup analysis.

### 3.3 Fixed-k Interruption and Empirical Choice

In this section, we study when fixed-$`k`$ interruption maintains the
advantage in
Theorem [3.3](#S3.Thmtheorem3 "Theorem 3.3 (Joint alternation exceeds the reference limits). ‣ 3.2 Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")
and motivate short caps empirically. We use Natural-Termination
Alternation (NTA), $`\mu_{\infty}:=(A,B,u_{0},\infty)`$, as the
reference without fixed-$`k`$ interruption, with
$`d_{i}^{(u)}=\min\{T_{i}^{(u)}(x_{i}),t\}`$.

Let $`x_{i,\ell}`$ be the state after $`\ell`$ experiments within option
$`i`$, with entry $`x_{i,0}=x_{i}`$ and final state
$`x_{i,-1}:=x_{i,d_{i}^{(u_{i})}}`$ before context reset. At a boundary,
$`x_{i+1,0}=f(x_{i,-1})`$, where $`f(W,E,c):=(W,E,\emptyset)`$. We
define
$`A^{\mu_{\infty}}(x_{i,-1}):=V^{\mu_{\infty}}(x_{i+1,0})-Q^{\mu_{\infty}}(x_{i,-1},o_{i}^{(u_{i})})`$.
Here $`Q^{\mu_{\infty}}`$ continues the current option, whereas
$`V^{\mu_{\infty}}`$ starts the reset option; the interruption
theorem ([Sutton et al., 1999](#bib.bib1), Theorem 2) gives
nondegradation when $`A^{\mu_{\infty}}(x_{i,-1})\geq 0`$.

For the capped policy $`\mu_{k}`$, define the interrupted option indices
$`\mathcal{J}_{\mu_{k}}(N):=\{i\geq 0:\sum_{j=0}^{i}d_{j}^{(u_{j})}<N,\allowbreak\ d_{i}^{(u_{i})}=k<T_{i}^{(u_{i})}(x_{i,0})\}`$.
The expected net gain and NTA margin are
$`\mathcal{A}_{\mu_{k}}(N):=\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{k}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}_{\mu_{k}}(N)}A^{\mu_{\infty}}(x_{i,-1})`$
and
$`\Delta_{\mu_{\infty}}(N):=\operatorname{Perf}_{\mu_{\infty}}(N)-U_{*}`$.

###### Theorem 3.5 (Expected advantage under fixed-k interruption).

For the optimal initial agent $`u_{0}`$, a sufficient condition to
exceed $`U_{*}`$ is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Delta_{\mu_{\infty}}(N)+\mathcal{A}_{\mu_{k}}(N)>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\operatorname{Perf}_{\mu_{k}}(N)>U_{*}.
``` |  | (6) |

###### Proof sketch.

Telescoping the option-level Bellman recursion gives
$`\operatorname{Perf}_{\mu_{k}}(N)-\operatorname{Perf}_{\mu_{\infty}}(N)=\mathcal{A}_{\mu_{k}}(N)`$.
Substitution proves the claim; see
Appendix [A.3](#A1.SS3 "A.3 Interruption Guarantee and Sufficient Caps ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Details and Proofs ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits").
∎

###### Corollary 3.6 (Upper bound on the required cap).

Suppose $`\Delta_{\mu_{\infty}}(N)>0`$ and interruption losses decay
geometrically with $`k`$, then some finite $`1\leq k\leq k_{0}`$
satisfies $`\operatorname{Perf}_{\mu_{k}}(N)>U_{*}`$.

### 3.4 HMA Workflow Overview

In this section, we describe the implementation of the HMA workflow,
including alternating agent execution and final review, as summarized in
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ Figure 3 ‣ Empirically selected cap range: 𝑘≤5. ‣ 3.3 Fixed-k Interruption and Empirical Choice ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits").

#### Agent Execution.

Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ Figure 3 ‣ Empirically selected cap range: 𝑘≤5. ‣ 3.3 Fixed-k Interruption and Empirical Choice ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")
implements $`\mu_{k}=(A,B,u_{0},k)`$ from $`x_{0}\sim\nu_{0}`$. Here
$`t`$ is elapsed time, $`t_{\mathrm{end}}=t_{\mathrm{total}}`$ is the
final deadline, and $`t_{\mathrm{s}}=t_{\mathrm{end}}-t_{\mathrm{rev}}`$
ends exploration. Agent $`u`$ executes $`\pi_{i}^{(u)}`$ until
$`\beta_{i}^{(u)}(\ell,t;k)=\mathbf{1}\{\ell\geq\min\{T_{i}^{(u)}(x_{i}),k\}\ \lor\ t\geq t_{\mathrm{s}}\}=1`$.
Then $`x_{i,-1}=x_{i,\ell}`$ and $`x_{i+1}=f(x_{i,-1})`$ reset context
for agent $`\bar{u}`$.

#### Final review.

The archive $`\mathcal{E}`$ stores evaluated candidates and their
authors in order; $`(E_{-1},u_{-1})`$ is the latest pair. We reserve the
final $`t_{\mathrm{rev}}`$ for the other agent $`\bar{u}_{-1}`$ to
review the archive and select
$`\widehat{E}\leftarrow\textsc{Review}^{(\bar{u}_{-1})}(\mathcal{E},E_{-1},t_{\mathrm{end}})`$,
with $`E_{-1}`$ as the fallback. This cross-agent check seeks agreement
on the best archived candidate without test-set scores, approximating
the result-retention objective of
Assumption [3.1](#S3.Thmtheorem1 "Assumption 3.1 (Retention of the best result). ‣ 3.2 Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")
at final selection.

## 5 Related Work

#### MLE agents and harness-level systems.

MLE-bench evaluates autonomous agents on Kaggle competitions ([Chan et
al., 2025](#bib.bib7)). Recent MLE systems improve individual agents
through search, reflection, memory, branch reuse, recoverable
workspaces, or task-conditioned interfaces ([Toledo et al.,
2025](#bib.bib14); [Chen et al., 2026](#bib.bib8); [Du et al.,
2026](#bib.bib9); [Zhu et al., 2026](#bib.bib19); [Zhao et al.,
2026](#bib.bib18); [Yu et al., 2026](#bib.bib16); [Zhang et al.,
2026](#bib.bib17); [Ye et al., 2026](#bib.bib15)). These methods modify
how one model acts within its harness. HMA instead leaves each native
model–harness pair unchanged and coordinates complete agents at the
workflow level.

#### Multi-agent and long-horizon workflows.

Prior workflows sustain long-horizon execution through reusable
experience, reflection, tree search, workflow search, communication,
role assignment, checkpointing, or independent review ([Shinn et al.,
2023](#bib.bib25); [Zhao et al., 2024](#bib.bib26); [Zhou et al.,
2024](#bib.bib27); [Zhang et al., 2025](#bib.bib28); [Wu et al.,
2024](#bib.bib23); [Qian et al., 2024](#bib.bib24); [Li et al.,
2025](#bib.bib12)). Recent handoff studies examine candidate transfer,
learned control exchange, and the quality cost of continuing another
model’s trajectory ([Luo et al., 2026](#bib.bib13); [Li et al.,
2026](#bib.bib11); [Ganz et al., 2026](#bib.bib10)). HMA studies
fixed-cap alternation of complete native agents on one shared workspace,
with a context reset at every handoff.

#### Options and semi-MDPs.

Semi-Markov decision processes model actions with variable duration,
while options represent such actions through an internal policy and
termination rule ([Bradtke and Duff, 1994](#bib.bib3); [Dietterich,
2000](#bib.bib2)). The interruption theorem characterizes when
terminating an option early preserves or improves continuation
value ([Sutton et al., 1999](#bib.bib1)); subsequent work studies
learned and off-policy termination ([Bacon et al., 2017](#bib.bib4);
[Harutyunyan et al., 2018](#bib.bib6)). HMA models each agent session as
an option and applies this framework to fixed-cap alternation with
context reset.

## 6 Conclusion

HMA alternates native agents, resetting context while preserving shared
task state. Our CR-SMDP analysis gives sufficient conditions and budget
bounds for exceeding single-agent reference limits and conditions for
preserving this advantage under fixed-cap interruption. Across six
MLE-bench configurations, HMA outperforms the stronger constituent in
medal rate under equal six-hour budgets, reaching 78.2% at best. A
supplementary AOPT case illustrates application to kernel optimization
over 48 hours; establishing comparative gains beyond MLE-bench remains
future work. Together, these results motivate further study of
workflow-level coordination for sustained exploration across iterative
optimization tasks.

### A.1 Model Details and Exact Performance

We supplement
Section [3.1](#S3.SS1 "3.1 HMA Preliminaries ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")
with finite-budget definitions, the general option recursion, and exact
performance expressions used in the alternation proof.

###### Definition A.1 (State and finite budget).

The state from
Section [3.1](#S3.SS1 "3.1 HMA Preliminaries ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")
implicitly includes agent identity, option age, and any scheduling
history needed for the Markov property ([Dietterich, 2000](#bib.bib2),
Section 3.2). For session $`o_{i}^{(u_{i})}`$ at $`x_{i}`$, let
$`n_{i}`$ be the completed experiment count and $`t_{i}`$ the remaining
budget. Then

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
n_{i}:=\sum_{h=0}^{i-1}d_{h},\qquad t_{i}:=N-n_{i},\qquad d_{i}=\min\{T_{i}^{(u_{i})}(x_{i}),k,t_{i}\}.
``` |  | (7) |

Here $`n_{0}=0`$, and $`T_{i}^{(u)}(x_{i})`$ is the positive,
almost-surely finite natural duration, which may depend on the entry
state. Thus early natural termination and a final partial option are
included; $`k=\infty`$ gives NTA. Values track the remaining budget,
vanish when it is exhausted, and no option starts at $`t_{i}=0`$.

###### Definition A.2 (Option rewards and transitions).

For $`\gamma\in(0,1]`$ and the experiment rewards in
Section [3.1](#S3.SS1 "3.1 HMA Preliminaries ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits"),
define

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\bar{r}^{(u)}(x):=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{\ell=0}^{d_{i}-1}\gamma^{\ell}r_{n_{i}+\ell+1}\;\middle|\;x_{i}=x,\ u_{i}=u\right].
``` |  | (8) |

Let $`\Phi_{k,t}^{(u)}(x^{\prime},d\mid x)`$ be the joint law of
duration and next option-entry state, including context reset, and
$`P_{k,t}^{(u)}(x,x^{\prime}):=\sum_{d}\Phi_{k,t}^{(u)}(x^{\prime},d\mid x)`$.
The complete-option kernel is $`P_{k}^{(u)}:=P_{k,\infty}^{(u)}`$;
finite $`t`$ truncates the same option. Suppressing fixed $`k,t`$, the
general semi-MDP recursion ([Sutton et al., 1999](#bib.bib1); [Bradtke
and Duff, 1994](#bib.bib3)) is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
Q^{\mu}(x,o_{i}^{(u)})=\bar{r}^{(u)}(x)+\sum_{d,x^{\prime}}\gamma^{d}\Phi^{(u)}(x^{\prime},d\mid x)V^{\mu}(x^{\prime}).
``` |  | (9) |

At $`\gamma=1`$, this reduces
to equation [1](#S3.E1 "In Workflow trajectory, rewards, and values. ‣ 3.1 HMA Preliminaries ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits"),
and total reward is $`q(E_{N})-q(E_{0})`$.

###### Proposition A.3 (Exact performance expressions).

Let $`\mathcal{P}_{\mu}`$ be the one-experiment kernel under $`\mu`$ on
the augmented state, including option boundaries, and
$`S_{\tau}:=\{x:q(x)\geq\tau\}`$. After workflow termination, retain the
terminal state. Then

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
p_{\mu}(N;\tau)=\nu_{0}\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{N}\mathbf{1}_{S_{\tau}},\qquad\operatorname{Perf}_{\mu}(N)=\nu_{0}\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{N}q,
``` |  | (10) |

where $`\mathbf{1}_{S_{\tau}}`$ indicates target attainment. These
expressions give the probability of reaching $`S_{\tau}`$ and the
expected final quality, respectively, with
$`\operatorname{Perf}_{\mu}(N)\geq\tau p_{\mu}(N;\tau)`$.

###### Proof of Proposition [A.3](#A1.Thmtheorem3 "Proposition A.3 (Exact performance expressions). ‣ A.1 Model Details and Exact Performance ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Details and Proofs ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits").

The endpoint law is $`\nu_{0}\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{N}`$; applying it to
$`\mathbf{1}_{S_{\tau}}`$ and $`q`$
gives equation [10](#A1.E10 "In Proposition A.3 (Exact performance expressions). ‣ A.1 Model Details and Exact Performance ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Details and Proofs ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits").
Taking expectations of $`q\geq\tau\mathbf{1}_{S_{\tau}}`$ yields the
inequality. ∎

### A.2 Alternation Guarantee and Experiment Budget

We follow
Section [3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits"):
cooperative access yields a failure recursion, which proves the
alternation guarantee and the experiment budget; we then discuss startup
effects.

###### Definition A.4 (Reference ceiling and cycle budget).

For the reference family in
Section [3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits"),
bounded quality and
Assumption [3.1](#S3.Thmtheorem1 "Assumption 3.1 (Retention of the best result). ‣ 3.2 Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")
give

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
U_{*}:=\max_{b\in\mathfrak{B}_{A,B}}U_{b},\qquad U_{b}:=\lim_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{Perf}_{b}(N)=\mathbb{E}_{b,\nu_{0}}\!\left[\lim_{N\to\infty}q(E_{N})\right].
``` |  | (11) |

Fix $`\mu_{k}`$ and $`\tau>U_{*}`$. Write $`u`$ for the first agent in
each cycle: cycle $`m`$ executes $`o_{2m}^{(u)}`$ followed by
$`o_{2m+1}^{(\bar{u})}`$, with kernel $`M_{\mu_{k}}`$. Before
total-budget truncation, the experiments used by $`m`$ cycles are
$`C_{\mu_{k},m}:=\sum_{i=0}^{2m-1}\min\{T_{i}^{(u_{i})}(x_{i}),k\}`$, so
$`C_{\mu_{k},m}\leq 2km`$ for finite $`k`$. Write
$`F_{m}:=\Pr\nolimits_{\mu_{k}}(q(x_{2m})<\tau)`$, where
$`\Pr\nolimits_{\mu_{k}}`$ and $`\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{k}}`$ include the
initial draw from $`\nu_{0}`$.

###### Lemma A.5 (Cooperative success and failure recursion).

Under
Assumptions [3.1](#S3.Thmtheorem1 "Assumption 3.1 (Retention of the best result). ‣ 3.2 Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")
and [3.2](#S3.Thmtheorem2 "Assumption 3.2 (Cooperative access and use). ‣ 3.2 Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits"),
each cycle has conditional success probability at least
$`a:=\alpha\beta`$ whenever $`F_{m}>0`$, and $`F_{m+1}\leq(1-a)F_{m}`$
for all $`m\geq 0`$.

###### Proof of Lemma [A.5](#A1.Thmtheorem5 "Lemma A.5 (Cooperative success and failure recursion). ‣ A.2 Alternation Guarantee and Experiment Budget ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Details and Proofs ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits").

The $`u`$-option enters $`G`$ with conditional probability at least
$`\alpha`$, and the $`\bar{u}`$-option reaches
$`\mathcal{T}\subseteq S_{\tau}`$ from each $`x\in G`$ with probability
at least $`\beta`$. By the Markov property, for $`F_{m}>0`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr\nolimits_{\mu_{k}}(x_{2m+2}\in S_{\tau}\mid x_{2m}\notin S_{\tau})\geq\beta\Pr\nolimits_{\mu_{k}}(x_{2m+1}\in G\mid x_{2m}\notin S_{\tau})\geq\alpha\beta=a.
``` |  | (12) |

Quality retention makes $`S_{\tau}`$ absorbing, so
$`F_{m+1}\leq F_{m}(1-a)`$. If $`F_{m}=0`$, it also gives $`F_{m+1}=0`$.
∎

###### Theorem A.6 (Joint alternation exceeds the reference limits; Theorem [3.3](#S3.Thmtheorem3 "Theorem 3.3 (Joint alternation exceeds the reference limits). ‣ 3.2 Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits") restated).

Under
Assumptions [3.1](#S3.Thmtheorem1 "Assumption 3.1 (Retention of the best result). ‣ 3.2 Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")
and [3.2](#S3.Thmtheorem2 "Assumption 3.2 (Cooperative access and use). ‣ 3.2 Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits"),
take $`a=\alpha\beta`$ as a lower bound on conditional success per
cycle, and $`F_{0}=\Pr\nolimits_{\mu_{k}}(q(x_{0})<\tau)`$. For finite
$`k`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\operatorname{Perf}_{\mu_{k}}(2km)\geq\tau[1-F_{0}(1-a)^{m}],\qquad U_{\mu_{k}}:=\lim_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{Perf}_{\mu_{k}}(N)\geq\tau>U_{*}.
``` |  | (13) |

###### Proof of Theorem [A.6](#A1.Thmtheorem6 "Theorem A.6 (Joint alternation exceeds the reference limits; Theorem restated). ‣ A.2 Alternation Guarantee and Experiment Budget ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Details and Proofs ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits").

Iterating
Lemma [A.5](#A1.Thmtheorem5 "Lemma A.5 (Cooperative success and failure recursion). ‣ A.2 Alternation Guarantee and Experiment Budget ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Details and Proofs ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")
gives $`F_{m}\leq F_{0}(1-a)^{m}`$. Since $`C_{\mu_{k},m}\leq 2km`$,
quality retention and
Proposition [A.3](#A1.Thmtheorem3 "Proposition A.3 (Exact performance expressions). ‣ A.1 Model Details and Exact Performance ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Details and Proofs ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")
yield

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\operatorname{Perf}_{\mu_{k}}(2km)\geq\tau p_{\mu_{k}}(2km;\tau)\geq\tau(1-F_{m})\geq\tau[1-F_{0}(1-a)^{m}].
``` |  | (14) |

Bounded monotone convergence ensures that $`U_{\mu_{k}}`$ exists;
letting $`m\to\infty`$ gives $`U_{\mu_{k}}\geq\tau>U_{*}`$. The same
proof permits early natural termination when
Assumption [3.2](#S3.Thmtheorem2 "Assumption 3.2 (Cooperative access and use). ‣ 3.2 Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")
holds for the resulting kernels, since $`C_{\mu_{k},m}\leq 2km`$ still
holds. ∎

###### Corollary A.7 (Experiment budget; Corollary [3.4](#S3.Thmtheorem4 "Corollary 3.4 (Experiment budget). ‣ 3.2 Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits") restated).

Under the theorem’s conditions, let $`F_{0}=1`$ and fix
$`p_{0}\in(0,1)`$. For $`0<a\ll 1`$, a sufficient budget for
$`p_{\mu_{k}}(N;\tau)\geq p_{0}`$ has the leading form

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
N_{\mathrm{suff}}(k,p_{0},\tau)=\frac{2k}{a}\log\frac{1}{1-p_{0}}\,\bigl[1+O(a)\bigr].
``` |  | (15) |

###### Proof of Corollary [A.7](#A1.Thmtheorem7 "Corollary A.7 (Experiment budget; Corollary restated). ‣ A.2 Alternation Guarantee and Experiment Budget ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Details and Proofs ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits").

For $`0<a<1`$, the bound $`p_{\mu_{k}}(2km;\tau)\geq 1-(1-a)^{m}`$
reaches $`p_{0}`$ once $`m\geq\log(1/(1-p_{0}))/[-\log(1-a)]`$. Hence
the exact rounded sufficient budget is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
N_{\mathrm{suff}}(k,p_{0},\tau)=2k\left\lceil\frac{\log(1/(1-p_{0}))}{-\log(1-a)}\right\rceil.
``` |  | (16) |

Using $`-\log(1-a)=a+O(a^{2})`$ and a rounding error below $`2k`$ gives
$`(2k/a)\log(1/(1-p_{0}))+O(k)`$, equivalent
to equation [15](#A1.E15 "In Corollary A.7 (Experiment budget; Corollary restated). ‣ A.2 Alternation Guarantee and Experiment Budget ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Details and Proofs ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")
for fixed $`p_{0}`$. Finally, $`p_{0}=(U_{*}+\eta)/\tau`$,
$`0<\eta<\tau-U_{*}`$, gives
$`\operatorname{Perf}_{\mu_{k}}(N)\geq U_{*}+\eta`$ by
Proposition [A.3](#A1.Thmtheorem3 "Proposition A.3 (Exact performance expressions). ‣ A.1 Model Details and Exact Performance ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Details and Proofs ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits").
∎

#### Startup and initial-agent effects.

Startup denotes the early cycles before cooperative progress stabilizes.
The initial agent can affect access to $`G`$ and finite-budget quality.
If the two starts later have similar conditional success rates, their
subsequent budget scales $`2k/a`$ are similar, while their startup
progress can still differ.

###### Corollary A.8 (Natural termination and quality limits).

For Natural-Termination Alternation (NTA), use
$`\mu_{\infty}=(A,B,u_{0},\infty)`$. Under
Assumptions [3.1](#S3.Thmtheorem1 "Assumption 3.1 (Retention of the best result). ‣ 3.2 Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")
and [3.2](#S3.Thmtheorem2 "Assumption 3.2 (Cooperative access and use). ‣ 3.2 Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")
and almost-surely finite option durations, let
$`M_{\tau}:=\inf\{m\geq 0:q(x_{2m})\geq\tau\}`$. Then
$`C_{\mu_{\infty},M_{\tau}}<\infty`$ almost surely and
$`U_{\mu_{\infty}}\geq\tau>U_{*}`$. If NTA and a finite cap satisfy
access for every $`\tau\in(U_{*},U^{\dagger})`$, where
$`U^{\dagger}\in(U_{*},1]`$ bounds both reachable quality spaces, then
$`U_{\mu_{k}}=U_{\mu_{\infty}}=U^{\dagger}`$.

###### Proof of Corollary [A.8](#A1.Thmtheorem8 "Corollary A.8 (Natural termination and quality limits). ‣ A.3 Interruption Guarantee and Sufficient Caps ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Details and Proofs ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits").

Applying equation [12](#A1.E12 "In Proof of Lemma . ‣ A.2 Alternation Guarantee and Experiment Budget ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Details and Proofs ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")
to NTA gives
$`\Pr\nolimits_{\mu_{\infty}}(M_{\tau}>m)\leq F_{0}(1-a)^{m}\to 0`$,
with $`a=\alpha\beta`$. Thus $`M_{\tau}`$ and the sum of its option
durations are almost surely finite. Monotone quality yields
$`U_{\mu_{\infty}}\geq\tau`$. Combining this argument with
Theorem [A.6](#A1.Thmtheorem6 "Theorem A.6 (Joint alternation exceeds the reference limits; Theorem restated). ‣ A.2 Alternation Guarantee and Experiment Budget ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Details and Proofs ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")
for every $`\tau\in(U_{*},U^{\dagger})`$ and using the common upper
bound gives equality for both limits. ∎

###### Definition A.9 (Interruption advantage and reference margin).

Fix $`\mu_{k}=(A,B,u_{0},k)`$ and $`N`$. The workflows $`\mu_{k}`$ and
$`\mu_{\infty}=(A,B,u_{0},\infty)`$ share internal policies and boundary
updates and differ only in option termination. Following
Section [3.3](#S3.SS3 "3.3 Fixed-k Interruption and Empirical Choice ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits"),
$`x_{i,0}=x_{i}`$ is option $`i`$’s entry,
$`x_{i,-1}=x_{i,d_{i}^{(u_{i})}}`$ its endpoint before reset, and
$`x_{i+1,0}=f(x_{i,-1})`$ the next entry, with
$`q(x_{i+1,0})=q(x_{i,-1})`$.

At an added cap interruption, NTA would continue from $`x_{i,-1}`$ with
the current context. Ending the option instead gives advantage
$`A^{\mu_{\infty}}(x_{i,-1}):=V^{\mu_{\infty}}(x_{i+1,0})-Q^{\mu_{\infty}}(x_{i,-1},o_{i}^{(u_{i})})`$.
Both values use NTA with the same remaining experiment budget; the next
entry activates the other agent with empty context. The interruption
contribution is zero at natural termination or budget exhaustion.

The added interruptions are indexed by

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{J}_{\mu_{k}}(N):=\left\{i\geq 0:\sum_{j=0}^{i}d_{j}^{(u_{j})}<N,\ d_{i}^{(u_{i})}=k<T_{i}^{(u_{i})}(x_{i,0})\right\}.
``` |  |

Thus natural termination takes precedence when it coincides with the
cap. The expected net gain is
$`\mathcal{A}_{\mu_{k}}(N):=\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{k}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}_{\mu_{k}}(N)}A^{\mu_{\infty}}(x_{i,-1})`$,
and the NTA margin is
$`\Delta_{\mu_{\infty}}(N):=\operatorname{Perf}_{\mu_{\infty}}(N)-U_{*}`$.

###### Theorem A.10 (Expected advantage under fixed-k interruption; Theorem [3.5](#S3.Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 3.5 (Expected advantage under fixed-k interruption). ‣ 3.3 Fixed-k Interruption and Empirical Choice ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits") restated).

For the selected initial agent $`u_{0}`$, a sufficient condition to
exceed $`U_{*}`$ is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Delta_{\mu_{\infty}}(N)+\mathcal{A}_{\mu_{k}}(N)>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\operatorname{Perf}_{\mu_{k}}(N)>U_{*}.
``` |  | (17) |

###### Proof of Theorem [A.10](#A1.Thmtheorem10 "Theorem A.10 (Expected advantage under fixed-k interruption; Theorem restated). ‣ A.3 Interruption Guarantee and Sufficient Caps ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Details and Proofs ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits").

Let $`M`$ be the number of options executed under $`\mu_{k}`$ within
budget $`N`$, with $`x_{M,0}`$ terminal, and let
$`\iota_{i}:=\mathbf{1}_{\{i\in\mathcal{J}_{\mu_{k}}(N)\}}`$. For each
$`i<M`$, the option-level Bellman recursion ([Sutton et al.,
1999](#bib.bib1), proof of Theorem 2) gives

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{k}}\!\left[q(x_{i+1,0})-q(x_{i,0})+V^{\mu_{\infty}}(x_{i+1,0})\mid x_{i,0}\right]-V^{\mu_{\infty}}(x_{i,0})`$ |  | (18) |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{k}}\!\left[\iota_{i}A^{\mu_{\infty}}(x_{i,-1})\mid x_{i,0}\right].`$ |  |  |

Since $`V^{\mu_{\infty}}(x_{M,0})=0`$ and $`x_{0,0}=x_{0}\sim\nu_{0}`$,
summing telescopes:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\operatorname{Perf}_{\mu_{k}}(N)-\operatorname{Perf}_{\mu_{\infty}}(N)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{k}}\!\left[q(x_{M,0})-q(x_{0,0})-V^{\mu_{\infty}}(x_{0,0})\right]`$ |  | (19) |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{k}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}_{\mu_{k}}(N)}A^{\mu_{\infty}}(x_{i,-1})=\mathcal{A}_{\mu_{k}}(N).`$ |  |  |

Thus
$`\operatorname{Perf}_{\mu_{k}}(N)-U_{*}=\Delta_{\mu_{\infty}}(N)+\mathcal{A}_{\mu_{k}}(N)`$,
which
proves equation [17](#A1.E17 "In Theorem A.10 (Expected advantage under fixed-k interruption; Theorem restated). ‣ A.3 Interruption Guarantee and Sufficient Caps ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Details and Proofs ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits").
∎

#### Initial-agent dependence of interruption gains.

The kernels $`P_{k}^{(A)}P_{k}^{(B)}`$ and $`P_{k}^{(B)}P_{k}^{(A)}`$
can induce different early task states, changing both
$`\Delta_{\mu_{\infty}}(N)`$ and $`\mathcal{A}_{\mu_{k}}(N)`$.
Consequently, the preferred initial agent can depend on $`k`$ and $`N`$;
the associated startup budget effects are discussed in
Appendix [A.2](#A1.SS2 "A.2 Alternation Guarantee and Experiment Budget ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Details and Proofs ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits").

###### Corollary A.11 (A sufficient cap in a finite candidate range).

Fix $`A,B,u_{0},N`$ with $`\Delta_{\mu_{\infty}}(N)>0`$ and integer
$`K\geq 1`$. For each candidate cap $`1\leq k\leq K`$, let
$`\mu_{k}=(A,B,u_{0},k)`$ and suppose there is
$`\varepsilon_{\mu_{k}}\geq 0`$ with
$`\mathcal{A}_{\mu_{k}}(N)\geq-\varepsilon_{\mu_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{k}}|\mathcal{J}_{\mu_{k}}(N)|`$.
Then

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\min_{1\leq k\leq K}\lfloor N/k\rfloor\varepsilon_{\mu_{k}}<\Delta_{\mu_{\infty}}(N)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\exists\,1\leq k\leq K:\operatorname{Perf}_{\mu_{k}}(N)>U_{*}.
``` |  | (20) |

###### Proof of Corollary [A.11](#A1.Thmtheorem11 "Corollary A.11 (A sufficient cap in a finite candidate range). ‣ Initial-agent dependence of interruption gains. ‣ A.3 Interruption Guarantee and Sufficient Caps ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Details and Proofs ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits").

Each added interruption uses a distinct block of $`k`$ experiments, so
$`|\mathcal{J}_{\mu_{k}}(N)|\leq\lfloor N/k\rfloor`$ and
$`\mathcal{A}_{\mu_{k}}(N)\geq-\lfloor N/k\rfloor\varepsilon_{\mu_{k}}`$.
Choose a minimizing cap
in equation [20](#A1.E20 "In Corollary A.11 (A sufficient cap in a finite candidate range). ‣ Initial-agent dependence of interruption gains. ‣ A.3 Interruption Guarantee and Sufficient Caps ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Details and Proofs ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")
and apply
Theorem [A.10](#A1.Thmtheorem10 "Theorem A.10 (Expected advantage under fixed-k interruption; Theorem restated). ‣ A.3 Interruption Guarantee and Sufficient Caps ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Details and Proofs ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits").
∎

###### Corollary A.12 (Explicit cap under geometric loss decay; quantitative form of Corollary [3.6](#S3.Thmtheorem6 "Corollary 3.6 (Upper bound on the required cap). ‣ 3.3 Fixed-k Interruption and Empirical Choice ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")).

Fix $`A,B,u_{0},N`$ with $`\Delta_{\mu_{\infty}}(N)>0`$. Under the
expected loss condition of
Corollary [A.11](#A1.Thmtheorem11 "Corollary A.11 (A sufficient cap in a finite candidate range). ‣ Initial-agent dependence of interruption gains. ‣ A.3 Interruption Guarantee and Sufficient Caps ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Details and Proofs ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits"),
assume $`\varepsilon_{\mu_{k}}\leq C\rho^{k}`$ for every
$`\mu_{k}=(A,B,u_{0},k)`$, $`k\geq 1`$, with constants $`C>0`$ and
$`0<\rho<1`$. The policy $`\mu_{k_{0}}:=(A,B,u_{0},k_{0})`$ with the
following cap preserves at least half the NTA margin:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle k_{0}`$ | $`\displaystyle:=\max\!\left\{1,\left\lceil\frac{\log(2NC/\Delta_{\mu_{\infty}}(N))}{-\log\rho}\right\rceil\right\},`$ |  | (21) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\operatorname{Perf}_{\mu_{k_{0}}}(N)`$ | $`\displaystyle\geq U_{*}+\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta_{\mu_{\infty}}(N)>U_{*}.`$ |  |  |

###### Proof of Corollary [A.12](#A1.Thmtheorem12 "Corollary A.12 (Explicit cap under geometric loss decay; quantitative form of Corollary ). ‣ Initial-agent dependence of interruption gains. ‣ A.3 Interruption Guarantee and Sufficient Caps ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Details and Proofs ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits").

The expected loss is at most
$`\lfloor N/k\rfloor C\rho^{k}\leq NC\rho^{k}`$. The choice of $`k_{0}`$
makes this at most $`\Delta_{\mu_{\infty}}(N)/2`$; substitution
in equation [19](#A1.E19 "In Proof of Theorem . ‣ A.3 Interruption Guarantee and Sufficient Caps ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Details and Proofs ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")
proves the claim. Taking $`k=k_{0}`$ for the selected initial agent
gives a sufficient cap in $`[1,k_{0}]`$, proving
Corollary [3.6](#S3.Thmtheorem6 "Corollary 3.6 (Upper bound on the required cap). ‣ 3.3 Fixed-k Interruption and Empirical Choice ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits").
Uniform $`C,\rho`$ and a positive margin lower bound across budgets give
$`k_{0}=O(\log N)`$. ∎

### B.1 Retention Statistics and Confidence Bounds

We establish the statistical guarantee supporting
Section [3.3](#S3.SS3 "3.3 Fixed-k Interruption and Empirical Choice ‣ 3 Method ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits").
Definition [B.1](#A2.Thmtheorem1 "Definition B.1 (Observed prefix gain). ‣ B.1 Retention Statistics and Confidence Bounds ‣ Appendix B Prefix Retention and Empirical Cap Selection ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")
specifies task-level prefix retention,
Assumption [B.2](#A2.Thmtheorem2 "Assumption B.2 (Independent task observations). ‣ B.1 Retention Statistics and Confidence Bounds ‣ Appendix B Prefix Retention and Empirical Cap Selection ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")
states the sampling condition, and
Proposition [B.3](#A2.Thmtheorem3 "Proposition B.3 (Simultaneous lower confidence bounds). ‣ B.1 Retention Statistics and Confidence Bounds ‣ Appendix B Prefix Retention and Empirical Cap Selection ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")
gives simultaneous $`1-\delta`$ lower confidence bounds.

###### Definition B.1 (Observed prefix gain).

For policy $`\mu`$ and task $`j`$, define

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{O}_{\mu,j}:=\{i:o_{i}^{(u_{i})}\text{ is recorded on task }j\text{ under }\mu\}.
``` |  |

For each session index $`i\in\mathcal{O}_{\mu,j}`$, let $`l_{i}`$ be its
recorded experiment count and $`q_{i}(d)`$ its best quality after $`d`$
experiments, with entry quality at $`d=0`$. For integer $`k\geq 0`$,
define

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
g_{i}(k)=q_{i}(\min\{k,l_{i}\})-q_{i}(0),\qquad\zeta_{\mu,j}(k)=\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal{O}_{\mu,j}}g_{i}(k)}{\sum_{i\in\mathcal{O}_{\mu,j}}g_{i}(l_{i})}.
``` |  | (22) |

The ratio is defined for positive total gain; $`n_{\mu}`$ counts these
tasks.

###### Assumption B.2 (Independent task observations).

For each prespecified policy $`\mu`$, cap $`k`$, and statistic, let
$`Y_{j}\in[0,1]`$ be $`\zeta_{\mu,j}(k)`$ or
$`\mathbf{1}_{\{\zeta_{\mu,j}(k)\geq 0.9\}}`$. Conditional on defined
ratios, assume independence across the $`n_{\mu}`$ tasks. Dependence
across configurations and caps is allowed.

###### Proposition B.3 (Simultaneous lower confidence bounds).

Under
Assumption [B.2](#A2.Thmtheorem2 "Assumption B.2 (Independent task observations). ‣ B.1 Retention Statistics and Confidence Bounds ‣ Appendix B Prefix Retention and Empirical Cap Selection ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits"),
set $`\bar{Y}=n_{\mu}^{-1}\sum_{j}Y_{j}`$ and
$`\mu_{Y}=n_{\mu}^{-1}\sum_{j}\mathbb{E}Y_{j}`$. For
$`M_{\mathrm{cmp}}`$ prespecified comparisons and $`\delta\in(0,1)`$,
define $`\underline{\mu}_{Y}\leq\bar{Y}`$ by

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
n_{\mu}d_{\mathrm{KL}}(\bar{Y}\|\underline{\mu}_{Y})=\log(M_{\mathrm{cmp}}/\delta),
``` |  | (23) |

with $`\underline{\mu}_{Y}=0`$ when $`\bar{Y}=0`$. Then
$`\Pr(\underline{\mu}_{Y}\leq\mu_{Y}\text{ for all comparisons})\geq 1-\delta`$.

###### Proof of Proposition [B.3](#A2.Thmtheorem3 "Proposition B.3 (Simultaneous lower confidence bounds). ‣ B.1 Retention Statistics and Confidence Bounds ‣ Appendix B Prefix Retention and Empirical Cap Selection ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits").

The exponential-moment bound ([Garivier and Cappé, 2011](#bib.bib5),
Lemma 9), Jensen’s inequality, and Chernoff optimization give
$`\Pr(\bar{Y}\geq z)\leq e^{-n_{\mu}d_{\mathrm{KL}}(z\|\mu_{Y})}`$ for
$`\mu_{Y}<z\leq 1`$. Inverting at failure level
$`\delta/M_{\mathrm{cmp}}`$
gives equation [23](#A2.E23 "In Proposition B.3 (Simultaneous lower confidence bounds). ‣ B.1 Retention Statistics and Confidence Bounds ‣ Appendix B Prefix Retention and Empirical Cap Selection ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits");
a union bound over the comparisons proves the claim. ∎

## Appendix G Case Studies of Heterogeneous Alternation

We examine four model pairs across five model families: GPT–Opus,
GPT–DeepSeek, GLM–Kimi, and DeepSeek–Kimi. The INGV case in
Section [4.4](#S4.SS4 "4.4 Case Study ‣ 4 Experiment ‣ Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits")
is followed by examples from text prediction, image regression, and
satellite positioning.

#### Selection and measurement.

We selected task runs with inspectable artifacts, controller logs,
evaluator receipts, and native response records. GPT–Opus, GPT–DeepSeek,
and DeepSeek–Kimi occur in the main comparison; GLM–Kimi is an
additional combination using the same $`k=5`$ workflow. Following the
trajectory analysis of [Zhao et al. (2026)](#bib.bib18), we align each
accepted candidate with its producing option using controller counts and
receipt times. Test scores are evaluated offline and were hidden from
the agents; all accepted scores, including regressions, are plotted.
Goal references use three runs per constituent. The appendix figures
compare disjoint first- and last-20-response windows; options with fewer
than 40 responses show their counts instead. Final review is excluded
from token statistics and preserves the standing candidate in all four
cases. Response renewal is compared within the same model, since
tokenizers and policies differ. These cases illustrate observed
mechanisms rather than estimating their average causal effect.

### G.1 INGV Volcanic Eruption Prediction

#### Repeated refinement.

On INGV (MBH-07), GPT and Opus complete seven exploration options with
5, 5, 5, 2, 5, 1, and 4 accepted experiments and 77, 263, 251, 59, 110,
49, and 48 responses, respectively. Options 1, 2, 3, and 5 reach the
cap; options 4 and 6 end naturally, and option 7 reaches the deadline.

and 34,234: five of the six post-handoff options improve the historical

predictions support successive refinements. The trace records GPT
reusing and pruning Opus’s retrieval features, Opus adding consistency
constraints for paired windows, GPT introducing an uncertainty-based
residual gate, Opus refining its calibration, and GPT enforcing joint
temporal feasibility.

After the first Opus session reaches 41,214 MAE, subsequent alternation
lowers it to 34,234, a further 16.9% improvement and 46.5% below the
best of six constituent goal runs (63,991). Native MAE resolves progress
beyond the already-gold goal results.
````
