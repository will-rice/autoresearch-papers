###### Abstract

Developing effective machine learning models requires complex manual
tuning, while current Automated Machine Learning (AutoML) frameworks
remain domain-specific and isolate model architecture, training
hyperparameter, and data ratio optimization. Here, we bridge this gap by
proposing a universal, large language model (LLM)-based AutoML framework
that jointly optimizes these configurations for the first time without
the need for task-specific fine-tuning. We propose NCode, a unified
numerical encoding scheme that translates diverse AutoML design choices
into universal discrete sequences and effectively reframes the
optimization process as a unified ranking task. Trained solely on
optimization trajectories derived from a limited, single-domain vision
task, the proposed LLM-based searcher demonstrates impressive
cross-task, cross-modality and joint search-space optimization
generalizability, achieving state-of-the-art performance across image
classification, image segmentation, speaker verification and
text-to-image benchmarks. Crucially, it exhibits superior scalability in
the large-scale training and joint optimization of a billion-parameter
Diffusion Transformer, requiring 896 GPU-days and attaining a
state-of-the-art GenEval score of 0.91. By leveraging reinforcement
learning to instill Chain-of-Thought reasoning, we provide a systematic
way to interpret the LLM-based searcher’s internal decision-making
mechanisms, revealing its optimization strategy shifted from the
early-stage diversity-driven exploration to the late-stage anchor
exploitation. Our proposed framework provides a scalable, generalizable,
and interpretable pathway toward general-purpose open-world machine
learning optimization.

## I Introduction

Recent advancements in Machine Learning (ML) have revolutionized diverse
domains, including computer vision, natural language processing, and
audio analysis. Despite the progress, developing effective ML models
remains a complex endeavor, often requiring extensive human experience
in designing network architectures, tuning hyperparameters, and
organizing training data. Consequently, Automated Machine Learning
(AutoML) has emerged as a pivotal solution, aiming to automate such
design choices of ML pipelines with minimal human intervention.

While intensive explorations in AutoML have been made, particularly in
Neural Architecture Search (NAS) for architecture
design \[[57](#bib.bib8), [58](#bib.bib9), [31](#bib.bib12)\] and
Hyperparameter Optimization (HPO) for determining training
hyperparameters \[[9](#bib.bib7)\], the field still lacks a
general-purpose AutoML framework. This gap has become increasingly
challenging in the era of foundation models. Currently, the realization
of such an open-domain system is impeded by three fundamental obstacles:

- •
  Open-world problem generalization. Existing AutoML approaches are
  typically optimized for specific search spaces, lacking the
  flexibility to generalize across diverse tasks, datasets and
  architectures. For instance, the popular gradient-based NAS method
  DARTS \[[31](#bib.bib12)\] achieves 97.24% accuracy on the CIFAR-10
  test set within its original search space, yet suffers a severe
  performance collapse to 39.77% when evaluated on the widely used
  NAS-Bench-201 benchmark \[[12](#bib.bib16)\]. Even though both
  settings share identical dataset and optimizers, this sharp decline
  underscores the inherent fragility of current NAS and AutoML
  techniques when subjected to structural shifts in the search space.
  Consequently, this limitation hinders the application of AutoML
  methods in open-world problems that data distributions, architecture
  search spaces and task definitions are novel or unseen.
- •
  Intricate interdependence of model optimization choices. The optimal
  performance of a model is the result of the complex interplay between
  model architectures, training hyperparameters, and data ratios, etc.
  Such highly non-linear interactions are difficult to optimize in
  isolation. Existing AutoML methods fail to capture the synergies and
  often lead to sub-optimal local minima when components are optimized
  separately.
- •
  The prohibitive cost of AutoML training data acquisition. Creating
  AutoML training datasets is computationally expensive and
  time-consuming, as it involves the repetitive process of model
  training and performance evaluation for every new task. The high cost
  hinders the development and scalability of learning-based AutoML
  methods.

To overcome these barriers, the field is witnessing a paradigm shift
from specialized optimization algorithms toward more generalizable
approaches. In particular, Large Language Models (LLMs) were leveraged
to optimize AutoML problems to take advantage of their strong reasoning
capabilities \[[54](#bib.bib56), [29](#bib.bib57)\]. However, they
remain heavily dependent on task-specific training, fine-tuning, and
manual prompt engineering. As a result, there has been little deployment
of AutoML frameworks that can navigate open-world problems spanning
across multiple architectures, tasks, and modalities.

In this work, we introduce an open-world LLM-based AutoML framework that
is capable of jointly optimizing the complex combination of model
architectures, training hyperparameters, data ratios for different tasks
without task-specific fine-tuning for the first time. To enable the
optimization over the multiple intrinsically diverse search spaces, we
introduce NCode, an encoding scheme that maps heterogeneous design
choices to their respective indices, representing them as unified
numerical sequences
(Fig. [1](#S1.F1 "Fig. 1 ‣ I Introduction ‣ Large Language Models as a Universal Searcher for Open-World Automated Machine Learning")b,c).
By converting diverse AutoML tasks into a universal discrete
optimization problem, NCode allows LLMs to easily generalize to unseen
open-world domains without domain-specific fine-tuning
(Fig. [1](#S1.F1 "Fig. 1 ‣ I Introduction ‣ Large Language Models as a Universal Searcher for Open-World Automated Machine Learning")a).
To train the LLM-based searcher, we curate optimization trajectories
from the NAS-Bench-301 dataset \[[53](#bib.bib18)\], which provides
model architectures and their corresponding image classification
performances on the CIFAR-10 dataset. We model the model arch instances
as a densely connected Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) and apply stochastic
edge pruning to generate diverse, localized architecture search spaces
for rich trajectory formulation. Furthermore, for the LLM-based
searcher’s outputs, we reframe the discrete NCode optimization as a
ranking problem, where the LLM identifies superior candidates from a
candidate pool rather than directly predicting their NCodes, a setting
demonstrated to be significantly simpler to learn than directly
generating full NCodes. Consequently, despite being trained exclusively
on single-domain vision data, our LLM searcher generalizes effectively
to cross-domain joint optimization tasks where data collection is
typically cost-prohibitive.

A preliminary version of our system that focused only on model
architecture search was presented in EMNLP2025 \[[21](#bib.bib3)\]. The
current study unveils the unified LLM-based searcher’s capabilities in
significantly more complex, heterogeneous search spaces and mastering
the synergy between model architectures, training hyperparameters, and
data ratios across seven domains, four tasks and three modalities
(vision, text, audio) without fine-tuning. More importantly, our
LLM-based searcher is trained to explain its optimization rationales by
learning explainable Chain-of-Thought (CoT) via Group Relative Policy
Optimization (GRPO)  \[[39](#bib.bib34)\]. Our analysis revealed how the
model navigates the solution space for optimization and offers critical
insights. Extensive experiments provide compelling evidence and
demonstrate that our framework achieves state-of-the-art performance
across a diverse suite of open-world machine learning problems.

We highlight our major contributions as follows:

1.  1.  Unified Problem Representation: We introduce a universal numerical
        encoding that maps model architectures, hyperparameters, and data
        ratios into a unified discrete space, allowing the AutoML framework
        to jointly optimize diverse design choices across distinct search
        spaces.
2.  2.  Search Trajectory Curation: We introduce a pruning-based
        architecture sampling strategy to curate rich, diverse optimization
        trajectories, mitigating the prohibitive cost of AutoML data
        collection. By simultaneously reframing the LLM’s objective from
        direct sequence generation to conditional ranking, we boost the
        searcher’s capacity to generalize across complex optimization
        landscapes.
3.  3.  Extensive Empirical Validation: By deploying our approach across
        real-world machine learning tasks in diverse scenarios, and
        achieving state-of-the-art (SOTA) performances, we demonstrate the
        framework’s robust zero-shot generalizability.
4.  4.  Interpretability via RL-driven Chain-of-Thought: To unveil the
        internal decision-making mechanism of the LLM-based searcher, we
        leverage Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to instill
        Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning without requiring human annotation.
        This self-explaining capability provides critical insights into the
        model’s learned optimization strategies, specifically revealing a
        dynamic shift from early-stage diversity-driven exploration to
        late-stage anchor exploitation, and highlights the inherent
        limitations of conventional discrete optimization methods in
        existing AutoML frameworks.

## II Related Work

### II-A Traditional Automated Machine Learning

Automated Machine Learning (AutoML) has evolved significantly,
progressing from early reinforcement learning \[[57](#bib.bib8),
[52](#bib.bib1)\] and gradient-based Neural Architecture Search
(NAS) \[[31](#bib.bib12)\] to Transformer-based Hyperparameter
Optimization (HPO) \[[9](#bib.bib7)\]. More recently, the field has
increasingly embraced advanced generative paradigms, such as leveraging
multi-conditioned graph diffusion models \[[2](#bib.bib14)\] to sample
candidate neural structures. Despite these advancements, conventional
AutoML approaches remain inherently tailored to predefined,
task-specific search spaces. Furthermore, even contemporary generative
NAS models typically treat architecture search and training
configuration tuning as disjoint problems. This isolation fails to
capture the intricate synergies among heterogeneous components,
restricting their applicability in open-world scenarios involving unseen
modalities or structural shifts. In contrast to optimizing components in
silos, our framework introduces NCode to unify these heterogeneous
design choices into a shared numerical space, enabling holistic joint
optimization.

### II-B LLM-Based Automated Machine Learning.

Recent years have witnessed a growing interest in leveraging Large
Language Models (LLMs) for Neural Architecture Search (NAS) and
Automated Machine Learning (AutoML), with representative frameworks
including AutoML-GPT \[[54](#bib.bib56)\], LLMatic \[[33](#bib.bib2)\],
and CoLLM-NAS \[[29](#bib.bib57)\]. Despite these advances, existing
LLM-based searchers suffer from key limitations: they either rely
heavily on task-specific fine-tuning or formulate search as a direct
textual generation task, which is prone to producing invalid
configurations and exhibiting high learning complexity in vast discrete
spaces. While our preliminary work, LM-Searcher \[[21](#bib.bib3)\],
initiated the exploration of cross-domain NAS via unified numerical
encoding, it remained restricted to architecture-only search. To
overcome these bottlenecks, the proposed framework unifies heterogeneous
configurations into a discrete NCode space and reframes the search as a
conditional candidate ranking problem. This design achieves robust
zero-shot joint optimization of architectures, hyperparameters, and data
ratios across diverse domains and modalities without task-specific
adaptation.

### II-C Explainability and Chain-of-Thought Reasoning.

Traditional AutoML algorithms and neural search agents are frequently
criticized for their black-box nature, providing minimal intrinsic
rationale for their optimization trajectories \[[23](#bib.bib58)\].
While Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting \[[43](#bib.bib10)\] and
reinforcement learning algorithms like Group Relative Policy
Optimization (GRPO) \[[39](#bib.bib34)\] have boosted logical reasoning
in LLMs, applying self-explaining CoT to interpret AutoML search logic
remains under-explored. To bridge this gap, the proposed framework
leverages GRPO to post-train the LLM-based searcher to articulate its
reasoning steps in natural language. By taxonomizing the resulting CoT
outputs into distinct behavioral patterns, we provide interpretability
into LLM-driven optimization mechanisms.

## III The LLM-based Searcher framework

### III-A Overview

To enable the joint optimization of complex AutoML design choices
including model architectures, training hyperparameters, and data
ratios, we propose a universal, LLM-based open-world search framework.
The core of our framework is a unified numerical encoding scheme termed
NCode, which maps heterogeneous configurations into discrete sequences.
During the search phase, the LLM-based searcher iteratively analyzes a
historical buffer of evaluated NCode-reward pairs to autonomously
identify and select the most promising configuration from an unevaluated
candidate pool. Rather than relying on domain-specific finetuning, our
LLM-based searcher is trained to implicitly learn optimization
trajectories and reasoning strategies, allowing it to seamlessly
generalize to unseen configurations and modalities.

### III-B Universal NCode Representation

To unify the representation of heterogeneous AutoML design choices
(e.g., model architecture, training hyperparameters and data ratios) and
task domains, we uniformly encode all configurations into discrete
numerical sequences termed NCode. Formally, let a given AutoML search
space be denoted as $`\mathcal{S}`$. An AutoML configuration
$`a\in\mathcal{S}`$ can be defined by a set of $`n`$ decisions, which
may represent operational primitives, network connectivity, training
module-specific hyperparameters or data ratios. We define an encoding
function $`f:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}`$, where $`\mathcal{C}`$
is the universal space of NCode sequences. The function maps the
configuration to a string of discrete indices, denoted as
$`c=(c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{n})`$, where each element $`c_{i}`$ is a
numerical index corresponding to a selected configuration option of the
$`i`$-th configuration.

NCode enables the joint states of model topologies, operations, training
hyperparameters, and data ratios. To represent network topologies,
networks can be modeled as Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs), where each
edge represents an operation and each node represents a summed feature
map. For instance
(Fig. [1](#S1.F1 "Fig. 1 ‣ I Introduction ‣ Large Language Models as a Universal Searcher for Open-World Automated Machine Learning")b),
in the NAS-Bench-201 search space, each architecture is defined by
assigning one of five distinct operations to each of the six edges
within a cell. The NCode representation establishes a mapping between
these operations and a set of integers. Notably, by framing the search
as a black-box combinatorial optimization problem, NCode natively
extends to jointly encode other heterogeneous configurations.
Fig. [1](#S1.F1 "Fig. 1 ‣ I Introduction ‣ Large Language Models as a Universal Searcher for Open-World Automated Machine Learning")c
illustrates how NCode jointly encodes model architectures, training
hyperparameters, and data ratios.

optimization problem into a tractable ranking task, training the model
to select the candidate with the highest expected performance
conditioned on historical context.

### III-C Optimization Trajectory Curation

To effectively leverage LLMs as a general-purpose searcher for AutoML,
we curated a dataset of optimization trajectories to capture the
sequential, causal steps of iterative search processes. Specifically,
each optimization trajectory comprises a sequence of AutoML
configuration–reward pairs, with architectures represented by our
unified NCode scheme. At each iteration, the search algorithm utilizes
historically evaluated pairs as context to sample a new model
architecture, which is subsequently evaluated to derive its performance
metrics. A weighted sum of these metrics serves as the reward signal for
the given architecture.

Instead of collecting AutoML joint optimization samples directly, which
is time-consuming and computationally expensive, we sampled model-reward
pairs from the established NAS dataset NAS-Bench-301 that includes
approximately $`10^{21}`$ distinct model architectures with their
performance on CIFAR-10 classification task. To augment the diversity of
the architectural search space within our optimization data, we
introduced a pruning-based architecture sampling strategy that generates
varied sub-spaces from a single overarching search space. As illustrated
in
Fig. [3](#S3.F3 "Fig. 3 ‣ III-B Universal NCode Representation ‣ III The LLM-based Searcher framework ‣ Large Language Models as a Universal Searcher for Open-World Automated Machine Learning"),
the comprehensive architecture search space $`\mathcal{S}`$ is modeled
as a fully connected DAG. We independently prune each edge with a
probability $`p=0.5`$. This stochastic independent pruning inherently
defines a localized sub-search space
$`\mathcal{S}_{sub}\subset\mathcal{S}`$. Iterating this independent
pruning generates a multitude of distinct sub-search spaces, yielding a
training dataset with highly varied structural distributions.

Given the immense scale of the architectural search space, which
comprises approximately $`10^{21}`$ architectures, identifying a single
optimal architecture to serve as an absolute ground truth is
computationally intractable and poses a significant learning challenge
for the model. Consequently, we reframed the objective of the LLM-based
searcher from direct prediction to candidate ranking. Within each
isolated sub-space $`\mathcal{S}_{sub}`$, we randomly sample a set of
$`N`$ models (where $`N\in[100,200]`$) alongside their corresponding
performance metrics. The contextual history $`\mathcal{H}`$, comprising
a sequence of previously evaluated NCode representations and their
rewards, is provided to the LLM-based searcher. The model is then tasked
with selecting the most promising NCode from a candidate pool
$`\mathcal{C}`$ of unassessed configurations sampled from the same
sub-space.

Through this methodology, we curated a robust dataset comprising
approximately 228,000 optimization trajectory instances. This extensive
dataset boosts the LLM’s capacity to optimize AutoML configurations in a
domain-agnostic manner.

### III-D Interpretability through LLM CoT Reasoning

To unveil the LLM-based searcher’s reasoning process for AutoML
optimization, we utilized Group Relative Policy Optimization
(GRPO) \[[39](#bib.bib34)\] to fine-tune the
Qwen3-8B \[[49](#bib.bib33)\] LLM as self-explaining searcher which
learns to navigate the optimal AutoML configuration guided by its own
CoT reasoning. We then analyzed the CoT reasoning explained in natural
language on the NAS-Bench-201 \[[12](#bib.bib16)\] and
GenEval \[[18](#bib.bib32)\] datasets, extracting its reasoning
trajectories during the search phase for subsequent interpretability
analysis.

Policy Optimization for AutoML Search. We cast the LLM-based AutoML
search as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) designed to optimize
decision-making trajectories within the universal NCode space. For a
given search context $`x`$ (comprising historical trials and current
candidates), the searcher acts as a policy $`\pi_{\theta}`$ that
generates a token sequence $`y`$ representing the combined reasoning
(CoT) and the final configuration selection. To efficiently navigate
this complex discrete search space without the memory overhead of an
explicit value network, we implement Group Relative Policy Optimization
(GRPO) \[[39](#bib.bib34)\]. The reward for GRPO is a weighted sum of
accuracy reward, verifying the selection of the optimal NCode candidate,
and format reward, enforcing the structural integrity of the reasoning
process required for interpretability analysis.

Taxonomy of Chain-of-Thought Reasoning Behaviors We employ LLM-based
searcher with CoT capability to search on NAS-Bench-201 for image
classification and GenEval for text-to-image generation. Experiments
yielded 2,500 search steps complete with the model’s intermediate
reasoning process on each task. To systematically analyze the searcher’s
reasoning trajectories, we taxonomized the generated Chain-of-Thought
outputs into six distinct behavioral patterns. The definitions of these
patterns, as shown in
Fig. [4](#S3.F4 "Fig. 4 ‣ III-D Interpretability through LLM CoT Reasoning ‣ III The LLM-based Searcher framework ‣ Large Language Models as a Universal Searcher for Open-World Automated Machine Learning"),
are as follows:

- •
  Positional Anchoring: This pattern occurs when the searcher explicitly
  identifies that the presence of a specific operational digit at a
  designated sequence position is strongly correlated with extreme
  (either high or low) performance outcomes. The LLM uses this
  single-point heuristic as an anchor for subsequent candidate
  selection.
- •
  Global Component Distribution: This applies when the searcher bases
  its decision on the global distribution of operations. The model
  observes that the aggregate count of specific functional digits across
  the entire configuration sequence systematically correlates with
  overall model performance, regardless of their absolute positions.
- •
  Neighborhood Mutation: We define this behavior as an evolutionary
  search strategy where the LLM selects a historically high-performing
  configuration as a baseline (“parent”) and targets candidates that
  represent minimal structural perturbations (e.g., modifying only one
  or two sequence digits). This reflects an exploitation tactic focused
  on exploring the immediate local optimum neighborhood.
- •
  Combinatorial Synergy: This describes instances where the reasoning
  process emphasizes the combinatorial interaction between multiple
  positions. The searcher identifies that specific multi-digit
  combinations (e.g., a coupled pair of operations at positions 3 and 5)
  yield a synergistic effect on performance that cannot be explained by
  individual digits alone.
- •
  Hierarchical Sequence Progression: This categorizes behavior where the
  LLM detects monotonically increasing or decreasing numerical patterns
  within specific sub-regions of the configuration string. The model
  interprets these ordinal trends as reflections of smooth or abrupt
  hierarchical transitions in the network topology that systematically
  impact the target metric.
- •
  Operational Heterogeneity: This pattern emerges when the searcher
  evaluates candidates based on their digital heterogeneity. By
  observing that high-performing models typically encompass a broader
  vocabulary of unique functional digits compared to homogeneous,
  underperforming ones, the LLM deliberately favors configurations that
  integrate a diverse array of numerical components.

### III-E Zero-Shot Inference Pipeline

Once the LLM-based searcher is trained on the curated trajectory dataset
(as detailed in
Sec. [III-C](#S3.SS3 "III-C Optimization Trajectory Curation ‣ III The LLM-based Searcher framework ‣ Large Language Models as a Universal Searcher for Open-World Automated Machine Learning")).
It can be directly deployed to jointly optimize architectures,
hyperparameters, and data ratios for unseen domains, novel search
spaces, and distinct modalities without any task-specific gradient
updates. This zero-shot inference pipeline acts as a closed-loop Markov
Decision Process, illustrated in
Fig. [5](#S3.F5 "Fig. 5 ‣ III-D Interpretability through LLM CoT Reasoning ‣ III The LLM-based Searcher framework ‣ Large Language Models as a Universal Searcher for Open-World Automated Machine Learning"),
and comprises the following core stages:

1.  1.  Cold-Start Initialization: To initialize the historical context for
        the LLM, we employ a Random Search strategy to sample a small set of
        initial configurations (e.g., $`K_{\text{init}}=5`$). These
        configurations are mapped to their respective discrete sequences via
        the universal NCode $`\hat{f}`$. They are subsequently evaluated on
        the target task to obtain their performance metrics, which are
        aggregated and normalized into scalar rewards to form the initial
        historical buffer $`\mathcal{H}`$.
2.  2.  Candidate Pool Generation: At each search iteration, we generate a
        candidate pool $`\mathcal{C}`$ of unassessed configurations. To
        ensure both exploration and exploitation, this pool is populated by
        applying an evolutionary algorithm (i.e., structural mutations on
        historically high-performing NCodes) alongside random sampling.
3.  3.  LLM-Guided Reasoning and Selection: The current historical buffer
        $`\mathcal{H}`$ (comprising evaluated NCode-reward pairs) and the
        candidate pool $`\mathcal{C}`$ are integrated into a standardized
        prompt. The frozen LLM-based searcher processes this context,
        identifies and outputs the most promising candidate $`c^{*}`$ from
        the pool.
4.  4.  Proxy Evaluation and Context Update: To mitigate the prohibitive
        computational cost of fully training large-scale models during the
        search phase, we implement an early-stopping proxy evaluation
        protocol. The selected candidate is decoded back into the actual
        machine learning pipeline and trained for a minimal number of steps
        or epochs (e.g., 1 epoch for segmentation, 2500 steps for
        text-to-image synthesis). The derived surrogate reward $`r^{*}`$ is
        then paired with its NCode $`c^{*}`$ and appended to the historical
        buffer $`\mathcal{H}`$.
5.  5.  Bounded Buffer Management: To respect the LLM’s maximum token
        context window and maintain high-quality context, the capacity of
        the historical buffer is bounded. Once the number of evaluated pairs
        exceeds a predefined threshold (e.g., $`M=200`$), a filtering
        mechanism is triggered to retain only the top-$`M`$ performing
        configurations, discarding sub-optimal trials.

This iterative cycle continues until a predefined computational budget
is exhausted. The overall procedure is formalized in
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ III-D Interpretability through LLM CoT Reasoning ‣ III The LLM-based Searcher framework ‣ Large Language Models as a Universal Searcher for Open-World Automated Machine Learning").

## IV Experiments

### IV-A Overview

To comprehensively evaluate the effectiveness, zero-shot
transferability, scalability, and interpretability of our proposed
LLM-based searcher, we conduct extensive empirical evaluations across
four distinct machine learning scenarios. Specifically, we evaluate our
framework on: (1) Cross-Domain Image Segmentation to test PEFT design
and hyperparameter optimization under zero-shot domain shifts; (2)
Cross-Modality Speaker Verification to assess cross-modal generalization
from vision to audio; (3) Large-Scale Text-to-Image Synthesis to
demonstrate joint architecture, hyperparameter, and data-ratio search at
scale; and (4) NAS Benchmark (NAS-Bench-201) to verify standard neural
architecture search performance. For each paradigm, we present the
detailed experimental settings followed by the quantitative results.
Furthermore, we investigate the interpretability of the LLM-based
searcher’s internal decision-making process, supported by thorough
ablation studies and qualitative case studies. Below, we first outline
the common implementation details before detailing each specific task.

Implementation Details

- •
  LLM-based Searcher Training: To build the primary search agent, the
  LLaMA-3.1-8B base model \[[1](#bib.bib15)\] undergoes full-parameter
  supervised fine-tuning (SFT). The training is anchored on the curated
  dataset of approximately 228,000 optimization trajectory instances. To
  robustly capture expansive sequences of historical trial rewards
  alongside candidate configurations, we employ an extended context
  window with a strict token cutoff length of 20,480. The SFT process is
  executed for 3 epochs with a learning rate of $`1.0\times 10^{-5}`$,
  regulated by a cosine learning rate scheduler following a 10% warmup
  phase. To ensure memory efficiency during full-parameter optimization,
  training is distributed across a single node equipped with 8 GPUs
  using the DeepSpeed ZeRO-2 framework in bfloat16 mixed precision,
  configured with a per-device batch size of 1 and a gradient
  accumulation step of 2.

  To analyze the searcher’s internal decision-making process, we
  post-train a Qwen3-8B model using Group Relative Policy Optimization
  (GRPO). For this reinforcement learning stage, we subsample 2,000
  representative trajectory instances from the primary 228,000-instance
  dataset and execute policy training for 3 epochs. To facilitate
  relative advantage estimation within the algorithmic framework of
  GRPO, the policy model is configured to generate a group of 8
  independent candidate responses for each individual prompt sample. The
  computationally intensive training is scaled across a distributed
  4-node cluster equipped with 8 GPUs per node (totaling 32 GPUs),
  running the DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 framework with CPU offloading in bfloat16
  mixed precision. The execution configuration utilizes a per-device
  batch size of 1 matched with a gradient accumulation step of 4. To
  mitigate the immense token overhead of long-form Chain-of-Thought
  (CoT) generation while preserving search alignment, we employ
  Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) via Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)
  targeted at the attention projection modules (q_proj and v_proj). The
  maximum prompt and completion boundaries are constrained to 10,240 and
  8,192 tokens respectively, allowing sufficient capacity for structural
  rationalization. A vLLM inference engine is colocated with the
  training framework to accelerate group-based relative reward sampling.

- •
  Search Phase: To initialize the AutoML configuration search within our
  framework, we utilized Random Search to sample and evaluate five
  configurations. This generated an initial set of five NCode-reward
  pairs for the LLM-based searcher to analyze. In each subsequent
  iteration, the LLM proposed an NCode, which was then decoded into a
  specific training configuration. To ensure computational efficiency
  during searches for image segmentation and speaker verification, we
  applied early stopping after a single epoch and used the partially
  trained model to derive a surrogate performance measure. For
  large-scale text-to-image synthesis, we jointly searched model
  architectures, training hyperparameters, and data sampling ratios
  using a surrogate model scaled down to half the block depth and hidden
  dimension size of the target model, with training truncated at 2,500
  steps. The overall reward was calculated as the weighted average of
  all metrics and subsequently normalized across all evaluated
  configurations. Notably, for metrics where lower values denote
  superior performance, their contribution to the final reward was
  negated. Throughout the search, we maintained a historical buffer of
  up to 200 NCode-reward pairs. Once the evaluated pairs exceeded this
  threshold, we filtered the context provided to the LLM, retaining only
  the top-200 performing configurations.
- •
  Retraining Phase: Once the optimal configurations are identified by
  the LLM-based searcher during the early-stopping proxy evaluation, the
  selected configurations undergo a complete retraining phase for
  rigorous evaluation. Since these optimal configurations (encompassing
  architectures, hyperparameters, and data ratios) represent the direct
  outcomes of our search process, the specific retraining implementation
  details are inherently task-specific. Therefore, we detail the
  complete retraining setups within the respective Search Configuration
  sections of each task

### IV-B Cross-Domain Image Segmentation

#### IV-B1 Experimental Setting

- •
  Datasets. To evaluate the cross-task and cross-domain generalizability
  of our proposed LLM-based searcher, we utilized a diverse suite of
  image segmentation datasets encompassing multiple distinct domains.
  The search phase was conducted in the medical imaging domain using the
  Kvasir-SEG and CVC-612 datasets. Specifically, Kvasir-SEG and CVC-612
  evaluate the model’s capability in gastrointestinal polyp segmentation
  using endoscopic and colonoscopy imagery, respectively. To test
  zero-shot transferability, the optimal configuration discovered during
  search was evaluated on seven datasets across domains. In addition to
  the two datasets, these include ISIC2017, which focuses on the precise
  boundary delineation of skin lesions for melanoma detection; CAMO,
  which features natural imagery of camouflaged objects that artfully
  blend into their surroundings; SBU, which tasks the model with
  differentiating true structural boundaries from complex shadow masks
  in everyday scenes; Leaf, an agricultural dataset focused on
  delineating individual plant leaves from overlapping foliage
  backgrounds; and Road, which utilizes high-resolution remote sensing
  imagery to evaluate the extraction of continuous topological road
  networks across vast landscapes.
- •
  Search Configuration. The search space comprises both model
  architectures and training hyperparameters. For architecture, we used
  the Segment Anything Model (SAM) as the base segmenter and searched
  the PEFT design by assigning the ranks of 32 LoRA modules in the ViT
  encoder from $`\{3,6,12,24\}`$, yielding a search space of
  approximately $`10^{19}`$ architectures. The hyperparameter search
  space incorporates the focal loss $`\gamma`$, weight decay, and
  learning rate. To efficiently navigate this expansive space, we
  implement an early-stopping proxy evaluation protocol during the
  search phase, where each candidate configuration is trained for only 1
  epoch to derive a surrogate reward. The discovered optimal
  configuration is subsequently subjected to full retraining for 30
  epochs, establishing a highly efficient search-to-full-training
  workload ratio of 1:30. Comprehensive details regarding the search
  space are provided in Appendix Table 1. For the subsequent retraining
  phase, we applied the identified best-performing AutoML configurations
  to each downstream dataset. Specifically, we fine-tuned the Segment
  Anything Model (SAM) \[[25](#bib.bib21)\] using our optimal
  LoRA \[[20](#bib.bib19)\] configuration. Training was conducted on a
  single GPU, utilizing a global batch size of 4, a learning rate of
  $`1\times 10^{-3}`$, a weight decay of $`3\times 10^{-5}`$, and a
  $`\gamma`$ value of 2.0.
- •
  Evaluation Metrics. To comprehensively assess the zero-shot
  transferability of the searched configurations across diverse target
  domains, we employ a multi-faceted evaluation protocol tailored to the
  morphological characteristics of each dataset. For all metrics except
  the Balanced Error Rate (BER), higher values indicate superior
  performance.
  - –
    Structural and Alignment Metrics: For datasets requiring strict
    foreground object consistency and structural integrity (Kvasir-SEG,
    CVC-612, and CAMO), we utilize the S-measure
    ($`S_{\alpha}`$) \[[14](#bib.bib37)\] and Enhanced-alignment measure
    ($`E_{\phi}`$) \[[15](#bib.bib38)\].
  - –
    Spatial Overlap and Region Metrics: For tasks where exact spatial
    overlap is the most critical success factor (ISIC2017, Leaf, and
    Road), we rely on the widely adopted Jaccard Index (Jac).
    Additionally, the weighted F-measure ($`F_{\beta}^{\omega}`$) is
    evaluated across multiple datasets to adaptively emphasize errors in
    highly salient regions.
  - –
    Balanced Error Metrics: The Balanced Error Rate (BER) is
    specifically applied to evaluate the performance on the SBU dataset.
    It ensures an unbiased assessment for shadow detection by equally
    weighting the classification error rates of both shadow and
    non-shadow regions.

  Detailed mathematical formulations for all the aforementioned
  evaluation metrics are provided in Appendix Section 2.

### IV-C Cross-Modality Speaker Verification

#### IV-C1 Experimental Setting

- •
  Datasets. We validated the cross-modality generalization capability of
  our LLM-based searcher on the audio modality evaluation suite
  3D-Speaker-Toolkit \[[8](#bib.bib36)\]. The 3D-Speaker corpus is a
  large-scale dataset containing over 10,000 speakers, which are
  partitioned into training and test sets. We evaluated the models
  across three challenging tracks: multi-device, multi-distance, and
  multi-dialect. The evaluation split comprises 240 unseen speakers,
  18,782 utterances, 11 dialects, and 15.42 hours of speech.
- •
  Search Configuration. Our framework navigates a comprehensive search
  space that comprises both model architectures and training
  hyperparameters for the speaker verification task. The architectural
  search space dictates the model’s capacity and feature resolution,
  specifically targeting the Mel-filterbank input dimensions, the base
  channel width of the ERes2NetV2 backbone, and the final speaker
  embedding size. Concurrently, the hyperparameter search space includes
  the data augmentation probability, learning rate, optimizer momentum,
  and weight decay. To maintain computational efficiency across this
  joint space, we implement an early-stopping proxy evaluation protocol
  during the search phase, truncating each candidate’s training at only
  1 epoch to derive a surrogate reward. The discovered optimal
  configuration is subsequently subjected to full retraining for a total
  of 77 epochs—comprising 70 epochs of standard training followed by a
  7-epoch large-margin fine-tuning stage—establishing a highly efficient
  search-to-full-training workload ratio of approximately 1:77. The
  exact search space for each design choice is detailed in Appendix
  Table 1. For the retraining phase, the optimal model architecture was
  trained on the 3D-Speaker-Toolkit dataset for 70 epochs. We employed a
  cosine learning rate scheduler with an initial learning rate of 0.3, a
  weight decay of $`5\times 10^{-5}`$, a momentum of 0.85, and a data
  augmentation probability of 0.4. The resulting model was subsequently
  fine-tuned utilizing a large-margin strategy for an additional seven
  epochs, maintaining the same hyperparameter settings.
- •
  Evaluation Metrics. We assessed the performance of the discovered
  configurations utilizing two standard metrics within the speaker
  verification domain: the Equal Error Rate (EER) and the minimum of the
  normalized detection cost function (minDCF). For both metrics, lower
  values signify superior detection and recognition performance. The
  optimal configuration was fully retrained and evaluated across five
  independent runs, from which we report the mean and standard
  deviation.

### IV-D Large-Scale Text-to-Image Synthesis

#### IV-D1 Experimental Setting

- •
  Heterogeneous Training Corpus. To train the DiT model, we constructed
  a diverse image-text corpus and dynamically search for the optimal
  data distribution ratios during training. This corpus amalgamates
  several high-quality datasets, each providing image-caption pairs with
  unique visual-semantic priors:
  - –
    BLIP3o (Short, Long, JourneyDB, and 60k
    variants) \[[4](#bib.bib28)\]: Provides highly descriptive,
    synthetically enriched captions that enhance fine-grained semantic
    alignment.
  - –
    LAION-high-resolution \[[38](#bib.bib29)\]: A large-scale,
    web-scraped dataset ensuring broad coverage of real-world concepts
    and natural image distributions.
  - –
    Midjourney-23m-2: Comprises high-fidelity, artistically curated
    synthetic images to elevate the overall aesthetic quality of the
    generated outputs.
  - –
    X2I2 \[[45](#bib.bib30)\]: A dataset designed to improve complex
    instruction-guided generation.
  - –
    Echo-4o \[[50](#bib.bib31)\]: A synthetic dataset generated by
    GPT-4o, providing clean supervision to complement rare scenarios and
    complex compositions less represented in real-world data.

- •
  Search Configuration. Our LLM-based searcher navigates a unified
  configuration space to jointly optimize the model architecture,
  training hyperparameters, and data sampling ratios. Architecturally,
  we adopt the MM-DiT as the foundational backbone, searching over the
  hidden dimension size, as well as the depths of the single- and
  double-stream blocks. The hyperparameter search space encompasses the
  learning rate, weight decay, and the optimizer parameters $`\beta`$
  and $`\epsilon`$. Finally, to optimize the training data composition,
  the sampling weight for each dataset during training is independently
  tuned within a range of 1.0 to 6.0. To scale this framework to the
  immense computational demands of large-scale pre-training, we execute
  the search phase using a surrogate model scaled down to half the block
  depth and hidden dimension size of the target model, with training
  truncated at 2,500 steps. The optimal configuration is then scaled up
  to the full 1.3-billion-parameter architecture and trained for a total
  of 100,000 steps across three distinct resource-intensive stages,
  establishing a highly efficient search-to-full-training step ratio of
  approximately 1:40. Detailed descriptions of the search space are
  provided in Appendix Table 3. For the full retraining phase, we
  conducted a large-scale pre-training experiment, training a
  1.3-billion-parameter model across 128 GPUs for approximately seven
  days. The training process consisted of three stages. In the first
  stage, all training images were resized to a $`256\times 256`$
  resolution. The model was trained for 85,000 steps on
  BLIP3o-JourneyDB, BLIP3o-Long, BLIP3o-Short, LAION-high-resolution,
  Midjourney-23m-2, and X2I2, with data sampling ratios of 1.0, 2.0,
  3.0, 5.0, 1.0, and 4.0, respectively. We utilized a learning rate of
  $`1\times 10^{-3}`$, a $`\beta_{2}`$ parameter of 0.99, an
  $`\epsilon`$ of $`1\times 10^{-15}`$, and a weight decay of 0.1. In
  the second stage, we scaled the image resolution to $`512\times 512`$
  and continually trained the model for 5,000 steps using identical
  hyperparameters and data ratios. Finally, the model was fine-tuned on
  the BLIP3o-60k and Echo-4o datasets for 10,000 steps.
- •
  Evaluation Metrics. We benchmarked the optimized DiT model using
  GenEval \[[18](#bib.bib32)\] to rigorously assess fine-grained
  compositional alignment at the instance level. We systematically
  evaluated the model across fundamental visual primitives, including
  single/multi-object rendering, object counting, spatial positioning,
  coloring, and attribute binding. The overall GenEval score is reported
  as the unweighted macro-average of the accuracies across these
  distinct sub-tasks, ensuring equal evaluation weight for each
  compositional skill. For evaluation, we generate four samples per
  prompt using an inference step number of 50 and a Classifier-Free
  Diffusion Guidance (CFG) scale of 5.0.

### IV-E NAS Benchmark

#### IV-E1 Experimental Setting

- •
  Datasets. While traditional NAS methods are highly specialized for
  image classification tasks, we benchmark our approach on NAS-Bench-201
  to verify that our domain-agnostic LLM-based searcher matches their
  performance in standard image classification, serving as a

  within the NAS-Bench-201 search space has been fully trained and
  evaluated across three distinct image classification datasets:
  CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-16-120. By querying this predefined

  training candidate architectures from scratch.
- •

  algorithm-agnostic, fixed search space based on a neural cell topology

  five predefined operation options (zeroize, skip connection, 1-by-1

  yields a comprehensive search space of 15,625 unique neural
  architecture candidates.
- •
  Evaluation Protocol. We executed five independent search trials of 200
  steps per dataset. The reported metrics represent the average
  classification accuracy across the five top-tier architectures, each
  selected based on the highest validation accuracy attained during its
  individual run.

### IV-F Interpretability of LLM-based Searcher’s Reasoning Rationales

#### IV-F1 Experimental Setting

To unveil the proposed LLM-based searcher’s reasoning process in
optimizing heterogeneous AutoML configurations, we post-train a Qwen3-8B
model with CoT capabilities using our curated optimization trajectory
dataset with GRPO
(Fig. [2](#S2.F2 "Fig. 2 ‣ II-C Explainability and Chain-of-Thought Reasoning. ‣ II Related Work ‣ Large Language Models as a Universal Searcher for Open-World Automated Machine Learning")).
Crucially, as validated in our ablation studies
(Section [IV-G](#S4.SS7 "IV-G Ablation Studies ‣ IV-F2 Experimental Results ‣ IV-F Interpretability of LLM-based Searcher’s Reasoning Rationales ‣ IV-E2 Experimental Results ‣ IV-E NAS Benchmark ‣ IV-D2 Experimental Results ‣ IV-D Large-Scale Text-to-Image Synthesis ‣ IV-C2 Experimental Results ‣ IV-C Cross-Modality Speaker Verification ‣ IV-B2 Experimental Results ‣ IV-B Cross-Domain Image Segmentation ‣ IV Experiments ‣ Large Language Models as a Universal Searcher for Open-World Automated Machine Learning")),
this Qwen3-8B-based searcher exhibits performance parity with our
primary LLaMA-3.1-8B backbone, establishing it as a faithful proxy to
decode the framework’s decision-making logic. We employed the LLM-based
searcher with CoT capability to search for image classification on
NAS-Bench-201 and text-to-image synthesis on GenEval, generating
optimization trajectories alongside natural language reasoning processes
for explainability. These “reasoning processes” were then taxonomized
using Gemini-2.5-Pro to identify six major optimization strategies
learned by the LLM-based searcher
(Fig. [7](#S4.F7 "Fig. 7 ‣ IV-E2 Experimental Results ‣ IV-E NAS Benchmark ‣ IV-D2 Experimental Results ‣ IV-D Large-Scale Text-to-Image Synthesis ‣ IV-C2 Experimental Results ‣ IV-C Cross-Modality Speaker Verification ‣ IV-B2 Experimental Results ‣ IV-B Cross-Domain Image Segmentation ‣ IV Experiments ‣ Large Language Models as a Universal Searcher for Open-World Automated Machine Learning")):
Positional Anchoring (e.g., fixing high-performing digit at critical
position), Combinatorial Synergy (e.g., combinational effect of digits
across positions), Neighborhood Mutation (e.g., making slight
perturbations on evaluated NCode), Global Component Distribution (e.g.,
tracking the occurrence of specific digit), Hierarchical Sequence
Progression (increasing/decreasing trends at local regions) and
Operational Heterogeneity (diversity of digits in NCodes).

#### IV-F2 Experimental Results

Subsequent analysis of strategy distributions, state transitions, and
temporal dynamics reveals that the LLM intrinsically develops a
sophisticated exploration-exploitation paradigm, progressively shifting
from broad structural exploration to the greedy exploitation of key
architectural anchors.

Analyzing the temporal evolution of these reasoning patterns unveils a
reasonable search logic rather than a random application of strategies
(Fig. [7](#S4.F7 "Fig. 7 ‣ IV-E2 Experimental Results ‣ IV-E NAS Benchmark ‣ IV-D2 Experimental Results ‣ IV-D Large-Scale Text-to-Image Synthesis ‣ IV-C2 Experimental Results ‣ IV-C Cross-Modality Speaker Verification ‣ IV-B2 Experimental Results ‣ IV-B Cross-Domain Image Segmentation ‣ IV Experiments ‣ Large Language Models as a Universal Searcher for Open-World Automated Machine Learning")).
Initially, the searcher predominantly employs Neighborhood Mutation to
navigate the configuration space, selecting candidate NCodes that
introduce localized structural perturbations to historically
high-performing baselines. As the search progresses, the probability of
utilizing this Neighborhood Mutation gradually diminishes, giving way to
exploitative patterns that leverage historical context. Crucially, this
transition adapts to the complexity of the target task. On the simpler
NAS-Bench-201 image classification benchmark, the searcher gradually
shifts to the Positional Anchoring strategy, indicating a learned
tendency to fix specific, high-yielding modules at critical topological
positions. Conversely, on the highly complex GenEval text-to-image
synthesis task, the model gradually shifts toward Combinatorial Synergy.
By focusing on multi-digit patterns within NCodes, it demonstrates an
ability to untangle the intricate combinatorial effects of heterogeneous
configurations.

Furthermore, the trajectory of Global Component Distribution, wherein
the searcher evaluates configurations based on the macro-level frequency
of specific operational digits rather than their absolute positions,
reveals distinct adaptations. In the homogeneous NAS-Bench-201 search,
the utilization of this strategy progressively intensifies, scaling up
from an initial 10% to nearly 20%. In sharp contrast, for the
heterogeneous GenEval task, this pattern is kept consistently subdued,
hovering steadily at a probability of approximately 5%. This divergence
offers compelling evidence of the system’s open-world generalizability:
while the model learns to capitalize on global frequency heuristics as a
stable descriptor for homogeneous model architecture search, it
correctly recognizes that joint optimization across architecture, data,
and hyperparameters demands a strict departure from simple global
counting. Ultimately, these dynamics demonstrate that the LLM-based
searcher does not merely memorize patterns; rather, it effectively
navigates search spaces by adaptively combining different strategies to
pinpoint optimal configurations.

To validate the effectiveness of our approach and the resulting
configurations, we compared our method against several baselines. As
shown in

circumvent the substantial computational overhead associated with
token-heavy CoT generation, we deploy the LLaMA-3.1-8B searcher to
execute all primary search environments reported in this study.
Concurrently, the Qwen3-8B searcher is leveraged exclusively for the
interpretability analysis to unpack and systematically interpret the
internal decision-making mechanisms and temporal strategic transitions
of the universal search agent.

## V Conclusion and Future Work

In this paper, we presented a universal LLM-based AutoML framework
designed to jointly optimize model architectures, training
hyperparameters, and data ratios across open-world scenarios without
task-specific fine-tuning. By introducing NCode, a unified numerical
encoding scheme, we mapped heterogeneous design choices into universal
discrete sequences and reframed the optimization process as a
conditional candidate ranking problem. Trained exclusively on trajectory
data curated from a single-domain vision dataset, our searcher
demonstrated remarkable zero-shot generalizability across seven domains,
four tasks, and three modalities (vision, text, and audio). Experimental
results validated its state-of-the-art efficiency and scalability,
notably optimizing a 1.3-billion-parameter Diffusion Transformer to
attain a GenEval score of 0.91.

Furthermore, by instilling Chain-of-Thought reasoning through Group
Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), we demystified the searcher’s
internal decision-making mechanisms, revealing an adaptive transition
from early-stage diversity-driven exploration to late-stage anchor
exploitation. This work establishes a scalable, generalizable, and
interpretable pathway toward autonomous, general-purpose machine
learning optimization. Future work will explore extending the searcher
to Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to integrate direct visual rewards for
richer generative quality assessment.
