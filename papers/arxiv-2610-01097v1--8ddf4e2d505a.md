###### Abstract

End-to-end research agents can now produce complete scientific papers,
yet manuscript claims often diverge from executed experiments. This gap
is structural: research state, failure histories, and claim–evidence
alignment are not maintained as persistent, verifiable state across
long-horizon pipelines. We present YouRA (Your Research Agent), an
architecture for stateful, evidence-traceable autonomous research. YouRA
preserves research state, execution evidence, and failure history across
the research trajectory by integrating three components: a Verification
State Architecture (VSA) that tracks hypotheses, gates, and evidence
pointers; an Independent Controller that turns state and reflection
records into lifecycle, recovery, and debate/review control while
separating control from execution; and Stateful Reflection that logs
failures as structured lessons and routes recovery through bounded
repair, redesign, or reset. On MLR-Bench’s predefined ten-task
end-to-end subset, YouRA improves over both MLR-Agent and
AI Scientist V2 on scalar Overall across all three matched backbones. An
automated diagnostic using MLR-Bench’s hallucination taxonomy reports
intersection/union counts for four fact-based failure types, and
data-provenance diagnostic shows more real-data-based outputs. Ablating
each of the four components (the VSA, the Independent Controller, MCP
tool access, and reflection-guided recovery) supports their separable
contributions. Removing either core-state component drops YouRA below
the full system. Code:
[https://github.com/PrayPrey/Your-Research-Agent](https://github.com/PrayPrey/Your-Research-Agent).

## 1 Introduction

Autonomous research agents that formulate hypotheses, run experiments,
and write papers have been pursued for decades, from closed-loop
experimentation ([King et al., 2009](#bib.bib1)) through proposals for
AI-amplified discovery ([Gil et al., 2014](#bib.bib2)) to explicit
Nobel-level challenges ([Kitano, 2021](#bib.bib3)). Large language
models have made this vision practical: AI Scientist ([Lu et al.,
2024](#bib.bib4)) and its tree-search successor V2 ([Yamada et al.,
2025](#bib.bib5)) automate the full pipeline from ideation to paper
writing, AI-Researcher ([Tang et al., 2025](#bib.bib6)) demonstrates
role-separated multi-agent collaboration, Agent Laboratory ([Schmidgall
et al., 2025](#bib.bib7)) integrates human feedback, and VIRSCI ([Su et
al., 2025](#bib.bib8)) and AI Co-scientist ([Gottweis et al.,
2025](#bib.bib9)) refine ideas through multi-agent debate.
Context-management strategies differ across these systems, but none
externalizes the research trajectory as explicit, persistent, verifiable
state spanning the full pipeline. That gap limits reliability over long
horizons.

This gap appears in three concrete limitations on the reliability of
automatically generated research. (1) Context and evidence
fragmentation. When research state is implicitly embedded in
conversation histories, it is vulnerable to truncation and
hallucination, which breaks long-horizon coherence across hypotheses,
experiments, conclusions, and manuscript claims ([Yan et al.,
2026](#bib.bib19)). Current agents also provide only limited mechanisms
for ensuring that manuscript claims remain aligned with executed code,
result files, and logs. As a result, a paper can contain superficially
persuasive claims generated from hallucinated results or failed runs,
and such claims may still appear strong under output-only judging that
does not inspect the underlying artifacts. (2) Lack of structured
failure memory. Agents lack mechanisms to record, diagnose, and learn
from experimental failures across stages. MLR-Bench’s hallucination
analysis reports that evaluated coding agents produced fabricated or
unverified experimental results in roughly 80% of cases, with case
studies tracing such failures to execution errors masked by simulated
outputs ([Chen et al., 2026](#bib.bib12)). (3) Unreliable workflow
control. In long-horizon research agents, the same task-execution
dialogue often both performs the work and decides how the workflow
should continue. It carries code-writing and paper-writing context
together with control decisions such as when to stop, retry, redesign a
hypothesis, or move to manuscript review. As this dialogue grows, these
control decisions become vulnerable to truncation, drift, and
inconsistent recall, making it difficult to resume or recover a run from
the actual research state.

All three limitations trace back to the same absence: the research
trajectory is not maintained as explicit, persistent, verifiable
structure across the pipeline. Two pieces are missing. One is a shared
state substrate that records hypotheses, gates, failures, and evidence
pointers. The other is an external control surface that uses this state
to resume, redirect, and review the research lifecycle.

To address these problems, we present YouRA (Your Research Agent), an
architecture for stateful, evidence-traceable autonomous research that
preserves research state, execution evidence, and trajectory-level
failure history as explicit, persistent structure
(Figure [1](#S2.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ Limitations of Output-Only Benchmarks. ‣ 2 Related Work ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")).
Its three components are: (i) a Verification State Architecture (VSA)
that centrally manages hypotheses, gates, and evidence pointers; (ii) an
Independent Controller, which consumes VSA and reflection records to
drive lifecycle transitions, recovery escalation, and multi-agent
debate/review roles while separating high-level control from task
execution; and (iii) Stateful Reflection, which logs failures as
structured lessons and routes recovery through bounded repair, redesign,
or reset. Given only a topic-level description, YouRA autonomously
expands it into a research question, hypothesis, experiment plan,
implementation, validation evidence, and final manuscript. In practical
research use, the same architecture supports dynamic researcher steering
through stage-specific CLI commands for redirecting, restarting, or
redesigning a run; for evaluation as an autonomous research agent,
however, all benchmark runs in this paper are held to fully autonomous
mode.

We evaluate on MLR-Bench ([Chen et al., 2026](#bib.bib12)), a benchmark
designed for open-ended ML research agents that provides 201
workshop-derived research tasks, stepwise and end-to-end evaluation
protocols, and structured MLR-Judge rubrics. It directly matches YouRA’s
problem setting because it evaluates the full path from ideation through
experimentation to paper writing, and explicitly surfaces fabricated or
unverified experimental results as a central reliability failure.
Following the benchmark’s execution-heavy paper-level setting, our
end-to-end and pairwise comparisons use its predefined ten-task subset.
On this subset, YouRA obtains the highest 4-judge end-to-end Overall
among all systems on every matched backbone, outperforming both
MLR-Agent and AI Scientist V2 ([Yamada et al., 2025](#bib.bib5)).
Order-controlled pairwise preferences also favor YouRA over both
baselines on every reported backbone. Beyond the rankings, an automated
diagnostic based on MLR-Bench’s hallucination taxonomy decomposes
concrete failure modes across matched backbones, and the data-provenance
diagnostic across matched backbones shows that YouRA outputs more often
rely on real data. Ablations of each of the four components support
their separable contributions. Ablations of all four components
significantly reduce Overall, while pairwise comparisons of their drop
magnitudes do not support a reliable ranking among components.

YouRA’s contribution is to make research state, execution evidence, and
the research trajectory explicit, durable objects in autonomous research
rather than byproducts of transient dialogue context: hypotheses,
failures, evidence pointers, and recovery decisions persist in shared
state and Reflective Memory, where later agents can read them. VSA-based
state externalization supplies the record, the Independent Controller
resumes, redirects, and reviews the lifecycle from that record while
keeping control separate from execution, and Stateful Reflection drives
failure-based recovery. YouRA also carries an evaluation perspective: an
autonomous research system should be judged on whether its final claims
remain traceable to executed evidence and the research trajectory that
produced them, and a persuasive manuscript alone should not settle that
judgment.

## 2 Related Work

#### End-to-End Autonomous Research.

Recent end-to-end systems fall along three architectural axes.
Single-pipeline designs chain ideation through paper writing into a
fixed flow [Lu et al. (2024)](#bib.bib4); V2 extends exploration depth
via agentic tree search [Yamada et al. (2025)](#bib.bib5).
Role-separated multi-agent designs specialize agents per stage [Tang et
al. (2025)](#bib.bib6), but do not make a shared persistent state the
coordinating substrate for long-horizon coherence.
Human-feedback-integrated designs accept researcher feedback at stage
transitions [Schmidgall et al. (2025)](#bib.bib7), but restrict
intervention to predefined checkpoints. Across these approaches, the
research trajectory is still not carried between stages as durable,
verifiable state, and failures never become structured constraints on
later work. This is the architectural gap behind the three limitations
identified in the Introduction.

#### Collaborative Systems and Persistent State.

A parallel line of work explores human–AI collaboration for research
quality. VIRSCI [Su et al. (2025)](#bib.bib8) refines hypotheses through
team-based multi-agent discussion, and AI Co-scientist [Gottweis et al.
(2025)](#bib.bib9) combines Elo-ranked tournament evolution with
researcher guidance, yet both stop at the pre-experimentation ideation
stage. Enterprise Deep Research [Prabhakar et al. (2025)](#bib.bib11)
introduces steerable multi-agent deep research with human-in-the-loop
guidance for analytical report generation, but targets information
synthesis and does not run hypothesis-testing pipelines with code
execution. YouRA targets a different setting: hypothesis-testing
pipelines where state must be preserved across ideation, implementation,
failure recovery, and writing. The most directly relevant mechanism for
learning from failures is Reflexion [Shinn et al. (2023)](#bib.bib15),
which applies per-task verbal reinforcement within an episodic memory
buffer scoped to a single task. YouRA extends this principle to a
structurally different regime. Failure lessons persist across
heterogeneous research stages, from hypothesis redesign to full
research-question reset, and are coordinated through the VSA instead of
being held in a single-task buffer. For hypothesis generation, YouRA
builds on the multi-agent scientific-debate paradigm from VIRSCI [Su et
al. (2025)](#bib.bib8) and AI Co-scientist [Gottweis et al.
(2025)](#bib.bib9), but couples it to VSA state and Reflective Memory so
debate outputs remain tied to downstream verification.

#### Evaluation and Reliability.

Research-agent reliability benchmarks now span multiple scopes.
MLAgentBench [Huang et al. (2024)](#bib.bib14) measures ML
experimentation success. DeepResearch Bench [Du et al.
(2026)](#bib.bib10) targets report quality; FML-bench [Zou et al.
(2026)](#bib.bib13) evaluates exploration breadth; and MLR-Bench [Chen
et al. (2026)](#bib.bib12) assesses the full ML-research pipeline from
ideation to paper writing. MLR-Bench demonstrates that agents can mask
execution failures with simulated or placeholder results, exposing a gap
between fluent paper generation and trustworthy experimentation. Failure
analyses trace related reliability problems to weak factual grounding
and insufficient intermediate verification [Zhang et al.
(2025)](#bib.bib16). YouRA targets this gap: it preserves failures as
structured records that constrain subsequent stages, and embeds
evidence-checking mechanisms before and after paper generation
(§[3](#S3 "3 Methodology ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")).

#### Limitations of Output-Only Benchmarks.

Output-only evaluation of final papers has known limits: reference-free
LLM judges show high agreement with human experts only on questions the
judges themselves can answer ([Krumdick et al., 2026](#bib.bib20)),
paper–code alignment benchmarks reveal that even top models detect fewer
than half of real discrepancies between papers and their implementations
([Baumgärtner and Gurevych, 2026](#bib.bib18)), and coding agents lose
implementation fidelity when task specifications emerge incrementally
over long horizons ([Yan et al., 2026](#bib.bib19)). These limitations
connect directly to the first problem raised in the Introduction,
context and evidence fragmentation: protocols that evaluate only the
final paper can structurally miss gaps between manuscript claims and
executed experiments ([Liu and Zhu, 2026](#bib.bib30)). We therefore use
MLR-Bench’s hallucination taxonomy to inspect concrete fact-based
failure types in final papers, and add a separate data-provenance
diagnostic that summarizes whether manuscripts rely on real, synthetic,
or fabricated artifacts. These diagnostics complement the primary
benchmark rankings by exposing claim–evidence audit signals that scalar
output scores can miss
(§[5](#S5 "5 Discussion ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")).

## 3 Methodology

Having identified the absence of persistent research state as the common
root of these limitations
(§[1](#S1 "1 Introduction ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")–[2](#S2 "2 Related Work ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")),
we now describe how YouRA addresses each one through a dedicated
architectural component. These structural mechanisms are intrinsic to
YouRA’s architecture and cannot be grafted onto existing research agents
as standalone modules; the individual effect of each component is
analyzed in detail in the ablation studies
(§[4.3](#S4.SS3 "4.3 Ablation Studies ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")).
Here, state externalization means storing research state (hypotheses,
gates, failures, and evidence pointers) in structured files instead of
conversation history, so each lifecycle stage starts from a stable
record. YouRA implements this through three components that together
address the three limitations from
§[1](#S1 "1 Introduction ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents"):
the Verification State Architecture
(§[3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 Verification State Architecture ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents"))
addresses context and evidence fragmentation by preserving hypotheses,
gates, and evidence pointers; the Independent Controller addresses
unreliable workflow control by consuming VSA and reflection records to
drive lifecycle transitions, recovery routing, and multi-agent
hypothesis debate
(§[3.3](#S3.SS3 "3.3 Independent Controller ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents"));
and Stateful Reflection
(§[3.4](#S3.SS4 "3.4 Stateful Reflection ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents"))
addresses the lack of structured failure memory through three-level
failure routing. In implementation, procedural context such as reusable
stage templates, reference documents, and modular skills is linked by
explicit file pointers, so each stage loads the relevant context on
demand.
Figure [1](#S2.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ Limitations of Output-Only Benchmarks. ‣ 2 Related Work ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")
illustrates the full lifecycle, and
§[3.1](#S3.SS1 "3.1 Collaborative Research Lifecycle Overview ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")
summarizes the stage sequence, including the evidence-checking and
refinement passes that connect experimental execution to manuscript
writing.

### 3.1 Collaborative Research Lifecycle Overview

The workflow comprises ten named stages under VSA-controlled transitions
(Figure [1](#S2.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ Limitations of Output-Only Benchmarks. ‣ 2 Related Work ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")).
Problem Scoping and Literature Grounding turn a broad interest into
grounded research gaps; Hypothesis Debate uses a six-persona debate
protocol to converge on a hypothesis; and Verification Planning converts
that hypothesis into a dependency-aware directed acyclic graph (DAG)
plan and gated sub-hypotheses. The subsequent Hypothesis Loop iterates
through Experiment Design, Implementation Planning, and Coding &
Validation, turning each sub-hypothesis into an experiment
specification, implementation plan, and validated code. Evidence Check
records which predictions and assumptions are consistent with executed
results before Manuscript Drafting uses a section-grouped drafting
protocol (Story Group Architecture;
Appendix [A](#A1 "Appendix A System Implementation Details ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents"))
to produce the paper. Adversarial Fact Review checks the generated
manuscript against the VSA and accumulated stage documents, and a final
Manuscript Refinement pass integrates the review output with prior
artifacts, results, logs, and code to improve logical consistency,
evidential alignment, and narrative clarity. Stage-level mechanics and
recovery decision logic are detailed in
Appendix [A](#A1 "Appendix A System Implementation Details ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents").

### 3.2 Verification State Architecture

YouRA’s first component targets context and evidence fragmentation. In
conventional agent loops, state is implicitly embedded in conversation
history, where it is vulnerable to truncation and hallucination. YouRA
instead maintains research state through a YAML-based Verification State
Architecture (VSA). A central verification_state.yaml tracks macro
pipeline state (hypotheses, gate results, routing decisions, evidence
pointers, and a failed-hypotheses registry), and per-stage checkpoint
files complement it. Later stages read gate outcomes, evidence pointers,
and failure records from the VSA, so executed experiments become
explicit constraints when hypotheses are revised, validated, or written
into manuscript claims. The VSA is a single persistent state record
under three invariants: gate-driven progression (a hypothesis advances
only when its MUST_WORK gates are satisfied), auditable history (every
mutation is timestamped and attributed), and steering as mutation
(researcher directives become VSA field updates).

### 3.3 Independent Controller

YouRA’s second component turns persistent state into control. The VSA
stores the research trajectory, and Stateful Reflection records failure
constraints and routing history; the independent controller consumes
these records to choose the next action. Concretely, the controller
supports three roles: lifecycle continuation, selecting the next stage
from VSA gate status; recovery escalation, consuming Stateful Reflection
records to choose repair, redesign, or reset boundaries; and independent
debate/review, used by Hypothesis Debate and Adversarial Fact Review. By
keeping control in a separate context from task execution, YouRA avoids
making the execution conversation itself the only source of control
state. The controller therefore reads compact VSA/artifact summaries
rather than the full trace and emits continuation context at stop or
human-in-the-loop boundaries.

Prior systems such as VIRSCI and AI Co-scientist provide functional
precedents for multi-agent scientific debate in hypothesis
generation ([Su et al., 2025](#bib.bib8); [Gottweis et al.,
2025](#bib.bib9)). YouRA does not adopt their pipelines; it implements
the analogous debate/review function inside its own stateful
architecture, through a Hypothesis Debate protocol tied to the VSA and
Reflective Memory. Six research personas debate candidate hypotheses
while the controller LLM and task-execution LLM alternate persona
contributions; Reflective Memory turns refuted approaches into
constraints and validated or partially validated elements into reusable
evidence. The debate terminates once specificity, mechanism clarity,
testable predictions, novelty, feasibility, and objection resolution are
jointly satisfied; the converged hypothesis is formalized and decomposed
into 3–7 gated sub-hypotheses in the VSA.

### 3.4 Stateful Reflection

YouRA’s third component, Stateful Reflection, turns failure signals into
structured constraints that later stages can use. It operates over
Reflective Memory
(§[3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 Verification State Architecture ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents"))
during the hypothesis loop: routing-relevant outcomes are recorded with
structured reasons, supporting or refuting evidence, and pivot-or-reuse
rationales, so refuted approaches become negative constraints while
validated and partially validated components become reusable evidence
for successor stages. Recovery proceeds in three levels. Bounded repair
first handles local Coding & Validation failures, and after each
execution an independent mock-data and hard-coded-result detector checks
whether reported outputs are derived from actual data processing;
detected fabrications trigger repair before evidence is passed
downstream. If a MUST_WORK gate remains unsatisfied after repair,
Stateful Reflection escalates to Hypothesis Debate redesign when
mechanism revision remains recoverable, or to Problem Scoping reset when
the current hypothesis contradicts executed evidence. Each
sub-hypothesis terminates when its gates resolve through validation,
refutation, or escalation, after which the final state is recorded and
the pipeline proceeds to Evidence Check. Routing telemetry is reported
in Appendix
Table [9](#A3.T9 "Table 9 ‣ Appendix C Operational Telemetry ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")
and
Appendix [C](#A3 "Appendix C Operational Telemetry ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents").

### 4.1 Setup

#### Benchmark.

We evaluate on MLR-Bench [Chen et al. (2026)](#bib.bib12), a benchmark
for open-ended ML research agents that provides 201 workshop-derived
research tasks, MLR-Judge rubrics, and the MLR-Agent baseline pipeline
for idea generation, proposal formulation, experimentation, and paper
writing. The 201 tasks are sourced from recent ICLR/ICML/NeurIPS
workshops and are categorized by MLR-Bench into nine core ML topics:
LLMs and Vision-Language Models, AI for Science, ML Theory, Trustworthy
AI, Computer Vision, ML Systems, Multimodality, Reinforcement Learning,
and other emerging topics. End-to-end and pairwise paper-level
evaluation use the predefined ten-task subset specified by MLR-Bench
(Table [1](#S4.T1 "Table 1 ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")
and
Table [2](#S4.T2 "Table 2 ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")).
As supplementary upstream context on the full task pool, Appendix
Table [13](#A4.T13 "Table 13 ‣ D.2 201-Task Upstream Context ‣ Appendix D Supplementary Interpretation Details ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")
compares YouRA and MLR-Agent on Idea/Proposal-stage quality across all
201 tasks.

#### Systems.

We compare three systems under Claude Sonnet 4.5 [Anthropic
(2025b)](#bib.bib24), Opus 4.5 [Anthropic (2025a)](#bib.bib25), and
Sonnet 4.6 [Anthropic (2026b)](#bib.bib26) backbones: (1) MLR-Agent
[Chen et al. (2026)](#bib.bib12), the reference scaffold released with
MLR-Bench, (2) AI Scientist V2 [Yamada et al. (2025)](#bib.bib5), the
benchmark’s primary external end-to-end comparison system and (3) YouRA
(ours).

Following the MLR-Bench convention ([Chen et al., 2026](#bib.bib12)), we
run each system in its released configuration: MLR-Agent’s default
scaffold, which invokes AI code editors such as Codex¹¹ 1
[https://github.com/openai/codex](https://github.com/openai/codex) and
Claude Code²² 2
[https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code)
but does not integrate a separate MCP stack; AI Scientist V2 with
Semantic Scholar API access and YouRA with the Model Context Protocol
(MCP) tool-access stack ([Model Context Protocol Contributors,
2025](#bib.bib29)) summarized in Appendix
Table [6](#A1.T6 "Table 6 ‣ External Tool Integration. ‣ Appendix A System Implementation Details ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents").
This is a released-configuration comparison with matched
research-execution backbones, not a fully tool-normalized comparison;
architecture-level interpretation therefore relies on the within-YouRA
ablations and the provenance/hallucination diagnostics in addition to
the cross-system scores. For YouRA, the Claude model is the
task-execution backbone and the independent controller is fixed as
GPT-5.2 [OpenAI (2025)](#bib.bib22) (removed only in the w/o Independent
Controller ablation.

so

varies execution rather than controller backbone.

#### Evaluation.

We adopt MLR-Bench’s End-to-End rubric ([Chen et al., 2026](#bib.bib12))
(Clarity, Novelty, Soundness, Significance, Overall; 1–10): Clarity
scores readability and structure, Novelty scores originality and advance
over prior work, Soundness scores method appropriateness, data support,
code consistency, and whether reported results are real, not fabricated,
Significance scores whether reliable results support field-level impact,
and Overall gives a holistic publishability-anchored judgment. We retain
these evaluation criteria and adapt MLR-Judge’s input handling to
account for each judge LLM’s context length limit. Each judge receives
the task description, paper, code, result files, and experimental logs.
Across all evaluated systems, when a request exceeded this limit, we
progressively truncated the combined code, result files, and logs from
the end and retried the request, while keeping the task description and
paper in full. The MLR-Bench study reports alignment between MLR-Judge
scores and human judgments, so we use this rubric as the primary
standardized metric while acknowledging the general limitations of
automated judging. We therefore complement scalar scores with
order-controlled pairwise preferences, hallucination diagnostics,
data-provenance diagnostics, and component ablations. End-to-end scoring
on the ten-task subset averages four frontier-class judges from distinct
model families (Gemini 3.1 Pro ([Google DeepMind, 2026](#bib.bib21)),
GPT 5.4 ([OpenAI, 2026](#bib.bib23)), Grok 4.3 ([xAI,
2026](#bib.bib28)), and Opus 4.6 ([Anthropic, 2026a](#bib.bib27))),
selected so that judge capability meets or exceeds that of the
generators under evaluation while limiting judge–generator overlap.
Pairwise win rates use the same four judges with order-bias control: for
each paper pair, we evaluate both presentation orders and count the
comparison as a tie if the preferred paper changes when the order is
reversed. All evaluations run fully autonomously.

### 4.3 Ablation Studies

Table [3](#S4.T3 "Table 3 ‣ Upstream stages. ‣ 4.2 Main Results ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")
reports four component ablations of YouRA across all three backbones,
each dropping one component from the full system. _w/o MCP_ removes the
MCP tool layer along with tool-grounded execution and structured access
to memory and external artifacts. _w/o Reflection_ disables
reflection-guided redesign and reset, so failures and partial successes
no longer turn into reusable constraints and evidence. _w/o VSA_ swaps
the shared persistent record of hypotheses, evidence, gate outcomes, and
failure constraints for a short in-context state summary at each stage.
_w/o Independent Controller_ moves stage progression, recovery, and
debate convergence into the research execution session, where they
become simple artifact checks. The drops are largest on _Soundness_ and
_Significance_ and smaller on _Clarity_ and _Novelty_, which means the
components matter mainly for research validity, not writing clarity or
assessed novelty. Removing either core-state component, the VSA or the
Independent Controller, also pushes YouRA’s Overall below both baselines
on Sonnet 4.5 and Opus 4.5
(Tables [1](#S4.T1 "Table 1 ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")

### 5.1 Human validation

The preceding analysis shows that YouRA’s mechanisms were exercised in
evaluation and that component ablations support their contribution to
scalar performance. We next examine how this advantage appears in the
failure modes tracked by MLR-Bench: faked experimental results,
hallucinated methodology, nonexistent or incorrect citations, and
mathematical errors ([Chen et al., 2026](#bib.bib12)). For papers
generated across the three matched backbones, we first apply the
MLR-Judge automated diagnostic using four judges. We report union and
intersection counts per category: the former count papers (out of ten
per system and backbone) in which at least one judge flagged that
category, whereas the latter count only papers flagged by all four
judges (Appendix
Figure [5](#A5.F5 "Figure 5 ‣ Interpretive takeaway. ‣ Appendix E Automated Hallucination Diagnostic ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")).
We then conduct a human validation of the automatically flagged
instances, summarized in
Table [4](#S5.T4 "Table 4 ‣ 5 Discussion ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents").
Three evaluators independently assess 270 flagged instances (90 per
system), confirming roughly three quarters as genuine hallucinations.
Precision is highest for hallucinated methodology and lowest for
nonexistent citations, and per-system precision is statistically
indistinguishable, so the automated diagnostic does not systematically
favor or disfavor any system. A fourth evaluator then audits the three
annotators by independently re-annotating a 90-instance subset.
Agreement is moderate, most disagreements are cases where the auditor is
stricter than the primary annotator, and the disagreement rate does not
differ significantly across systems ($`\chi^{2}(2)=2.61`$, $`p=0.27`$),
giving no evidence of systematic bias toward or against any of the three
systems.

Because precision is comparable across systems,
Table [5](#S5.T5 "Table 5 ‣ 5 Discussion ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")
reports precision-corrected hallucination counts. On these corrected
counts, MLR-Agent is significantly worse than both other systems, while
YouRA and AI Scientist V2 are not statistically distinguishable; this is
absence of evidence for a difference, not evidence of equivalence. On
Overall, in contrast, YouRA is significantly better than both baselines
(§[4.2](#S4.SS2 "4.2 Main Results ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")).
YouRA thus sits in the low-hallucination group yet alone leads on
Overall, so its score advantage is not achieved through more fabricated
content.

## 6 Conclusion

YouRA addresses a persistent gap in autonomous research agents, the
drift of manuscript claims away from executed experiments, by making
research state, execution evidence, and the research trajectory explicit
and durable across the pipeline. VSA-based state externalization, the
Independent Controller, and Stateful Reflection record hypotheses,
failures, evidence links, and recovery decisions as persistent
structures, and removing any one of them significantly lowers end-to-end
quality. On MLR-Bench, YouRA improves scalar end-to-end scores over both
baselines across matched backbones. Human-validated hallucination counts
on the final papers, checked against code and logs, and a provenance
classification of the data behind each run’s experiments together show
that the gain does not rest on fabricated content. YouRA has
significantly fewer confirmed hallucinations than MLR-Agent, its count
cannot be distinguished from that of AI Scientist V2, its runs rely on
real data more often than either baseline under a conservative reading,
and the scalar score separates YouRA from both. A scalar end-to-end
score cannot by itself tell whether a gain comes from better research or
from more persuasive fabrication, so it should be paired with
diagnostics that check manuscript claims and their underlying data
against executed evidence, such as the human-validated hallucination
diagnostic and the automated data-provenance analysis used here.

## Limitations

All experiments focus on machine learning and Deep learning tasks,
leaving the applicability of YouRA to other scientific domains for
future investigation. Research quality is assessed using the MLR-Judge
framework with a judge LLM different from those evaluated in the
original MLR-Bench study. Alignment with human reviewers therefore
requires separate validation for our judge configuration. Input
truncation due to the judge LLMs’ context length limits may disadvantage
papers with extensive code, result files, or logs. The systems compared
in our experiments operate with different tool configurations and token
budgets, so the reported scores reflect performance under each system’s
resource conditions. A promising direction for future work is to develop
a benchmark with standardized tool access and token budgets, providing a
common experimental setting for comparing autonomous research systems
under matched resource constraints.

## Ethical Considerations

Autonomous paper-generation systems can accelerate legitimate research
workflows, but they also create risks of unverified submissions,
misleading claims, and over-reliance on AI-generated manuscripts. YouRA
is intended to assist researchers, not replace them, so all outputs
should be independently verified and AI involvement in manuscript
preparation should be disclosed. Because the system uses commercial LLM
APIs and external tools, deployments should follow institutional and
data-use policies before sending private code, data, or unpublished
research ideas to external services; such dependencies may also limit
accessibility for under-resourced groups, motivating future support for
open-weight or local alternatives. We use MLR-Bench tasks under their
stated license and document external-tool usage policies in the released
repository. The hallucination and data-provenance diagnostics in this
paper are audit tools for checking claim–evidence alignment, not methods
for polishing unsupported manuscripts; responsible use requires
preserving artifact provenance and making claim-to-evidence links
inspectable.

## Appendix A System Implementation Details

This appendix explains how the VSA, Independent Controller, and Stateful
Reflection mechanisms introduced in the main methodology are implemented
in the deployed workflow. The implementation is organized into four
layers: external tool integration, state externalization mechanisms for
long-horizon persistence, a centralized state file shared by all agents,
and stage-specific agent assignments.

#### External Tool Integration.

YouRA uses Claude Code as the execution host and connects the five
MCP-facing tools in
Table [6](#A1.T6 "Table 6 ‣ External Tool Integration. ‣ Appendix A System Implementation Details ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")
as a common tool-access layer. Archon provides retrieval-augmented
generation (RAG)-backed knowledge management, storing project-specific
documents and prior implementation cases in a vector database. Exa
offers Search, Contents, and Research APIs for web-based evidence
collection. Semantic Scholar supplies Academic Graph and Recommendations
APIs for paper metadata and citation networks. Serena enables
symbol-level code navigation and editing, with failure-context recording
for Reflective Memory. Clear Thought provides structured reasoning
scaffolds, including scientific-method and mental-model templates.
Claude Code hooks provide termination control and iterative execution
loops;

#### State Externalization Mechanisms.

MCP tool calls are stateless, so YouRA separates transient tool calls
from durable artifacts. BMAD-Method³³ 3
[https://github.com/bmad-code-org/bmad-method](https://github.com/bmad-code-org/bmad-method)
provides structured artifact templates so each stage receives the prior
stage’s outputs in a fixed schema. During Problem Scoping, YouRA adapts
BMAD’s brainstorming workflow as a Socratic Discovery procedure for
topic expansion: a topic-level prompt is expanded into diverse research
angles through iterative questioning, anti-bias pivots, and convergence
into a research-question package passed to Literature Grounding.
Archon’s RAG-backed knowledge management stores project-specific
documents, code patterns, and prior implementation cases in a vector
database, which agents query during Implementation Planning and Coding &
Validation. Agent Skills⁴⁴ 4
[https://agentskills.io/specification](https://agentskills.io/specification)
loads procedural knowledge on demand, including prompt templates,
verification protocols, and output schemas. These mechanisms are
supporting infrastructure around the VSA: they keep state available
outside the transient conversation context.

#### Independent Controller.

The Independent Controller turns VSA records into stage transitions,
recovery choices, and persona selection, making persistent state an
active control surface rather than a passive log. It runs in an
independent LLM context decoupled from the task-execution agent; in our
implementation, this controller is GPT-5.2 [OpenAI (2025)](#bib.bib22),
accessed through OpenRouter API. At each step it reads only two inputs:
a compact summary of the current VSA and the most recent per-stage
artifact pointer. Because the controller never sees the full execution
trace, its context does not grow with the pipeline length, and its
routing decisions are less directly exposed to the task-execution
agent’s internal reasoning. The same Independent Controller is reused in
Hypothesis Debate, where it selects personas and judges convergence; in
Adversarial Fact Review, the independent critics are the reviewer
personas, which run as context-isolated task agents of the backbone LLM,
while the controller’s Stop hook handles only round-to-round
continuation. Operationally, the controller’s per-stage output is
consumed by a Stop hook that either approves termination, emits a resume
prompt back to the task-execution LLM, or escalates a MUST_STOP signal
when human intervention is required.

#### Reflection-Guided Failure Handling.

When a Coding & Validation gate fails, recovery first separates local

#### Hypothesis Debate.

Hypothesis Debate implements the function of multi-persona scientific
debate inside YouRA’s stateful workflow. The six personas cover novelty
and ideation (Dr. Nova), falsifiability and experiment design
(Prof. Vera), scientific contribution and impact (Dr. Sage),
technical/theoretical feasibility (Prof. Pax), evidence-based hypothesis
refinement (Dr. Ally), and assumptions/failure modes (Prof. Rex). The
protocol has three stages. First, Gap Selection chooses the priority
research gap and supporting evidence from Literature Grounding. During
Debate, the independent controller LLM (GPT-5.2 [OpenAI
(2025)](#bib.bib22)) reads recent dialogue and VSA state to decide
convergence and select the next personas, while the backbone LLM
produces the role-conditioned responses. If a network or API failure
prevents a controller call, backbone-LLM fallback preserves execution
continuity for that round. Result Structuring converts the debate
outcome into structured YAML for downstream stages. When this stage is
re-entered after a failed or superseded hypothesis, Reflective Memory is
checked first so the system does not repeat refuted directions. The
converged hypothesis is formalized with variables, causal mechanisms, a
null hypothesis, and testable predictions, then decomposed during
Verification Planning into a 3–7-node sub-hypothesis DAG with
MUST_WORK/SHOULD_WORK gates recorded in the VSA.

#### Hypothesis Loop Details: Experiment Design through Coding & Validation.

Each sub-hypothesis traverses the hypothesis loop from Experiment Design
through Coding & Validation. Experiment Design turns the sub-hypothesis
into an experiment specification covering the dataset, model
architecture, and evaluation metrics. Implementation Planning expands
this specification into PRD, architecture, logic, and configuration
documents, then consolidates them into the VSA task artifact
03_tasks.yaml, a prioritized implementation task list whose entries
store references to the relevant specification sections. Coding &
Validation uses these references to apply SDD (Spec-Driven Development):
for each task, it extracts the governing requirements, generates and
runs compliance tests before implementation, implements the task, and
invokes an independent Validator for multi-stage verification. Failures
are fed back with diagnostic context, unmet gates trigger
reflection-guided routing, and a hook-based mock-data/hard-coded-result
detector checks whether outputs come from actual data processing or from
fabricated placeholders. Each completed sub-hypothesis is marked as
validated, refuted, or escalated; the loop continues until all
sub-hypotheses reach a terminal state, after which the pipeline proceeds
to Evidence Check.

#### Evidence Check.

Evidence Check produces an evidence-check summary that records, for each
prediction, whether the executed result supports it (supported,
partially_supported, refuted, or inconclusive); the same pass also
tracks explicit causal-mechanism claims and assumptions. This stage is
an evidence-transfer check: it records which predictions and assumptions
match executed results, then passes that summary to Manuscript Drafting
and Adversarial Fact Review. The resulting evidence-check summary is a
core input to writing and review alongside the VSA and Coding &
Validation artifacts; its downstream effect on manuscript-claim
traceability is assessed through the hallucination and data-provenance
diagnostics in
Appendices [E](#A5 "Appendix E Automated Hallucination Diagnostic ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")
and
 [F](#A6 "Appendix F Data Provenance Analysis ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents").

#### Manuscript Drafting: Story Group Architecture.

Manuscript Drafting generates the manuscript in three sequential story
groups, guided by a blueprint that is written before any section
content. Foundation (Introduction + Related Work + Methodology) frames
the problem and motivates the solution. Evidence (Experiments +
Results + Discussion) presents and interprets the experimental findings.
Closure (Conclusion + Abstract) is produced last, and the abstract is
written last to capture the full paper context. The Evidence Check
summary is one input to manuscript writing, alongside the VSA and
artifacts from Problem Scoping through Coding & Validation (e.g.,
Problem Scoping for introduction motivation, Experiment
Design/Implementation Planning for methodology, Coding & Validation for
results). VSA-recorded values are the intended source for reported
numbers, discouraging unsupported reported values.

#### Adversarial Fact Review and Manuscript Refinement.

Adversarial Fact Review applies an adversarial fact-review protocol
(Devil’s Advocate) with three reviewer personas. The Accuracy Checker
cross-references every claim against ground truth, compares reported
numbers against a dedicated ground-truth registry, and checks
methodology consistency and cross-section contradictions against Coding
& Validation artifacts. The Bored Reviewer examines whether the abstract
and framing would compel further reading from the perspective of a
time-pressed conference reviewer. The Skeptical Expert probes novelty
claims and baseline fairness. Findings are classified into two blocking
levels. Major findings are claim–implementation contradictions or
reviewer-attackable weaknesses such as unfair baselines that must be
corrected with evidence before convergence; Minor findings are polish
issues such as typos, awkward phrasing, and formatting, collected as
human-review notes. Manuscript Refinement revisits the VSA, Evidence
Check summary, stage artifacts, result files, logs, and code until no
major findings remain.

#### Failure Classification Logic.

The Validator and reflection step together map each gate failure to one
of the three externally visible routing levels in
Table [8](#A1.T8 "Table 8 ‣ Reflection-Guided Failure Handling. ‣ Appendix A System Implementation Details ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents");
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ Reflection-Guided Failure Handling. ‣ Appendix A System Implementation Details ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")
gives the compact paper-level decision procedure. Repair is bounded
in-loop handling inside Coding & Validation, whereas redesign and reset
are escalations that write structured archive snapshots.

- •
  Repair (runtime / test / fabricated-output failure): the
  implementation produces an exception, assertion error, test failure,
  or mock-data/hard-coded-output signal that is localizable to a
  specific code location. The Validator attempts bounded in-place fixes
  within Coding & Validation before any archive-producing escalation.
- •
  Redesign routing (design / mechanism failure): a MUST_WORK failure
  persists after bounded repair, but reflection judges that the failure
  remains recoverable through hypothesis or mechanism revision (e.g.,
  architectural mismatch, invalid causal mechanism, dependency structure
  that requires redesign). The system routes to Hypothesis Debate with a
  diagnostic record specifying the failed component and attempted fixes.
- •
  Reset (fundamental failure): a MUST_WORK failure reflects evidence
  that fundamentally contradicts the current hypothesis (e.g., predicted
  correlation does not hold, effect size remains absent after correct
  implementation). The system routes to Problem Scoping with the full
  failure context, archiving the current research folder under a
  timestamped backup before beginning a new research direction.

### D.1 Statistical Significance of the End-to-End Comparison

The unit of analysis is the matched (backbone, task) cell. Each cell
score is the mean of the four LLM judges’ Overall scores, and because
the three systems share the same 30 cells, task and backbone variation
cancels within pairs. We test the mean Overall difference with a paired
sign-flip permutation test using 100,000 random sign assignments
(two-sided).

$`p{=}0.012`$ and $`0.044`$). The per-backbone mean differences are

## Appendix E Automated Hallucination Diagnostic

#### Protocol.

MLR-Bench’s hallucination analysis defines four fact-based failure types
(nonexistent or incorrect citations, hallucinated methodology,
mathematical errors, and faked experimental results) and applies human
verification after automated LLM detection ([Chen et al.,
2026](#bib.bib12)). We apply the automated detection stage to the
generated papers across all three matched backbones, updating the
original Gemini/Claude two-judge setup to a four-judge panel: Grok 4.3,
GPT 5.4, Gemini 3.1 Pro, and Claude Opus 4.6. We report the results as
judge-union and judge-intersection bounds, and validate the
automatically flagged instances with three human annotators and an
independent auditor
(Table [4](#S5.T4 "Table 4 ‣ 5 Discussion ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents");
§[5.1](#S5.SS1 "5.1 Human validation ‣ 5 Discussion ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")).

#### Union/intersection semantics.

Union counts capture any judge-flagged case, while intersection counts
capture only cases flagged by all four judges.
Figure [5](#A5.F5 "Figure 5 ‣ Interpretive takeaway. ‣ Appendix E Automated Hallucination Diagnostic ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")
shows intersection counts in the top row and union counts in the bottom
row; bar labels give the number of flagged papers out of ten. The gap
between the two values reflects judge disagreement, and we report both
instead of reducing the diagnostic to a single hallucination score.

#### Interpretive takeaway.

Union counts are high for most rows, so we report the diagnostic as an
automated profile of hallucination flags, and keep union and
intersection counts separate; the profile is not used as a standalone
ranking metric. On the all-judge-agreed intersection flags, YouRA shows
a favorable profile on execution-evidence categories such as
hallucinated methodology and faked experimental results; we report these
as hallucination-flag counts; the human validation in
§[5.1](#S5.SS1 "5.1 Human validation ‣ 5 Discussion ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")
(Table [4](#S5.T4 "Table 4 ‣ 5 Discussion ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents"))
estimates the precision of these automated flags at 75.2% overall.
Citation flags vary by backbone and are treated as a secondary signal.
This pattern complements the Soundness/Overall gains in
Table [1](#S4.T1 "Table 1 ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")
and the real-data provenance contrast in
Figure [2](#S5.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 5.2 Data provenance ‣ 5 Discussion ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")
with concrete failure-type and data-provenance views.

## Appendix F Data Provenance Analysis

#### Protocol.

Each generated paper is classified at the paper level by the kind of
data its experiments ran on. An automated analyzer receives the paper
and the root of its experiment folder, explores the folder without
assuming its layout, and inspects the experiment code and result files
to check that execution actually occurred, then assigns one of three
labels: _Real_ (experiments run on real datasets, e.g., loaded through
datasets.load_dataset or torchvision.datasets), _Synthetic_ (experiments
run on artificially generated data, e.g., random draws or
synthetic-dataset generators), or _Fabricated_ (reported values without
runnable experiment code, e.g., hardcoded result arrays). Every label is
stored with the file-level evidence it rests on. Two analyzers built on
distinct model families, Claude Code (Opus 4.6) and Codex (GPT-5.4), run
the same protocol independently, so the classification does not hinge on
a single model family; they agree on 85 of the 90 (system, backbone,
task) cells. The counts reported in
§[5.2](#S5.SS2 "5.2 Data provenance ‣ 5 Discussion ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")
use a conservative, maximum-synthetic reading: a run counts as
real-data-based only when both analyzers label it _Real_, so a
_Synthetic_ or _Fabricated_ label from either analyzer counts against
the run.

#### Comparison with the baselines under ablation.

On Sonnet 4.5, all four ablated configurations score below both
MLR-Agent and AI Scientist V2 on Overall
(Tables [1](#S4.T1 "Table 1 ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")
and [3](#S4.T3 "Table 3 ‣ Upstream stages. ‣ 4.2 Main Results ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")).
The same holds on Opus 4.5, although the w/o Reflection configuration is
only marginally below MLR-Agent. On Sonnet 4.6, the ablated
configurations all remain above AI Scientist V2; only w/o VSA falls
below MLR-Agent, while w/o MCP and w/o Reflection tie it and w/o
Independent Controller stays above. Either core-state removal (w/o VSA
or w/o Independent Controller) drops YouRA below both baselines by clear
margins on two of the three backbones (Sonnet 4.5 and Opus 4.5).

#### Backbone dependence.

Across all four ablations, the drops are largest on Sonnet 4.5 and
Opus 4.5 and smallest on Sonnet 4.6 (backbone-drop columns of
Table [16](#A7.T16 "Table 16 ‣ Ablation significance. ‣ Appendix G Component Ablations ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")),
so a stronger execution backbone partially compensates for a missing
component. The compensation is incomplete. The full configuration is
still the highest-scoring YouRA variant on every backbone, and even the
smallest drop (w/o Independent Controller on Sonnet 4.6) leaves the
ablated configuration below the full one.

#### Failure profiles under core-component ablation.

Judge justifications for the two Sonnet 4.5 core-component ablations
show distinct failure signatures aligned with each component’s role.
Under w/o VSA, recurring flags include numbers that disagree across
sections and tables, code implementing different experiments than the
paper describes, claims contradicting execution logs, and selective
reporting of favorable runs, all consistent with losing the single
persistent record connecting hypotheses, gates, and evidence links.
Under w/o Independent Controller, judges repeatedly flag failed or
crashed runs whose outputs are still reported as completed findings,
validation gates relaxed post hoc instead of escalated, and pipelines
that shrink far below their plans without re-attempt, consistent with
losing independent stop/continue judgment and recovery escalation. In
both configurations Soundness is the most damaged dimension while
Clarity stays high, matching the fluent-but-unsupported failure mode
that motivates YouRA’s design
(§[1](#S1 "1 Introduction ‣ YouRA: A Persistent-State Architecture for Evidence-Traceable Autonomous Research Agents")).

#### Note on Clarity $`\Delta`$ under ablation.

On Sonnet 4.5, the no-MCP configuration leaves Clarity nearly unchanged
($`-0.05`$); the no-reflection configuration shows a small Clarity
increase ($`+0.075`$). On Sonnet 4.6, both the MCP and reflection
ablations slightly reduce Clarity and increase Novelty. The Sonnet 4.5
VSA and controller ablations follow the same pattern: Clarity decreases
only slightly ($`-0.325`$ and $`-0.275`$) while Soundness and
Significance drop sharply. Across all configurations, the Overall drops
are driven primarily by Soundness and Significance, the dimensions in
which VSA-conditioned and reflection-informed reasoning is most heavily
exercised; surface-level clarity contributes little.
